# Funding options

Kept by Sales team B (Funding and partnerships). Checked on 2026-11-10. Amounts
and deadlines change, so confirm on each program's own site before applying.
This is research, not financial or legal advice. Only the owner applies, signs
or pays a fee.

## The honest picture

- **The government does not give "free money" to start or run a business.**
  The SBA's grants go to nonprofits and training partners, not to individual
  businesses (sba.gov/loans/additional-funding-opportunities/grants). Grants.gov
  is mostly for nonprofits, research and public bodies. Anyone charging a fee to
  get you "guaranteed government grants" is almost certainly a scam.
- **SBIR and STTR** are federal R&D contracts for new technology with real
  research risk. A $49 productivity app is unlikely to qualify, and applying is
  a large job.
- **Public companies and foundations** do fund small businesses through
  competitions, usually $1,000 to $25,000, with many applicants. Treat these as
  lottery tickets worth a few hours, not a plan.
- **Revenue is still the fastest and most reliable money.** About 20 sales a
  month pays for a typical small grant application's time many times over.

## Low-cost loans (only if the owner wants debt)

- **SBA Microloans** through nonprofit lenders, up to $50,000.
- **Accion Opportunity Fund**, $5,000 to $250,000, with coaching.
- **Kiva**, 0% interest crowd loans for small businesses (check current limits).

## Free help (no money, but useful)

- **SCORE**: free mentoring from experienced business owners.
- **SBDC** (Small Business Development Centers): free local advice, and they
  know local and state grants that aren't listed nationally.

## What a strong application needs (prepare once, reuse)

1. A registered business (an LLC with an EIN). Most programs require one.
2. A one-paragraph story: who it helps, the problem, what's built and live.
3. Real numbers only: visitors, sign-ups and sales to date.
4. What the money is for: for example, a $X ad test and a demo video.
