  Community post 1 (r/freelance or a freelancer Facebook group), title "What I send when a client sits on files and the deadline slides": Most stalled projects I've seen aren't ghosting. The client still replies to other things, they just haven't sent the logo files or the feedback. What's worked for me is one email in the same thread that does three things. 1) List exactly what's missing as bullets ("final logo as SVG", "your notes on round 1"), not "feedback". 2) Move the date in writing: "since these were due on [date], delivery moves from [old] to [new]. Each extra day of waiting moves it one more day." 3) Give a choice they can answer in one line: "send them by Thursday and I'll hold the new date, or I can carry on with placeholders and swap them in later. Which works better?" No apology, no "I know you're busy". If a week passes with nothing: "Should I pause [project] until you have [item]?" Then actually pause. I wrote up the full version with a feedback-stall variant here: https://buzzyfly.com/blog/client-stalling-feedback-files-exact-reply/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=stall-reply
  Community post 2 (freelancer/small-business group), title "The 3 invoice reminders I use so I never write one from scratch": Day 1 overdue: "A quick reminder that the invoice for [project] ([amount]) was due on [date]. Could you let me know when it's scheduled to be paid?" Week 1: same thread, shorter, and ask for a payment date. Week 2: "The [amount] invoice for [project] is two weeks overdue. I'll pause new work on the account until it's settled. Can you confirm a payment date by the end of this week?" Only say you'll pause work if you will. Put the three dates on your calendar the day you send the invoice, so you're not deciding whether to chase.
