# Marketing playbook

What actually sells a $49 tool to freelancers and coaches, in priority order.
Every marketing, sales and web agent reads this each run. CLAUDE.md always wins.

## The one truth

No visitor has come from outside the site yet (all traffic "direct", 0 Stripe
charges as of 2026-11-02). Content on our own site doesn't sell until people
see it. **Distribution beats content.** Put the offer where buyers already are,
every day.

## The tactics that work (use these)

1. **Show the product before the price.** A buyer can't see inside the app
   before paying $49. Fix it with a 20 to 30 second screen recording of the
   real app (Today list, then "Write the email", then a filled-in email) on
   /store and in every post. Also build a free `/demo`: the real app with
   sample clients, nothing saved. Seeing it work is the strongest proof we can
   honestly give.
2. **Name the exact moment of pain.** "Proposal sent 6 days ago, no reply, and
   you don't know what to say" beats "stay organised". One moment per post. Hook
   in the first line or the first 2 seconds of a video.
3. **Go where buyers already gather.** Freelance and coaching subreddits,
   Facebook groups, LinkedIn, Indie Hackers, and newsletters for freelancers.
   Give a complete answer first (the exact email to send). Add the link last,
   and only where the group's rules allow links.
4. **Free tool as bait.** `/overdue-invoice-email` is a free generator. Build
   more free one-job tools (a proposal follow-up writer, a scope-creep reply, an
   hourly-rate floor calculator). Each one ends with "the full system does this
   for every client: $49". People share free tools, and search engines rank them.
5. **Capture the email, then follow up.** Most buyers don't buy on the first
   visit. Every page offers the free weekly-reset checklist, and the email
   sequence (`src/data/emailSequence.ts`) teaches, then makes the offer.
6. **Launch in public once.** Product Hunt, Show HN (Hacker News), Indie Hackers,
   BetaList and "I built this" threads, each with the founder story: built by a
   solo operator for solo operators. A launch is one good day of traffic, so
   prepare the demo video first.
7. **Direct outreach, one person at a time.** The Sales team finds people who
   publicly asked for help with follow-ups, onboarding or scope creep, and
   drafts a short, helpful personal reply. Every message is useful on its own.
8. **Partners and affiliates.** Coaches, VAs, bookkeepers and freelance-community
   owners recommend tools to their audience. Offer a referral cut (decided by
   the owner) and a free copy to review.
9. **Real proof, as soon as it exists.** After the first buyers, ask each one:
   "What did it fix for you?" Publish their answer only with written permission
   and their real name, or keep it anonymous and say so.
10. **Paid ads once there is proof the page converts.** Start with $10 to $20 a
    day on Facebook or Instagram, aimed at freelancers and coaches, with the
    demo video. Only with the owner's yes and a daily budget. Turn ads off if
    3 days pass with no buy clicks.
11. **Offer craft (already on the site; keep it):** one clear price, one payment,
    no subscription, the refund rule next to the button, and "Most popular" on
    the $49 system.

## What we never do, and why

These tactics are banned because they break the law (FTC rules on fake reviews
and deceptive offers) and get the Stripe, PayPal, Meta and Google accounts shut
down, which would stop every payment. They also cause the refunds, chargebacks
and complaints the owner wants to avoid.

- Fake scarcity or countdowns, "only 3 left", fake "sale ends tonight".
- Invented testimonials, reviews, ratings, user counts, stats or "as seen on".
- Income or outcome promises ("make $10k", "never lose a client").
- Hidden subscriptions, pre-ticked boxes, confusing cancellation, bait-and-switch.
- Spam: bulk cold email or DMs, buying email lists, posting the same link
  everywhere, breaking a group's rules.
- Copying other brands' names, logos or content.

## How we know it works

Every link we share carries
`?utm_source=<site>&utm_medium=<social|community|email|partner|ad>&utm_campaign=<slug>`.
The CEO's Monday numbers show visits, buy clicks and sales by source. Double
down on any source that produces buy clicks, and drop one that hasn't after
2 weeks.
