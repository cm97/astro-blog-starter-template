You do not need a brand, a following, or a funnel to get paid. You need one person with the problem, one short message, and a same-day start line the hour they say yes. If the first week after they pay is rebuilt from memory, they go quiet and you are back at zero.

This is the sprint. Run it this week. Do not add a logo first.

## Day 1 — one offer, one buyer (25 minutes)

Write one sentence: who you help, the broken thing, the result, the price. Stop editing after one pass.

- I help: [one type of person, not "everyone"]
- Who is stuck on: [one visible problem]
- So they can: [one outcome in a week]
- Price to start: [one number you will say out loud]
- Start window: [this Friday or next Monday — one date]

"I build a one-page offer for coaches who are still explaining their service in DMs" beats "I do marketing."

Say the price out loud once. If you will not say it, you will not invoice it.

## Day 2 — 20 names, not 200 (30 minutes)

Open the app you already use. Write 20 names of people who already show the problem. LinkedIn, Instagram, a local directory, old texts. No cold list you bought.

For each name, one line: what is visibly broken. A dead link, a vague bio, a proposal they posted and never followed up, a "DM me for prices" with no prices.

If you cannot name the broken thing, skip the name. A vague list produces vague messages.

## Day 3 — 20 messages, one question (40 minutes)

Not a pitch deck. Copy this and swap the brackets:

> Saw [specific thing]. Most [role] lose the next client there because [one reason]. I have a 15-minute fix. Want it?

No attachment. No "just circling back" on day one. Send all 20 in one sitting. Log the send time next to the name so day 4 is a list, not a feeling.

## Day 4 — one follow-up, then stop (15 minutes)

Half will not reply. That is the job, not a rejection. One bump, only to people who have not replied:

> Bumping this — still useful, or should I close it?

Then move. Do not write a new novel. Two bumps is a chase. One bump is a close-or-clear.

## Day 5 — the 15-minute audit, not free work (15 minutes per reply)

To anyone who replied:

> I will look at [the broken thing] for 15 minutes and send three fixes. You keep the notes. If you want me to do the fixes, I will quote the number from day 1.

Cap it at three audits. A free afternoon is not a sales call. If a fourth person replies, book them for next week or decline.

## Day 6 — deliver the audit, name the price (20 minutes)

Three bullets only:

1. What is broken
2. What you would change this week
3. Price and start date

Then send this line, unchanged except the brackets:

> I can do this. Price is [number]. We start [day]. Reply yes and I send the welcome note today.

## Day 7 — invoice or next name (10 minutes)

Yes means invoice the same day. No means thank them and go to the next name. Do not discount to "get a win." A cheap first client who needs a custom process is a second unpaid job.

If nobody said yes, the sprint is not failed. You have 20 notes and a message you can send again next Monday. Do not rebuild the offer.

## The same-day start line

This is where most first clients die. Do these five things before you celebrate. Time box: 20 minutes after payment clears.

1. Welcome email. Subject: You are in — start date [date]. Body: what you need from them (access, files, decision-maker), when you reply (one window, e.g. weekdays before 4pm), and the first deliverable date.
2. Intake, five fields only: goal, deadline, access, who decides, what is out of scope. If out of scope is blank, you will eat the extra work.
3. Kickoff on the calendar. Agenda is already written: outcome, constraints, first deliverable, next check-in. 25 minutes. Not a discovery safari.
4. 48-hour follow-up on the calendar: "Confirm I have everything from intake." One paragraph. Do not skip it because you are embarrassed it is late.
5. One-page client note: name, amount paid, next step, date. If it is not written, Thursday wipes it.

Fill-in welcome line you can paste today:

> Hi [Name] — payment received. We start [date]. I need [access or file] by [date]. I reply [window]. First thing you get is [deliverable] on [date]. Kickoff is [time]. Out of scope for this start: [one line].
