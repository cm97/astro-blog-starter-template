You do the weekly reset on Monday. You feel organized. By Thursday you have forgotten it exists. The checklist is buried under three new projects. The plan is a joke. You are back to reacting.

The problem is not the reset itself. It is that you built a system that requires you to remember to run it. That is not a system. That is a wish.

Here is the version that still works when the inbox wins.

## The 20-minute reset that survives Thursday

Run it the same window every week. Monday morning before Slack, or Friday afternoon before you close the laptop. Pick one and lock it.

### Monday (15 minutes)

1. Open last week's notes or calendar. Write three lines only:
   - What actually shipped
   - What got pushed (and why)
   - What you promised that is still open
2. Clear every action item out of the inbox. Reply, schedule, or delete. Zero open decisions left sitting there.
3. Write the three priorities for this week. Not five. Not ten. Three. If a fourth appears, one of the three has to leave.
4. Put the three priorities on the calendar with a real time block. If it is not scheduled, it is not a priority.

### Wednesday (5 minutes)

1. Look at the three priorities. Still the right three?
2. If a client request or fire knocked one off, replace it. Do not add a fourth.
3. Send any follow-up that is now overdue. One clear sentence each. No novels.

### Friday (10 minutes)

1. Close the week: what got done, what rolls to next Monday, what dies.
2. Capture open loops in one place (same note or checklist every week). Do not invent a new system.
3. Update the checklist if a step failed twice. Then close the laptop.

## Exact phrases that keep it short

- "Still the priority, or does this move?"
- "One clear next step: [date] or off the list."
- "This week’s three: 1) 2) 3). Everything else waits."

## What still breaks when it only lives in your head

A Monday plan that lives in your memory dies the first time three clients reply at once. A Wednesday check that is not on the calendar never happens. A Friday close that is “later” becomes Sunday scramble.
