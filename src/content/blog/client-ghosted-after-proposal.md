You sent the proposal. They said they were excited. Then nothing.

The deal is not dead. You just stopped chasing it because writing another follow-up feels awkward.

Most people either ghost or send a long “just checking in on the proposal I sent last week” that forces them to do mental work. Neither gets replies.

These three lines do. Run them on a fixed cadence so the silence does not live in your head.

## The 4-minute log the minute you hit send

Copy this before you close the tab. Blank fields mean the follow-up does not exist yet.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________ (date / time)
Start date you named: ________
Deliverable named: ________
Day 3 due: ________
Day 7 due: ________
Day 14 due: ________
Status: waiting / replied / closed
Channel: email / other
Last touch sent: ________
Next action: ________
```

Four minutes. Do it while the proposal is still open. Thursday will erase it if you wait.

Put three 8-minute blocks on the calendar labeled "Proposal: [name] day 3/7/14". If the date lands on Friday, move it to Thursday morning. Do not skip.

Block template (paste into calendar):

- Title: Proposal follow-up [Name] day [3/7/14]
- Duration: 8 minutes
- Note: Send the exact line. Log the send. Do not rewrite.

## Day 3 — Hold the date or release it

Send on day 3, Tuesday or Wednesday morning before 11am. Not Friday. Not after 3pm.

> Still want me to hold the [start date], or should I release it?

One sentence. The date creates urgency. The “or release it” gives them an easy out so they reply instead of avoiding you.

Subject: Re: [Project name] proposal

After send: update log with time sent. Move status to waiting. Do not check for a reply for 24 hours.

## Day 7 — Binary choice

If day 3 got silence:

> Quick one: do you want the [deliverable] locked for [date], or should I drop the hold?

Still one line. Still a binary. People reply to yes/no faster than open-ended questions.

Same window: before 11am, mid-week. Same subject: Re: [Project name] proposal

After send: log the time. If they asked a clarifying question, answer in one paragraph and restate the binary close. Do not open a new scope talk.

## Day 14 — Close the loop

Still quiet:

> Closing the loop on the [project] proposal. If the timing is off, no problem — reply “later” and I’ll note it. Otherwise I’ll release the date.

Subject: Closing the file on [Project name]

This either gets a reply or cleanly kills the lead so it stops living in your head.

After send: set status to closed or parked. Delete the remaining calendar blocks. Do not send a fourth email.

## Exact subject lines that do not look like spam

- Day 3: Re: [Project name] proposal
- Day 7: Re: [Project name] proposal
- Day 14: Closing the file on [Project name]

Keep the Re: even if it is a new thread. It reads as continuation.

## Exact timing windows that raise reply rate

- Day 3: Tuesday or Wednesday morning, before 11am.
- Day 7: same, mid-week only.
- Day 14: any weekday before 11am.
- Never Friday. Never after 3pm. The reply rate drops and the thread dies over the weekend.
- If day 3 falls on a Friday, send Thursday instead. Do not slide it to Monday and lose the window.
- If you miss the window, send the next morning before 11am. Do not wait for a “better” day.

## What you do when they reply

- Yes: send the invoice the same hour. Welcome note with the start date and the one thing you need from them. Mark the log closed. Add them to next Monday’s onboarding row.
- Later: write the month they named. One note that month. Not a drip. Status = parked.
- Price question: restate what is in and what is out. Do not cut the number. One line: "The scope is [X]. Price stays [Y]. Want to lock the [date] or release it?"
- Scope change: new line, new price, new date. The original proposal stays the record. Restart the three-touch clock only if the new proposal is sent.
- Busy / later this week: reply "Understood. I’ll hold the [date] until [next specific day]. Reply by then or I release it." Log the new date.
- No: mark closed. Do not argue. Do not send a fourth email.

If you cannot name the next date after a yes, the client is not onboarded. That is a separate checklist.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the ghost

1. Paste the original start date into the log the same minute you send the proposal. Without the date the day-3 line has nothing to hold.
2. Keep the three lines in the same note as the Monday reset. One open, both available.
3. Cap at three emails. A fourth becomes a novel and they mute you.
4. After any yes, add the client to next Monday’s “Still open” so the day-7 onboarding touch does not live only in your head.
5. On Day 7, if they ask a question, answer in one paragraph and restate the close date. Do not open a new scope conversation.
6. After Day 14, do not send a fourth email because you felt guilty on a quiet Friday.
7. At the next weekly reset, add a row: "Open proposals past Day 7." If none, write none.
8. Screenshot or copy the proposal send time into the log. Memory of “I sent it last week” is not a date.
9. If the start date you named has already passed by Day 7, replace it with the next available start you can actually hold before you send the line.
10. Set a phone reminder for the 8-minute block the night before. The calendar alone dies when the week is on fire.

## Why these work when “just checking in” fails

“Just checking in” asks them to remember the proposal, decide, and write context. These ask for one word. The date makes the decision time-bound. You stop rebuilding the follow-up from memory every time a proposal goes quiet.
