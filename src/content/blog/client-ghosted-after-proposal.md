You sent the proposal. They said they were excited. Then nothing.

The deal is not dead. You just stopped chasing it because writing another follow-up feels awkward.

Most people either ghost or send a long “just checking in on the proposal I sent last week” that forces them to do mental work. Neither gets replies.

These three lines do.

## Day 3 — Hold the date or release it

> Still want me to hold the [start date], or should I release it?

One sentence. The date creates urgency. The “or release it” gives them an easy out so they reply instead of avoiding you.

Send it. Log the date. Move on.

## Day 7 — Binary choice

If day 3 got silence:

> Quick one: do you want the [deliverable] locked for [date], or should I drop the hold?

Still one line. Still a binary. People reply to yes/no faster than open-ended questions.

## Day 14 — Close the loop

Still quiet:

> Closing the loop on the [project] proposal. If the timing is off, no problem — reply “later” and I’ll note it. Otherwise I’ll release the date.

This either gets a reply or cleanly kills the lead so it stops living in your head.

## Why these work

“Just checking in” asks them to remember the proposal, decide, and write context. These ask for one word. The date makes the decision time-bound. You stop rebuilding the follow-up from memory every time a proposal goes quiet.
