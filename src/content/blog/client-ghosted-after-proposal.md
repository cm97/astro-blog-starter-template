You sent the proposal. They said they were excited. Then nothing.

The deal is not dead. You just stopped chasing it because writing another follow-up feels awkward.

Most people either ghost or send a long “just checking in on the proposal I sent last week” that forces them to do mental work. Neither gets replies.

These three lines do. Run them on a fixed cadence so the silence does not live in your head.

## The 4-minute log the minute you hit send

Copy this before you close the tab. Blank fields mean the follow-up does not exist yet.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________ (date / time)
Start date you named: ________
Day 3 due: ________
Day 7 due: ________
Day 14 due: ________
Status: waiting / replied / closed
```

Four minutes. Do it while the proposal is still open. Thursday will erase it if you wait.

## Day 3 — Hold the date or release it

Send on day 3, Tuesday or Wednesday morning. Not Friday.

> Still want me to hold the [start date], or should I release it?

One sentence. The date creates urgency. The “or release it” gives them an easy out so they reply instead of avoiding you.

Log the send. Move on. Do not rewrite it.

## Day 7 — Binary choice

If day 3 got silence:

> Quick one: do you want the [deliverable] locked for [date], or should I drop the hold?

Still one line. Still a binary. People reply to yes/no faster than open-ended questions.

Same window: before 11am, mid-week.

## Day 14 — Close the loop

Still quiet:

> Closing the loop on the [project] proposal. If the timing is off, no problem — reply “later” and I’ll note it. Otherwise I’ll release the date.

This either gets a reply or cleanly kills the lead so it stops living in your head.

## Exact subject lines that do not look like spam

- Day 3: Re: [Project name] proposal
- Day 7: Re: [Project name] proposal
- Day 14: Closing the file on [Project name]

Keep the Re: even if it is a new thread. It reads as continuation.

## What you do when they reply

- Yes: send the invoice the same hour. Welcome note with the start date and the one thing you need from them.
- Later: write the month they named. One note that month. Not a drip.
- Price: restate what is in and what is out. Do not cut the number.
- No: mark closed. Do not argue.

If you cannot name the next date after a yes, the client is not onboarded. That is a separate checklist.

## Why these work when “just checking in” fails

“Just checking in” asks them to remember the proposal, decide, and write context. These ask for one word. The date makes the decision time-bound. You stop rebuilding the follow-up from memory every time a proposal goes quiet.
