You sent the proposal. They said they would look at it. Three days later the inbox is still empty. You wait another day because you do not want to sound pushy. By day 7 the lead is cold and you start the next one from scratch.

That silence is normal. The cost is the hours you already spent on the proposal and the cash that never lands because no one followed up on a fixed day.

## The failure mode that kills quiet proposals

You remember the proposal went out. You do not remember the exact day. You open the thread, rewrite a new email from scratch, then close the tab because something else is due. The follow-up never leaves. The client never replies. You tell yourself they were not serious.

The fix is a fixed cadence that already exists before you need it. Day 3. Day 7. Same words. Same length. No rewrite under pressure.

## The 10-minute quiet-proposal sequence

Run this the morning of day 3 after you sent the proposal. Timer on. Stop at 10 minutes.

1. Open the proposal thread.
2. Copy the day-3 email below and change only the name and project.
3. Send it.
4. Add a note with the send date and the day-7 date.
5. Close the tab.

Five actions. Ten minutes. The email already exists.

Fill this before you start:

```
Proposal sent: ________
Client: ________
Project: ________
Day-3 follow-up sent: ________
Day-7 follow-up date: ________
```

## Exact emails that do the work

**Day 3 (first nudge)**
Subject: Quick note on the [project] proposal

Hi [Name],

Checking that the proposal for [project] landed. It covers the scope, timeline, and price we discussed.

Reply with any questions or the go-ahead and I will lock the start date.

[Your name]

**Day 7 (second nudge)**
Subject: Still open on [project]?

Hi [Name],

Following up on the proposal for [project]. Still happy to move forward if the timing works.

If you have decided against it, a quick note is fine. Otherwise reply and I will send the next step.

[Your name]

**If they reply "still thinking"**
Subject: Re: [project] proposal

Hi [Name],

Understood. I will leave the proposal open until [date, usually +5 days]. After that I will assume the timing is not right and close it so the calendar stays clear.

Reply before then if you want to proceed.

[Your name]

These three cover the first two weeks. Nothing is rewritten from memory.

## Extra concrete steps that keep the sequence alive

- Send the day-3 email on day 3 even if you feel awkward. Waiting past day 4 is the failure mode this checklist exists to kill.
- Cap every follow-up at five sentences. Longer versions get edited under pressure and never ship.
- Paste the exact subject line into the private note the moment you send it. Next time you open the note the words are there.
- If they reply with a question instead of a yes, answer once and re-paste the day-7 date. Do not restart the clock.
- Add the day-7 date as a phone reminder titled "[Client] proposal day 7". If it is not on the reminder, it dies when the next message arrives.
- If they pay a deposit before day 7, switch to the onboarding sequence the same day. Do not keep sending proposal follow-ups.
- Log the exact subject lines you used. The next quiet proposal will not require a rewrite.
- End the 10 minutes by confirming the day-7 reminder is set. If it is not, the second email never leaves.
