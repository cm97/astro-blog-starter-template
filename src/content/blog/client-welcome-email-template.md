A new client just paid. You're happy for about four minutes. Then you open a blank email and try to remember what you sent the last one.

You dig through your sent folder. You find something from March. It mentions a different package and a form link that no longer works. You rewrite it from scratch. Forty minutes gone.

The welcome email is the first thing your client sees after they pay. It sets the tone for every week after it. It should take two minutes to send, not forty.

## What a welcome email has to do

It has one job: make the next step obvious, for both of you.

Every good welcome email answers five questions before the client has to ask:

1. **Did my payment go through?** Say so in the first line.
2. **What happens next?** One or two steps, with dates.
3. **What do you need from me?** One thing, with a deadline.
4. **How do I reach you, and when?** One channel and your hours.
5. **When is the first real thing happening?** The kickoff or first session date.

Miss one, and you'll answer it by email three times this week.

## Template 1 — Freelance project

Subject: Welcome — next steps for [project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Payment received, thank you. I'm looking forward to [the outcome they hired you for].
>
> Here's what happens next:
>
> 1. Fill in this short intake form by [day]: [link]. It takes about 10 minutes.
> 2. We'll meet for a kickoff on [day, time]. Calendar invite is on its way.
>
> The first draft of [deliverable] will be with you by [date].
>
> Email is the best way to reach me. I reply within one business day, [days and hours].
>
> Talk soon,
> [Your name]

## Template 2 — Coaching client

Subject: Welcome — your first session is [day]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Welcome. Your payment came through and your spot is confirmed.
>
> Before our first session on [day, time], please fill in this short form: [link]. It asks what you want to work on, so we don't spend the first session on background.
>
> Here's how we'll work together:
>
> - Sessions: [length], [how often], on [platform]. The link is in your calendar invite.
> - Between sessions: email me at [address]. I reply within one business day.
> - Rescheduling: [your notice period, e.g. 24 hours' notice].
>
> See you on [day],
> [Your name]

## Template 3 — Monthly retainer

Subject: Welcome — how the retainer works

> Hi [Name],
>
> Payment received. Welcome aboard.
>
> Quick rundown of how the month works:
>
> - Included: [what's in the retainer, in one line].
> - Requests: send them to [channel]. I confirm each one within one business day.
> - Updates: you'll get a short status email every [day].
> - Anything outside the list above gets a quote before I start.
>
> First step: send me [access, assets or logins] by [day] so I can start on [first task].
>
> [Your name]

The "outside the list" line matters most. It's the polite version of the scope conversation, and it's much easier to say now than in week six.

## Subject lines that get opened

- Welcome — next steps for [project]
- Welcome — your first session is [day]
- You're in. Here's what happens next.
- [Project]: payment received + next steps

Keep the client's project or the date in the subject. They'll search for this email later.

## Mistakes that cause a week of back-and-forth

- **Asking for five things at once.** They do none of them. Ask for one, with a date.
- **No dates.** "Soon" and "next week" turn into follow-up emails. Write the day.
- **A 12-page welcome packet as an attachment.** Nobody reads it. Put the five answers in the email body.
- **Leaving out your hours.** If you don't set them now, the 10pm texts start in week two.
- **Waiting a day to send it.** Send it within the hour. The client is paying attention right now.
