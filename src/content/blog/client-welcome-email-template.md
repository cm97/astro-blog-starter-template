A client just signed. They are excited and a little nervous. The next email they get from you decides whether that nervousness turns into trust or into doubt.

Most welcome emails fail in one of two ways. They are three lines long and say nothing. Or they are a wall of text that buries the one thing the client needs to do.

This post gives you four versions you can copy today. Pick the one that matches the client. Fill in the brackets. Send it within an hour of the contract and payment landing.

## What every client welcome email needs

A welcome email has one job. It tells a new client what happens next, so they stop wondering.

It needs five things. Nothing else.

1. **A short thank-you.** One line. Not a paragraph about how excited you are.
2. **What happens next, with dates.** The intake form, the kickoff call, the first deliverable.
3. **The one thing they need to do.** Usually the intake form, with a deadline.
4. **How to reach you.** And how fast you reply.
5. **Your name.** A person, not "The Team".

If a sentence in your welcome email does not serve one of those five, cut it.

## Subject lines that get opened

The subject line should say what the email is. A new client opens it because they are waiting for it. Do not get clever.

- Welcome. Here is what happens next
- [Project name]: next steps and your kickoff date
- You're in. One quick thing before we start
- Welcome aboard, [Name]. Your first week

## Version 1: The standard welcome (coaching or consulting)

Use this for most one-to-one clients.

> **Subject:** Welcome. Here is what happens next
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for choosing to work together on [goal or service].
>
> Here is what the next week looks like:
>
> - **By [day]:** Please fill in the short intake form. It takes about five minutes: [link]
> - **[Kickoff date and time]:** Our first call, [30/60] minutes. The calendar invite is on its way.
> - **After the call:** You will get a one-page summary with next steps.
>
> If anything comes up before then, reply to this email. I answer within one working day.
>
> Looking forward to it,
> [Your name]

## Version 2: The freelance project welcome

Use this when the work is a defined project with a delivery date.

> **Subject:** [Project name]: next steps and your kickoff date
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for signing off on [project name]. I'm glad to be working on it with you.
>
> To keep the timeline on track, here is what I need and when:
>
> - **By [day]:** Answers to the intake questions, plus any files or logins: [link]
> - **[Kickoff date]:** A [30]-minute call to confirm scope and the first milestone.
> - **[First milestone date]:** First draft or first deliverable.
>
> The agreed scope is [one-line scope]. Anything outside that gets a quick quote before I start on it, so there are no surprises on the invoice.
>
> Questions any time. Reply here.
>
> [Your name]

The scope line matters. It names the boundary on day one, so the first "one small extra thing" is an easy conversation instead of an awkward one. The [scope creep script](/blog/scope-creep-script/) covers what to say when that request comes.

## Version 3: The retainer or ongoing client welcome

Use this when there is no end date and the work repeats every month.

> **Subject:** Welcome aboard, [Name]. How we will work together
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Welcome. Thank you for trusting me with [area of work].
>
> Here is the rhythm we will run on:
>
> - **Every [weekday]:** a short update on what got done and what is next.
> - **[Monthly/fortnightly]:** a [30]-minute check-in call. The first one is [date].
> - **Requests:** send them to [email or tool]. I reply within [one working day].
>
> To get started, please fill in the intake form by [day]: [link]
>
> Glad to have you,
> [Your name]

Ongoing clients go quiet when the rhythm is vague. Naming the weekday and the channel in the first email sets the habit before it can drift.

## Version 4: The welcome when the kickoff date is not set yet

Sometimes the client signs before you can agree a time. Do not wait to send the welcome. Send it now and ask for the time inside it.

> **Subject:** You're in. One quick thing before we start
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for signing up for [service]. Here is how we get started.
>
> 1. **Pick a time for our kickoff call:** [scheduling link]. Any slot in the next [7] days works.
> 2. **Fill in the intake form before the call:** [link]. About five minutes.
>
> Once both are done, you will get a calendar invite and a short agenda.
>
> Any questions, just reply.
>
> [Your name]

Numbered steps work well here. The client can see there are only two things to do.

## What to leave out of a welcome email

- **Your whole process.** Save it for the kickoff call.
- **Long attachments.** One link to the intake form is enough.
- **Payment details they have already sent.** Confirm payment in the receipt, not here.
- **Three different tools to sign up for.** Add tools one at a time, when they are needed.
- **"Let me know if you have any questions!" as the only next step.** Give them a real next step.

## When to send it

Send the welcome email within an hour of the contract and the first payment landing. The client is paying attention right then. A welcome email that arrives two days later tells them the next six months will feel the same.

If you can't send it within the hour, send a one-line holding note: "Got it. Thank you. Full details coming by end of day." Then send the real one.

## What happens if they don't fill in the intake form

Give it 48 hours past the deadline. Then send one short nudge.

> Hi [Name], just checking in on the intake form. I need it before our kickoff so we can use the time well. Here is the link again: [link]

That is the whole email. If the call is close, ask for the three most important answers by reply instead. The [client intake form template](/blog/client-intake-form-template/) has the five questions worth asking.

## Where the welcome email fits

The welcome email is step one. It sits inside the full [client onboarding checklist](/blog/client-onboarding-checklist-template/), which runs from signed contract to the end of week two. The next step after it is the kickoff call. The [kickoff call agenda](/blog/client-kickoff-call-agenda/) gives you a 30-minute agenda and the summary email to send afterwards.

Write your welcome email once. Save it where you can find it in ten seconds. The next time a client signs, you fill in the brackets instead of starting from a blank screen.
