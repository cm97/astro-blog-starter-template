You sent the proposal. Day 3 bump. Day 7 check. Day 10 close-out. Still nothing. Now it is day 14 and the start date you named is already gone. The calendar block is still there. Every Monday you open the list and see the same name. Silence this long is not a maybe. It is a closed file that you have not closed.

Leaving it open trains you to wait. Closing it at day 14 frees the hours for the clients who already paid and the proposals that still have a date.

## The 3-minute log before you send the day-14 close

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________
Last touch: ________
Start date offered: ________
Price: $________
Day 14 close due: ________
Status after send: closed / reopened / paid
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The close only works when the dates are already written.

## Exact day-14 close email — send today

Subject: Closing the file on [Project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> I have not heard back on the proposal from [date] for [one-line scope] at [price].
>
> I am releasing the calendar hold and closing the file.
>
> If timing changes later, reply and I will reopen it as a new proposal.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No “let me know if you have questions.” The release is the close. The reopen line keeps the door open without leaving the file active.

Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Closing the file on [project] from [date]. Releasing the calendar. Reply if you want it reopened later.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status closed in the log.
2. Delete the hold on the start date from your calendar.
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Closed [Name] day 14.”
4. Do not check the thread for 72 hours. If they reply, treat it as a new proposal and restart the clock.
5. If they pay after the close, send the deposit confirmation and open the onboarding checklist the same hour.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet file from living in your head

- Cap open proposals at four. Anything past day 14 without a reply is closed or parked. A fifth means you are rewriting the week.
- Copy the day-14 close into the same note as the day-3, day-7, and day-10 templates. One open, all four available.
- After you send, set a 72-hour no-check rule. Looking early turns the close into another wait.
- If they reply “still thinking,” send one line: “Understood. The file is closed. Reply by [specific day] if you want it reopened as a new proposal.” Do not leave it open.
- Never discount in the close. The original price stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every closed proposal older than 14 days out of the active list. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.
- Write the exact start date you offered into the close email before you send. Guessing it from memory creates a soft close that does not free the calendar.
- Put a 5-minute calendar block titled “Day 14 close: [Name]” the night before. The block is the trigger. Without it the email never leaves.
- After the send, screenshot or copy the sent time into the log. “I think I sent it” is not a closed file.
- If three day-14 closes land the same morning, send all three before 11am. Do not spread them. One sitting, three files closed.
- Keep a “parked past day 14” note separate from the active list. One monthly scan of that note is enough. Do not let them re-enter the Monday reset.
- When a reply arrives after the close, treat it as a brand-new proposal. Restart the day-3 clock. Do not reopen the old row.
- If they ask for a call after the close, answer: “Happy to talk. First step is a short written yes on the original scope and price so we both know what we are discussing.” Do not jump on a call with an open maybe.
- At Friday close, count how many day-14 closes you sent that week. Write the number on the Monday still-open line. Zero is the goal for open ghosts.
- Pre-write the day-14 subject and body in the calendar note the day the proposal first goes out. The 5-minute block only needs a paste and send.
- Never send the close from your phone while standing in a line. Sit, open the log, fill the three-minute block, then send. The log is the gate.
- After any paid yes that arrives post-close, open the onboarding checklist the same hour. The follow-up sequence ends when money lands.
- Cap the reopen window at 30 days. After that the original price and start date are no longer valid. A new proposal is required.
- If the original start date has already passed by more than 7 days, do not offer to hold it again. The close simply releases the file.
- Keep the five most common objection replies in the same note so you never search under pressure after a late reply.

“I’ll check back next week” keeps the file open and the hours blocked. The day-14 close names the end. You stop carrying the maybe into Monday. The clients who want the work reply. The ones who do not free the calendar.
