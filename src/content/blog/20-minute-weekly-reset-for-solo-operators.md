Monday arrives and the first hour is spent figuring out what you are supposed to be working on. Inbox first. Then the fire. Then the proposal you meant to follow up on. By Thursday the plan is gone.

That is the failure mode. The reset lives in your head. Delivery erases it.

Run this the same window every week. Twenty minutes. Same steps. If Thursday already died, skip the fantasy and run the cash salvage version.

## The 20-minute weekly reset

1. **Clear the board (5 minutes).** Open last week. Write three lines: what shipped, what got pushed and why, what you promised that is still open. Clear open decisions in the inbox. Cap at five minutes. If the inbox still has more than three unanswered client threads after the five minutes, flag them for the follow-up block and move on. Do not clear the whole inbox.

2. **Three priorities (5 minutes).** Write exactly three. One client delivery due or late. One follow-up that is aging. One admin item that unblocks money. Put each on the calendar with a hard block. If a fourth appears, one of the three leaves. Name the block times before you leave this step. "Sometime this week" is not a block.

3. **Follow-ups that die (5 minutes).** Who is waiting on you? Who went quiet after a proposal? Who finished work and has not heard from you? Send one sentence each. No novel. Log the send. Two is the max if the timer is already at four minutes. Exact lines below.

4. **Tomorrow’s first move (5 minutes).** One line. The only thing that has to be true when you open the laptop. Write it on the calendar as the first block. If the first move is a follow-up, the email is already drafted in the previous step.

That is the sequence. Same window every week. If the slot moves, the reset dies. Put the window on the calendar as a recurring event titled "20-min reset". Protect it the same way you protect a client call.

Fill this before you leave the window:

```
Week of: ________
Shipped last week: ________
Still open: ________
This week’s three:
1) ________ — block: ________
2) ________ — block: ________
3) ________ — block: ________
Follow-up 1 sent: ________
Follow-up 2 sent: ________
Tomorrow first move: ________
```

## Exact follow-up lines

- Proposal, no reply: "Still want me to hold the [date] start, or should I release it?"
- Finished work, no check-in: "Checking in after [deliverable]. Anything to tighten, or book the next piece?"
- Invoice that never left: "Invoice for [deliverable] is attached. Due [date]. Reply if the address is wrong."
- Quiet lead after intake: "Intake is in. Kickoff is still [time]. Reply if anything changed."
- Past client, no contact in 60 days: "Quick check — anything new I should know about, or is the last piece still working?"

Send two. Do not perfect them. Copy, swap the bracket, send.

## Wednesday check (5 minutes)

Look at the three. Still the right three? If a fire knocked one off, replace it. Do not add a fourth. Send any follow-up that is now overdue. One sentence. Update the fill-in sheet so Friday does not start from memory.

## Friday close (3 minutes)

Three lines only:

- What shipped this week
- What is still open and why
- The one thing that must be true Monday morning

If you skip Friday, Monday’s clear-the-board step takes longer. Do the three lines before you shut the laptop.

## Cash salvage version (when Thursday already died)

Skip the three priorities. Do this instead:

1. List every open invoice or unpaid proposal older than 7 days.
2. Send one sentence to the oldest two.
3. Put the next unpaid follow-up on tomorrow’s first block.
4. Stop.

The full reset returns next Monday. The salvage keeps money moving this week.

## What still breaks when this only lives in your head

You can run the sheet on a calm Monday. It works until three clients reply at once. Then Wednesday never gets a calendar hold. Thursday becomes inbox, and the proposal follow-up waits for a good moment that is not on the clock. The Friday close never happens, so Monday starts with a blank page again.
