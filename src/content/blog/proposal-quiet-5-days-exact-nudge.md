You sent the proposal on Monday. No reply. Tuesday and Wednesday passed. Now it is day 5 and the thread is still silent. The start date you named is still open. Your calendar still holds the block. Every morning you check the inbox for a yes that has not arrived.

Silence at day 5 is not a maybe. It is an open file with no next date. Leaving it open trains you to wait. A short nudge either gets the reply or starts the close clock.

## The 3-minute log before you send the day-5 nudge

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________
Last touch: ________
Start date offered: ________
Price: $________
Day 5 nudge due: ________
Status after send: replied / still quiet / closed
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The nudge only works when the dates are already written.

## Exact day-5 nudge email — send today

Subject: Still holding [start date] for [Project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Checking in on the proposal from [date] for [one-line scope] at [price].
>
> I am still holding the [start date]. Reply by [day after tomorrow] if you want it, or I will release the calendar.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No “let me know if you have questions.” The hold and the release date make the decision concrete. The reply-by date starts the close clock if they stay silent.

Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Still holding [date] for [project]. Reply by [day] if you want it, or I release the calendar.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status “nudge sent” in the log.
2. Set a calendar reminder for the reply-by date.
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Day 5 nudge sent [Name].”
4. Do not check the thread for 24 hours. If they reply, treat it as a yes or a new proposal and restart the clock.
5. If they pay after the nudge, send the deposit confirmation and open the onboarding checklist the same hour.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet file from living in your head

- Cap open proposals at five. Anything past day 5 without a reply gets this nudge. A sixth means you are rewriting the week.
- Copy the day-5 nudge into the same note as the day-3, day-7, and day-10 templates. One open, all available.
- After you send, set a 24-hour no-check rule. Looking early turns the nudge into another wait.
- If they reply “still thinking,” send one line: “Understood. Reply by [specific day] if you want the [start date], or I release it.” Do not leave it open.
- Never discount in the nudge. The original price stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every proposal older than 10 days with no reply out of the active list. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.

Nudge sent: ________ (date/time)

## Why this works when “I’ll check back next week” fails

“I’ll check back next week” keeps the file open and the hours blocked. The day-5 nudge names the hold and the release date. You stop carrying the maybe into Monday. The clients who want the work reply. The ones who do not free the calendar on the date you named.
