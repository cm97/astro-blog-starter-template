Day 7 reminder went out. Still nothing on day 14. The invoice is two weeks past due. The money has been in their account the whole time. Every extra day is cash you cannot use and a client who has learned that late is acceptable.

Leaving it open past day 14 trains both of you that the due date was a suggestion. The day-14 close names the amount one last time and ends the loop: pay, name a firm date inside 7 days, or the file closes.

## The 2-minute log before you send the day-14 close

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Invoice number: ________
Amount due: $________
Original due: ________
Day 7 sent: ________
Day 14 close due: ________
Status after send: paid / promised / closed
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The close only works when the numbers are already written.

## Exact day-14 invoice close — send today

Subject: Closing invoice [number] — [amount] still open

> Hi [Name],
>
> Invoice [number] for [one-line project] is now 14 days past the due date of [original due] and still open for [amount].
>
> Please send payment today or reply with the exact date it will clear (must be within 7 days). After Friday the file closes on my side and the slot is released.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No “just following up.” The amount, the days late, and the hard end date are the close. The ask is binary: pay or name a date inside the window.

Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Invoice [number] for [amount] is 14 days past due. Pay today or name the clear date (within 7 days). File closes Friday.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status “day 14 close sent” in the log.
2. Set a calendar block for the following Friday labeled “close file [Name] if unpaid.”
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Day 14 close sent [Name] [amount].”
4. Do not check the thread for 48 hours. If they reply with a date inside 7 days, log it and confirm in one line.
5. If they pay, mark paid and send the receipt the same hour. The sequence ends.
6. If Friday arrives and it is still open with no firm date, mark the file closed. Do not send another soft note.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the open invoice from living in your head

- Cap open invoices older than 14 days at one. Anything past the day-14 close without payment or a dated promise is closed or written off. A second means you are financing their business.
- Copy the day-14 close into the same note as the day-1 and day-7 templates. One open, all three available.
- After you send, set a 48-hour no-check rule. Looking early turns the close into another wait.
- If they reply “next month,” answer: “Understood. The file closes Friday unless payment clears or a date inside 7 days is confirmed in writing.” Do not leave it vague.
- Never discount in the close. The original amount stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every invoice that hit day 14 without payment to the “closed unpaid” line. The list is for money still due, not for hopes.
- Write the exact amount and original due date into the close before you send. Guessing it from memory creates a soft ask that does not get paid.
- Put a 5-minute calendar block titled “Day 14 close: [Name]” the night before. The block is the trigger. Without it the email never leaves.
- After the send, screenshot or copy the sent time into the log. “I think I sent it” is not a closed loop.
- If two day-14 closes land the same morning, send both before 11am. Do not spread them. One sitting, two files moved.
- Keep a “past day 14 closed” note separate from the active list. One monthly scan of that note is enough. Do not let them re-enter the Monday reset as soft maybes.
- When a payment arrives after the close, mark it paid the same hour and send the receipt. The follow-up sequence ends when money lands.
- Cap the promised-date window at 7 days. After that the original terms are no longer valid. A new written plan or the file stays closed.
- If the original due date has already passed by more than 14 days, do not offer to wait again. The close simply names the amount still open and the Friday end.
- Keep the three most common delay replies in the same note so you never search under pressure after a late reply.

## Why this works when “I’ll check again next week” fails

“I’ll check again next week” keeps the invoice open and the cash blocked. The day-14 close names the end of the hold. You stop carrying the maybe into Monday. The clients who intend to pay reply or pay. The ones who do not free the calendar and the mental space.
