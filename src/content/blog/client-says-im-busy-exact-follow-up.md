They read the proposal. They reply once: "Looks good, but I'm slammed this week. I'll get back to you when things calm down."

Then nothing. No date. No next step. The thread sits in your inbox while you rebuild the same follow-up from memory every time a lead says they are busy.

You need the exact words that keep the next touch on the calendar without sounding like you are chasing.

## The 60-second log before you reply

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Add one line.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________
Their words: ________
Reply sent: ________
Next touch date: ________
Status: waiting / closed
```

If you cannot name the next date in one line, do not send yet. That date is the whole follow-up.

## Exact reply that books the next touch without the chase

Send this in the same thread before end of day. Subject: Re: [Proposal name]

> Got it. No problem. I will check back on [specific weekday, e.g. next Tuesday]. If that works I will send a one-line note then. If something changes before that, just reply.

One paragraph. Concrete date. Easy out. No "let me know when you are free" that dies in their inbox.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Got it. Checking back next Tuesday. Reply if that changes.

## What you do when they reply

- Yes, Tuesday works: log the date. Do not send another email until that day. On the date, send the one-line check: "Still good for a quick yes/no on the proposal?"
- Different date: accept it. Log the new date. Same one-line check on that day.
- Silence after your date email: one close on day 2. "No reply, so I am closing this for now. Reply if you want the proposal reopened." Mark closed.
- "I am still busy": repeat the same binary once. New date or close. Do not invent a third option.

## Exact one-line check on the booked date

```
Still good for a yes/no on the [project] proposal, or should I close it?
```

Subject: Quick check — [project]

No new proposal. No new pricing. The original thread is the record.

## Timing that keeps the pipeline clean

- Reply the same day they say busy. After 24 hours it looks like you waited to see if they would follow up first.
- Never book a date more than 10 days out. Longer than that and the proposal is cold.
- Cap open "busy" rows at three. More than three means the original proposal was never concrete.
- On the next weekly reset, move every closed busy-thread older than 7 days out of the active list.

## Extra concrete steps that stop "I'll get back to you" from becoming a ghost

1. Write the next date in the log the same hour the "busy" reply arrives. Without that date the thread has no owner.
2. Keep the reply template in the same note as the Monday scan. One open, both available.
3. After any date is booked, add one line to the next weekly reset: "Busy check on [name] due [date]."
4. If they say busy a second time, send the close. Do not book a third date.
5. Never re-send the full proposal on the check-in. The original thread is enough.
6. At the end of the week, the Friday scan lists every booked date still open. Zero is the goal.
7. If you cannot name a date in under 30 seconds, the lead is not real. Close it.
8. Screenshot the original proposal into the project note the day you send the busy reply. Memory of what you offered is not a follow-up.
9. Log every busy reply the same hour it arrives. Blank log means the pipeline dies on a slammed day.
10. On the next weekly reset, move every closed busy-thread older than 7 days out of the active list. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.

## Fill-in after every send or reply

```
Touch sent: ________ (date/time)
Reply: date confirmed / new date / still busy / silent
Decision logged: ________
Next action: one-line check on date / close
```

## Why this works when "let me know when you are free" fails

"Let me know" puts the next move on them. They are already busy. The concrete date puts the next move on you and gives them an easy yes. You stop rebuilding the follow-up from memory every time a lead says the week is slammed.
