A client asks for "one small thing." You say yes. Then another. Then another. By Friday you have done three days of work for the price of one. And you are the one who said yes.

The problem is not the client. The problem is you have no script. You freeze, you smile, you say "sure, no problem," and you eat the cost.

## The script

**When they ask for something outside scope:**

"I can do that. It is outside what we agreed on, so it is a separate line item. Here is what it costs: [price]. Want me to add it?"

That's it. No apology. No "I would love to but." No rambling explanation. One sentence, one price, one question.

## Why it works

It reframes the request as a choice, not a favor. The client gets to decide if it is worth the money. You get to decide if you want to do it. Nobody loses face. Nobody eats free work.

## The three rules

1. **Never say yes in the moment.** Buy time. "Let me check my calendar and get back to you in an hour."
2. **Always name the price.** Even if it is small. Even if it is "$25." The act of naming it resets the frame.
3. **Always give them the out.** "If it is not worth it, no worries — we stick to the original scope."
