They liked the call. They said the proposal looked good. Then: “Let me think about it.”

That is not a yes. It is also not a no. It is the sentence that kills most solo-operator deals because the next email is either silence or a long “just checking in” that makes them do work.

You do not need a new close. You need one short reply that forces a decision or a clean exit, plus a fixed next date so it does not live in your head.

## The 3-minute log the second they say it

Copy this before you reply. Blank fields mean the follow-up does not exist.

```
Name: ________
Call / proposal date: ________
What they said they would think about: ________
Price you named: $________
Start date you offered: ________
Reply due: ________ (today + 2 days)
Status: waiting / replied / closed
Next action: ________
```

Two minutes. Do it while the message is still open. Tomorrow erases the exact words.

## The exact reply (send within 2 hours)

Subject: Re: [Project name]

> Got it. To keep the [start date] open I need a yes or no by [day after tomorrow].
>
> Reply “yes” and I send the invoice + the one thing I need from you.
>
> Reply “later” and I release the date and note to check in [month they named].
>
> Either way is fine. Just need the word so it does not sit open.

One paragraph. Binary. Dated. No “let me know if you have questions.”

## If they go quiet after that reply

Send this on the date you named, before 11am, mid-week only.

Subject: Closing the file on [Project name]

> Closing the loop. Releasing the [start date] unless you reply “yes” by end of day.
>
> If timing is off, reply “later” and I’ll note the month.

Then stop. No fourth email.

## What you do when they reply

- Yes: invoice the same hour. Welcome note with start date and the one asset you need. Add them to next Monday’s onboarding row.
- Later: write the month. One note that month. Status = parked.
- Price or scope question: restate what is in and out. Do not cut the number. Restate the binary close in the same reply.
- No: mark closed. Do not argue.

## Why “let me think about it” dies without this

The sentence lets them leave the decision open forever. Your reply turns it into a yes, a later, or a clean no. The date keeps it out of your head so Thursday delivery does not erase the follow-up.

You can paste it today. It works until a slammed week. Then the two-hour window passes, the log is blank, and the prospect sits in sent items until you notice two weeks later.
