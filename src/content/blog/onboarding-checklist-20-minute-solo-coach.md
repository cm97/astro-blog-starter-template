A new client paid. You have twenty minutes before the next call or the inbox reopens. If the welcome, the intake, and the kickoff are not sent in that window, the client sits in limbo and week two starts with silence.

This is the sequence that fits in twenty minutes. Same steps every time. No new tools.

## The 20-minute onboarding

Do these in order. Stop at twenty minutes even if step 5 is unfinished. The first three are the gate. If you only finish the first three, the client still has something in their inbox the same hour they paid.

1. **Welcome email (4 min).** Subject: Welcome — [project]. Payment received. Next is the intake. Kickoff booked for [day/time]. Anything outside the listed scope is a separate quote.
2. **Intake form (3 min).** Five fields max. Link in the welcome. Deadline: three business days.
3. **Kickoff hold (3 min).** Calendar invite for 30 minutes within three business days. Title: [Client] kickoff. Agenda is five bullets you paste once.
4. **Private note (4 min).** Name, paid amount, first-week goal, day-7 date, who decides. Same note you use for the weekly reset.
5. **Day-7 block (3 min).** 15-minute hold titled [Client] day-7. The email is one line you write once.
6. **Log and close (3 min).** Confirm the three sends happened. Write the next date. Close the tab.

## Exact welcome (paste)

> Hi [Name],
>
> Payment received for [project]. Welcome.
>
> Next is the short intake (link below). Once it is back I send the kickoff agenda.
>
> Kickoff is on the calendar for [day/time]. Reply if that slot does not work.
>
> This start covers [exact deliverable]. Anything outside that is a separate quote.
>
> [Your name]

Subject line that already works: "Welcome — [project] — payment received"

## Exact intake fields (copy into the form)

- What has to be true in 30 days for this to be a win?
- What did you already try that failed?
- Who else has to say yes?
- What would make you walk away?
- Anything else before kickoff?

Deadline line you paste under the link: "Please send this back by [three business days]. Kickoff stays on the calendar either way."

## Kickoff agenda (paste into the invite)

- Confirm the 30-day win from the intake (2 min)
- First deliverable and date (5 min)
- Who decides and who gets copied (3 min)
- Day-7 check-in channel (2 min)
- Out of scope this week (2 min)

Invite description line: "Five bullets. Bring the intake answers. We leave with a first deliverable date."

## Day-7 one-liner

> Quick check on [project]. Still on track for [next date], or do you need me to hold?

If the intake is missing, the same line becomes: Still need the intake so the first deliverable can move. Link again.

Calendar title that survives a scan: "[Client] day-7 check"

## Extra concrete steps that stop the silence

- Copy the original scope line into the private note the same minute you send the welcome. If it is not written, you cannot defend it later.
- Set the intake deadline to three business days, not "when you get to it." The calendar invite is the real deadline.
- If they pay on a Friday, still send the welcome the same day. Monday silence is the failure mode this checklist exists to kill.
- Put the kickoff invite on the calendar before you close the welcome email. Unsent invites become "let’s find a time" threads.
- Write the day-7 hold while the payment screen is still open. Future-you will not remember the date.
- If the client replies with a question in the welcome thread, answer it in one line and re-paste the intake link. Do not open a new conversation.
- At the end of the 20 minutes, screenshot or copy the three sent items into the private note so the weekly reset can see them without searching email.
- Cap the intake at five questions. A sixth question is the reason the form never comes back.
- If they book the kickoff outside the three-day window, keep the day-7 hold. Do not move both.
- Log the next action date in the same note you will open on Monday. The weekly reset finds it there or it disappears.
