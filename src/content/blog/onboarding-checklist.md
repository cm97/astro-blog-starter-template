Onboarding is where most one-person businesses quietly bleed. You get a new client, you wing it, you forget a step, and by Friday you're apologizing for something that should have been automatic.

Here's the checklist I actually run. Steal it.

## Before the kickoff call

- [ ] Confirm the scope in writing (even a 3-line email).
- [ ] Send the intake form (name, goal, deadline, budget, "what does done look like").
- [ ] Block 30 minutes on the calendar for the kickoff.
- [ ] Prepare the onboarding checklist itself — don't improvise it live.

## During the kickoff

- [ ] Restate the goal in one sentence. Get a yes.
- [ ] Walk through the 3–5 steps you'll take.
- [ ] Agree on the first deliverable and its date.
- [ ] Confirm how you'll communicate (email, Slack, whatever) and how fast you'll reply.

## After the call

- [ ] Send a recap email within 2 hours — steps, dates, next action.
- [ ] Add the client to your weekly reset list.
- [ ] Set a reminder for the first check-in.

## The rule

If a step isn't on the checklist, it doesn't exist. The checklist is the floor. Run it on the messy weeks, not just the calm ones.
