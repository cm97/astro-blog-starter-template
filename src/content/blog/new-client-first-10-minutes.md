A new client just paid. Your stomach drops because you know what comes next: digging through old emails for the welcome note, inventing an intake form on the spot, and hoping you remember to send the kickoff agenda before Thursday.

That scramble is why week two feels chaotic. The work starts before the process exists.

Run this the minute the Stripe notification hits. Ten minutes. Same sequence every time. If you only have seven, stop after step 3.

## The 10-minute sequence

1. **Reply to the payment email (or send a fresh one) with the welcome.** Subject: Welcome — [project name]. Three lines: you received payment, here is what happens next, here is the one thing you need from them today. Attach or link the intake form.
2. **Send the intake form.** One form, not a Google Doc they have to edit. Five fields max. The answers fill the kickoff.
3. **Put the kickoff on the calendar.** 30 or 45 minutes within the next three business days. Title: [Client] kickoff. Invite them. The hold exists before you close the laptop.
4. **Drop a private note to yourself.** Client name, start date, what they said they want in the first week, next check-in date. If it is not written, Thursday will erase it.
5. **Set the first check-in.** Day 7. Not “I’ll follow up sometime.” A date on the same list you use for the weekly reset.

That is the whole sequence. No new tools. No Notion rebuild. Copy the welcome and the intake once, then reuse them.

Fill this before you leave the payment notification:

```
Client: ________
Paid: ________ (date / amount)
Welcome sent: yes / no — time: ________
Intake sent: yes / no — link: ________
Kickoff booked: ________ (day/time)
Private note written: yes / no
Day-7 check-in on calendar: ________
```

## Exact welcome email

> **Subject:** Welcome — [project name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Payment received. Welcome.
>
> Next step is the short intake form (link). Once that is back I will send the kickoff agenda and the first-week checklist.
>
> Kickoff is on the calendar for [day/time]. Reply if that slot does not work.
>
> [Your name]

Send it before you do anything else. The form and the calendar hold do the rest of the work.

## Exact intake fields

Five fields. Phone-friendly. No account required.

- Most important outcome from this engagement?
- What have you already tried that did not stick?
- Who else needs to be in the loop on decisions?
- What would make this feel like a clear win in 90 days?
- Anything else I should know before kickoff?

Deadline in the form or the email: three business days. If it is not back by then, the day-7 check-in becomes a poke, not a progress call.

## Kickoff agenda (paste into the invite)

- Confirm the outcome they wrote on the intake (2 min)
- Walk the first deliverable and the date it ships (5 min)
- Name who decides and who gets copied (3 min)
- Agree the day-7 check-in and the channel (2 min)
- Anything out of scope this week (2 min)

The invite exists before you write the agenda. The agenda is five bullets. Do not redesign it per client.

## Private note (the part that dies in your head)

```
Client: ________
Start: ________
First-week goal: ________
Next check-in: ________
Who decides: ________
Open risk: ________
```

Write it in the same place you keep the weekly reset. If the note is not there, the client is not onboarded.

## Day-7 hold

Put a 15-minute block on the calendar the same day you send the welcome. Title: [Client] day-7. The block is the reminder. The email is one line:

> Quick check — did the first deliverable land as expected, or is something blocking it?

If they have not returned the intake, the same block becomes:

> Still need the intake before we can move the first deliverable. Here is the link again.

## What still breaks when this lives only in your head

You can copy the email above into a note right now. It works until the next client signs on a slammed Thursday. Then the welcome gets delayed, the intake is reinvented, and the kickoff slides to next week.

Client dates live in threads you will not reopen until they complain. The day-7 check-in waits for a good moment, and the moment is never Monday at 9:12. The private note is a feeling instead of a written card.
