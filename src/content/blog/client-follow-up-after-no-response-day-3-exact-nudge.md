You sent the proposal Monday. No reply. Tuesday passed. Now it is day 3. The start date is still on the calendar. You check the thread, rewrite a softer version in your head, and close the app. Silence at day 3 is not a maybe. It is an open file with no next date. Leaving it open trains you to wait.

A short nudge either gets the reply or starts the close clock. Send the exact one. Same words every time.

## The 3-minute log before you send the day-3 nudge

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________
Start date offered: ________
Price: $________
Day 3 due: ________
Status after send: replied / still quiet / closed
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The nudge only works when the dates are already written.

## Exact day-3 nudge email — send today

Subject: Still holding [start date] for [Project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Checking in on the proposal from [date] for [one-line scope] at [price].
>
> I am holding the [start date] through end of this week. Reply by Friday if you want it held, or I will release the calendar.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No “let me know if you have questions.” The hold and the release date make the decision concrete. The reply-by date starts the close clock if they stay silent.

Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Checking in on [project] proposal. Holding [start date] through Friday. Reply by then if you want it held, or I release.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status “day 3 sent” in the log.
2. Keep the calendar hold for now. Do not delete it yet.
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Day 3 sent [Name].”
4. Do not check the thread for 24 hours. If they reply, treat it as a yes or a new date and update the log.
5. If they pay, send the deposit confirmation and open the onboarding checklist the same hour.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet file from living in your head

- Cap open proposals at five. Anything past day 3 without a reply gets this nudge. A sixth means you are rewriting the week.
- Copy the day-3 nudge into the same note as the day-5 and day-7 templates. One open, all available.
- After you send, set a 24-hour no-check rule. Looking early turns the nudge into another wait.
- If they reply “still thinking,” send one line: “Understood. Reply by Friday if you want the [start date] held, or I release it.” Do not leave it open.
- Never discount in the nudge. The original price stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every proposal older than day 10 without a reply out of the active list. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.
- Write the exact start date you offered into the email before you send. Guessing it from memory creates a soft nudge that does not free the calendar.
- Put a 4-minute calendar block titled “Day 3 nudge: [Name]” the night before. The block is the trigger. Without it the email never leaves.
- After the send, screenshot or copy the sent time into the log. “I think I sent it” is not a closed loop.
- If three day-3 nudges land the same morning, send all three before 11am. Do not spread them. One sitting, three files moved.
- Keep a “parked past day 3” note separate from the active list. One weekly scan of that note is enough.
- When a reply arrives, update the start date or close the file the same hour. Do not let a yes sit overnight.
- If they ask for a call, answer: “Happy to talk. First step is a short written yes on the original scope and price so we both know what we are discussing.” Do not jump on a call with an open maybe.
- At Friday close, count how many day-3 nudges you sent that week. Write the number on the Monday still-open line. Zero open ghosts is the goal.
- Pre-write the day-3 subject and body in the calendar note the day the proposal first goes out. The 4-minute block only needs a paste and send.
- Never send the nudge from your phone while standing in a line. Sit, open the log, fill the three-minute block, then send. The log is the gate.
- After any paid yes, open the onboarding checklist the same hour. The follow-up sequence ends when money lands.
- Cap the hold window at the named Friday. After that the original start date is no longer valid without a new proposal.
- If the original start date has already passed, do not offer to hold it again. Move straight to the next close.
- Keep the five most common objection replies in the same note so you never search under pressure after a late reply.

## Exact replies for the day-3 replies you actually get

When they finally answer, do not invent a new email. Paste one of these.

**Still thinking / need more time:**
> Understood. Reply by Friday if you want the [start date] held. Otherwise I release the calendar.

**Can we hop on a call?**
> Happy to. First send a one-line yes on the original scope and price so we know what the call is about. Then pick a time.

**Price is high / can you do less?**
> Scope and start date stay the same. Price is [original]. Reply yes if you want the hold through Friday.

**I need to check with [partner/boss]:**
> Got it. The hold stays through Friday. Reply by then with yes or release so the calendar stays clean.

**Send more info:**
> The proposal is the info. One line is enough: want the [start date] held through Friday or released?

**They pay:**
Open the onboarding checklist the same hour. Welcome, intake, kickoff hold. Do not celebrate first.
