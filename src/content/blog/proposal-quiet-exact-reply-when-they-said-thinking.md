They read the proposal. They said “let me think about it.” Then silence. Day 2. Day 4. The start date is still on your calendar. The price is still in your head. You keep checking the thread because a soft “just checking in” feels safer than forcing the answer.

That silence is not thinking. It is an open file with no next date. Leaving it open trains you to wait and trains them that your follow-ups can be ignored.

You need the exact short reply that turns the maybe into a yes, a later, or a clean no before the week disappears.

## The 60-second log before you send the reply

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________
They said: let me think about it
Start date offered: ________
Price: $________
Reply-by date: ________ (today + 2 days)
Status after send: replied / still quiet / closed
```

If the start date or price is blank, do not send. The reply only works when those are already written down.

## Exact reply that forces the decision

Send this before noon on a weekday. Subject: Holding [start date] for [Project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Still holding the [start date] for the [one-line scope] at [price].
>
> Reply “yes” by [day after tomorrow] and I send the invoice + the one thing I need from you.
>
> Reply “later” and I release the date and note to check in [month].
>
> Either way is fine. Just need the word so it does not sit open.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. Binary. Dated. No apology. No “let me know if you have questions.” The hold and the release date make the decision concrete.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Still holding [date] for [project]. Reply yes or later by [day] so I know whether to keep it.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status “decision reply sent” in the log.
2. Set a calendar reminder for the reply-by date titled “Close [Name] think-about-it.”
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Decision reply sent [Name].”
4. Do not check the thread for 24 hours.
5. If they pay after the reply, send the deposit confirmation and open the onboarding checklist the same hour.

## Exact next email if they stay quiet

On the reply-by morning, before 11am, send this and stop.

Subject: Closing the file on [Project name]

> Closing the loop. Releasing the [start date] unless you reply “yes” by end of day.
>
> If timing is off, reply “later” and I’ll note the month.

No fourth email. Mark closed. The next touch is either the month they named or never.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the open-thread pile-up

1. Write the start date and price in the log the day you send the proposal. Without those the reply has nothing to point at.
2. Keep this reply template in the same note as the Monday scan. One open, both available.
3. After you send the decision reply, move the row out of “waiting on maybe” the same hour. The scan only shows live items.
4. If three of these land in the same week, look at the original proposal. Vague scope creates “let me think about it.”
5. Never reopen a closed row without a new reply from them. Memory of “they seemed interested” is not a reason to email again.
6. At the weekly reset, count how many decision replies you sent. Zero is a signal you are still carrying dead threads.
7. Screenshot or paste the original “let me think about it” message into the project note the day it arrives. The exact words make the binary fair.
8. Cap open “think about it” rows at three. A fourth means you are collecting maybes instead of closing.

## Fill-in after every send

```
Decision reply sent: ________ (date/time)
Reply-by date set: yes / no
Reply: yes / later / no / silent
Status logged: closed / reopen / parked
Next action: invoice + onboarding / 90-day check / none
```

## Why this works when “I’ll check back next week” fails

“I’ll check back next week” keeps the file open and the hours blocked. The binary reply names the hold and the release date. You stop carrying the maybe into Monday. The clients who want the work reply. The ones who do not free the calendar on the date you named.
