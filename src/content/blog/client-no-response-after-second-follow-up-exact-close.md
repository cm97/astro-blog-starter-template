You sent the proposal. You followed up once. You followed up again. Nothing.

The silence is louder than a no. You keep the thread open in your head because closing it feels like admitting the deal is dead. Meanwhile the next lead arrives and you still have no script for the final touch.

You need the exact email that ends the chase without burning the relationship or leaving the door cracked forever.

## The 60-second log before you send the close

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Add one line.

```
Name: ________
Last touch: ________
Days silent: ________
Close sent: ________
Status: closed / reopen later
Next check: ________
```

If you cannot name the last date you emailed them, do not send yet. That date is the whole reason the close is fair.

## Exact close that ends the chase cleanly

Send this on a weekday before noon. Subject: Closing the loop on [Project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> I haven't heard back on the [proposal / follow-up] from [date]. I'm going to close this out on my side so it doesn't sit open.
>
> If timing changes or you want to revisit, just reply and I'll reopen it. Otherwise no need to respond.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No "just checking in." The date makes it specific. The reopen line keeps the door open without making you chase.

Do not add a discount. Do not ask why. Do not send a third soft follow-up.

## What you do when they reply (or don't)

- They reply and want to move forward: treat it as a fresh start. Send the next concrete step the same day (invoice, kickoff date, or intake). Log status as reopen. Do not restart the old silence clock.
- They reply with a no or "not now": thank them, mark closed, set a 90-day check if it makes sense for your business. One line only.
- Silence after the close: done. Mark closed. Do not send a fourth email. The next time you touch them is either a 90-day check or never.
- They reply weeks later out of nowhere: same as reopen. Do not reference the old silence.

## Timing that makes the close feel professional

- Send only after at least 7 full days of silence since the second follow-up.
- Weekday morning only. Never Friday or after 3pm.
- Cap open "waiting on reply" rows at three. More than three means the Monday scan is not running.
- If they were a past client, use the same words but change the subject to "Closing the loop on next steps."

## Extra concrete steps that stop the open-thread pile-up

1. Write the last-touch date in the log the moment you send any follow-up. Without that date the close has no anchor.
2. Keep the close template in the same note as the Monday scan. One open, both available.
3. After you send the close, move the row out of the active follow-up list the same hour. The scan only shows live items.
4. If three closes land in the same week, look at the original proposal. Vague scope creates silence.
5. Never reopen a closed row without a new reply from them. Memory of "they seemed interested" is not a reason to email again.
6. At the weekly reset, count how many closes you sent. Zero is a signal you are still carrying dead threads.
7. Screenshot or paste the original proposal date into the project note the day you send it. The close only works when the timeline is visible.
8. If you cannot decide whether 7 days is enough, default to 10. The exact number matters less than having one.


## Why this works when "I'll just leave it" fails

Leaving it open trains you that silence is normal and trains the client that your follow-ups can be ignored forever. The clean close frees the mental slot for the next live lead and gives them a low-pressure way back in if they actually want it.
