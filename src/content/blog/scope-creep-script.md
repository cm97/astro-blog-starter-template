## Why a flat no loses the job

"That's not in scope" ends the sentence and starts the argument. They do not know the next move. They pushed a boundary and got a wall.

The reply that keeps the client is two options. Add it as a paid line. Or park it until this job wraps. No third option of squeezing it in.

## The 20-minute response

Do these in order. Do not answer yes in the same message the ask arrived.

1. **Pause in one line.** Reply: "Got it. Let me check what we already locked and I will come back in an hour." Time box: 1 minute. Do not price it in that first reply.
2. **Open the original scope.** Signed note, kickoff email, or the onboarding checklist. Highlight the line that covers this request, or the line that does not. If you cannot find a line, the scope was never written. That is the bug.
3. **Name the extra work in minutes.** "About 90 minutes" beats "this is getting bigger." Write the number before you write the email.
4. **Put a price on those minutes.** Use the rate you already charge for a similar add-on. If you do not have one, use the number on the original proposal divided by the hours you quoted. Write it down. Do not invent a discount because the ask sounded small.
5. **Send two options only.** Add it now at that price, or park it. No "I will just squeeze it in."
6. **Wait for a written yes.** A thumbs-up in a voice note is not a yes. One line back: "Reply YES and I add the line item."
7. **Log the decision the same day.** Save the date on the same card.

## Exact phrases for common pushes

When they ask for something outside scope:

> I can do that. It is outside what we agreed on, so it is a separate line item. Here is what it costs: [price]. Want me to add it, or should we keep it for after this project wraps?

No apology. No "I would love to but." One sentence, one price, one choice.

If they push with "can't you just…?":

> I can. Just not inside the current fee. Same two options: add it now at [price], or we park it.

If they say they thought it was included:

> That piece was not in the original list. I can add it as a line item at [price], or we stay with what we already locked. Which do you want?

If the brief changed mid-project:

> This is a different project than the one we scoped. I can write a short addendum with the new deliverable and price, or we finish the original and start a new one after.

If you do not want the extra work at all:

> I am not taking that on this round. We finish [original deliverable] on [date]. If you still want [ask] after that, I can quote it as its own job.

## If the ask arrives as a text

Do not price it in the text thread. Texts get a yes by accident.

1. Reply in under two minutes: "Got it. I will check the locked list and send two options by [time today]."
2. Move the decision to email or the same project thread. Subject: "Add-on for [ask] — two options."
3. Paste the two-option script. Do not add a third option in the text because they used a smiley.

Text you can send as-is:

> Got the note on [ask]. Checking it against what we locked. Two options by [time] — add it as a line item, or park it. Not starting it in this thread.

Phone version under 160 characters:

> Got [ask]. Checking locked list. Two options by [time]: add at price or park. Not starting in this thread.

## What to do after they pick

**They reply YES.** Send one confirmation the same day:

> Added. [Ask] is a line item at [price], due [date]. Original delivery stays [date]. I will not start the extra until this note is in the thread.

Then invoice the line or add it to the next invoice before you open the file. Work that is not on an invoice is still a favor.

Change-order line, one sentence, paste under the yes:

> Change: add [ask]. Price: [price]. Due: [date]. Original [deliverable] still due [date]. This note is the yes.

**They say park it.** Write the date on the same card:

> Parked. I will not start [ask]. We stay on [original deliverable] by [date]. If you want it after [wrap date], reply then and I will price it again.

**They go quiet.** Do not start the extra. On day 3, one bump, same thread:

> Still need a yes or a park on [ask] at [price]. Original job stays on [date] either way.

If they do not answer by day 6, mark the ask declined and keep the original date. Silence is not a yes.

Day-6 close, one sentence:

> No reply on [ask] at [price], so I am marking it declined. Original delivery stays [date]. Reply if you want a fresh quote after that date.

## Three rules that keep it from collapsing

1. Never say yes in the moment. Buy the hour.
2. Always name the price. Even if it is small. Naming it resets the frame.
3. Always give the out. "If it is not worth it, we stick to the original scope."

## Weekly reset hook so Thursday cannot erase it

On the Monday reset, add one row: "Open scope asks waiting on yes." If none, write none.

- Screenshot or copy the original scope into the project note the day the ask arrives. Do not rely on searching email later.
- If the ask is a third or fourth extra, send the full script again. Do not soften the second one.
- Set a 24-hour timer on the two-option email. If no reply by then, send the day-3 bump without starting the work.
- Log the ask on the same card as the original onboarding note so the weekly reset can see it.
- Cap open scope asks at three. A fourth means you are rewriting the engagement. Close or re-scope the whole job.
- After any yes, write the invoice line the same hour. The file does not open until the line is on the invoice.
- At Friday close, mark every parked ask with a date or mark it declined. Unmarked parks become free work next month.
- Add a line to the next Monday reset: "Scope asks waiting on written yes: [count]." Zero is the goal.
