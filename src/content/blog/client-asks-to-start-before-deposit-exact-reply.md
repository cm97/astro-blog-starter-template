They said yes on the call. Then the message arrives: "Can we just get started on the first piece while I sort the deposit?"

You feel the pull. Saying no feels like losing the client. Saying yes means you just opened a free hour on a project that still lives in your head.

This is the reply that keeps the yes and the boundary. You send it once. The deposit is the start date. Nothing before it.

## The 90-second log before you reply

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Add the row.

```
Name: ________
Verbal yes date: ________
Deposit amount: $________
Deposit due: ________
"Start early" ask received: ________
Reply sent: ________
Status: waiting on deposit / started / closed
```

If the deposit amount is blank, do not reply yet. Name the number first.

## Exact reply that holds the start

Send within two hours of the ask. Subject: Re: [Project name] — start after deposit

> Hi [Name],
>
> Glad we're moving. I start the first piece the day the $[amount] deposit clears. That keeps the timeline and the scope locked for both of us.
>
> I'll send the invoice link again here so it's easy: [invoice link]. Once it lands I'll send the intake and kickoff times the same day.
>
> [Your name]

One short paragraph. No apology. No "I can squeeze in a little." The deposit is the only unlock.

Do not attach sample work. Do not open a new scope conversation in the same thread.

## What you do when they reply (or don't)

- Deposit lands the same day: send the welcome + intake the same hour. Log status as started. Move them to the onboarding row.
- They ask for a smaller first step: restate the original deposit amount and the original start condition. One line. Do not invent a new cheaper entry.
- Silence after this reply: wait 3 full days, then send the same day-3 deposit nudge you already use for missing deposits. Do not invent a softer version.
- They push again with "just one file": reply with the same sentence. Cap at two total messages on this ask. Then park it.

## Timing that keeps the boundary

- Reply the same day the ask arrives. Delay turns it into a negotiation.
- Weekday hours only. Never after 6pm or on Friday.
- Cap open "waiting on deposit" rows at three. More than three means the Monday scan is not running.
- If they are a past client, use the same words. The rule does not change because the relationship is warm.

## Extra concrete steps that stop free work

1. Write the deposit amount and due date in the log the moment the verbal yes happens. Without that number the reply has nothing to hold.
2. Keep this template in the same note as the Monday scan and the unpaid-invoice lines. One open, all available.
3. After you send the reply, move the row out of "active call" the same hour. The scan only shows live items.
4. If three of these asks land in the same week, look at the original proposal. Vague "we can start whenever" language creates the ask.
5. Never start a file, send a sample, or open a shared folder until the deposit clears. Memory of "they seemed ready" is not a reason to open the work.
6. At the weekly reset, count how many "start early" replies you sent. Zero is a signal you are still saying yes in the moment.
7. Paste the original deposit line from the proposal into the reply so the number is visible in the thread.
8. If you cannot decide whether to give them "just the outline," default to the reply above. The exact boundary matters more than the feeling.

## Why this works when "sure, let's just start" fails

Saying yes to early work trains the client that the deposit is optional and trains you that free hours are the cost of closing. The reply frees the mental slot for the next live lead and gives them a clear, low-friction way to unlock the work if they actually want it.
