## The 4-minute log the minute you know you are late

Open a note before you open the email.

Fill every blank.

```
Client: ________
Original due: ________
What was promised: ________
New due you can actually hit: ________
Reason in one phrase: ________
Update sent: yes / no — time: ________
Status: late / on new date / delivered
```

Four minutes.

Do it while the calendar still shows the original date.

If you wait until Monday the slip is already a week.

Copy.

Fill the three blanks.

Send.

Do not add the story of why.

Do not offer a discount.

Do not open a call unless they ask.

## What you do the same hour

1. Write the new date on the log and on the calendar block.
2. Delete the old calendar hold so it cannot confuse you later.
3. Add the client to next Monday’s “Still open” row so the weekly reset sees the new date.
4. If the new date is more than five days out, set a day-3 reminder to yourself only: confirm you are still on the new date.

## If they reply with a problem

- New date does not work: ask for one alternative day in the same thread. Write it on the log the same hour. Do not negotiate price.
- They want a call: book 15 minutes max. Agenda is already written: confirm the new date, confirm scope is unchanged, confirm the next check-in.
- Silence after 48 hours: treat it as accepted. Do not chase.

## Timing rules that keep the relationship intact

- Send the update the day you know, before 11am if possible.
- Never Friday afternoon. The message sits all weekend and looks worse on Monday.
- Cap the slip notice at one email. A second apology the next day makes it bigger.
- If you are more than five days late, use the same template and name the real number of days. Do not round down.
