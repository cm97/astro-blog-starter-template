You sent the invoice. They said the work was done. Then nothing.

The payment is not dead. You just stopped chasing it because writing another follow-up feels awkward and you already did the delivery.

Most people either wait and hope or send a long "just checking on the invoice I sent last week" that forces them to dig up the number and the context. Neither gets money.

These two lines do. Run them on a fixed cadence so the silence does not live in your head.

## The 3-minute log the minute you hit send

Copy this before you close the tab. Blank fields mean the follow-up does not exist yet.

```
Client: ________
Invoice amount: $________
Sent: ________ (date / time)
Due date you named: ________
Day 3 due: ________
Day 7 due: ________
Status: waiting / paid / closed
```

Three minutes. Do it while the invoice is still open. Thursday will erase it if you wait.

Put two 5-minute blocks on the calendar labeled "Invoice: [name] day 3/7". If the date lands on Friday, move it to Thursday morning. Do not skip.

## Day 3 — Confirm it landed

Send on day 3, Tuesday or Wednesday morning before 11am. Not Friday. Not after 3pm.

> Confirming the invoice for $[amount] landed. Reply with the date you expect to send it and I will mark it paid.

One sentence. The amount creates the record. The date request makes them reply instead of ignoring.

Subject: Re: Invoice [number or project name]

Log the send. Move on. Do not rewrite it.

## Day 7 — Binary choice

If day 3 got silence:

> Quick one: is the $[amount] invoice paid, or should I resend the link?

Still one line. Still a binary. People reply to yes/no faster than open-ended questions.

Same window: before 11am, mid-week. Same subject: Re: Invoice [number or project name]

## Exact subject lines that do not look like spam

- Day 3: Re: Invoice [project name]
- Day 7: Re: Invoice [project name]

Keep the Re: even if it is a new thread. It reads as continuation.

## Exact timing windows that raise reply rate

- Day 3: Tuesday or Wednesday morning, before 11am.
- Day 7: same, mid-week only.
- Never Friday. Never after 3pm. The reply rate drops and the thread dies over the weekend.
- If day 3 falls on a Friday, send Thursday instead. Do not slide it to Monday and lose the window.

## What you do when they reply

- Paid: mark the log closed the same hour. Send the short thank-you only if you already have the next date written. Do not open a new scope conversation.
- Date named: write the date on the same row. One note that day. Not a drip. Status = waiting on date.
- Problem with the invoice: restate the amount and the original due date. Do not cut the number. One line: "The amount is $[X]. Due date stays [Y]. Want the link resent or is there a different date?"
- No: mark closed. Do not argue. Do not send a third email this week.

If you cannot name the next date after a paid reply, the client is not retained. That is a separate checklist.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet

1. Paste the original due date into the log the same minute you send the invoice. Without the date the day-3 line has nothing to hold.
2. Keep the two lines in the same note as the Monday reset. One open, both available.
3. Cap at two emails. A third becomes a novel and they mute you.
4. After any paid, add the client to next Monday’s “Still open” so the day-14 retention touch does not live only in your head.
5. On Day 7, if they ask a question, answer in one paragraph and restate the due date. Do not open a new scope conversation.
6. After Day 7, do not send a third email because you felt guilty on a quiet Friday.
7. At the next weekly reset, add a row: "Open invoices past Day 7." If none, write none.

## Why these work when “just checking in” fails

“Just checking in” asks them to remember the invoice, find the number, and write context. These ask for one date or one word. The amount makes the decision time-bound. You stop rebuilding the follow-up from memory every time an invoice goes quiet.
