The final deliverable is in. Invoice is paid. You feel done.

Most operators stop here. The client is happy in the moment, then life moves on and the next project goes to someone else who asked.

You need three short emails that run on a fixed cadence after the work ends. Not a long thank-you note. Not a soft "let me know if you need anything." Exact lines that ask for the referral and keep the door open for the next engagement.

## The 3-minute log the day the final file leaves

Copy this while the project is still open in your head. Blank fields mean the next touch does not exist.

```
Name: ________
Project: ________
End date: ________
What they got: ________
Price paid: $________
Day 1 wrap-up due: ________
Day 7 referral due: ________
Day 30 check-in due: ________
Status: open / referred / rebooked / closed
Last touch: ________
Next action: ________
```

Three minutes. Do it the same day the final deliverable is confirmed. Thursday will erase the details if you wait.

Put three 6-minute blocks on the calendar labeled "Post-project [name] day 1/7/30". If the date lands on Friday, move it to Thursday morning.

## Day 1 — Wrap-up that closes the loop cleanly

Send the same day the final file is accepted or the last call ends. Before 3pm on a weekday.

Subject: [Project name] — complete

> [Name], [project] is complete. You have [exact deliverable]. Everything we agreed is in the folder / link below.
>
> If anything needs a quick fix in the next 7 days, reply to this email. After that the file is closed.
>
> Glad we got this done.

One short paragraph. No soft pitch. No "let me know if you need more." The 7-day window is the boundary.

After send: update log with time. Status stays open. Do not check for a reply for 48 hours.

## Day 7 — Direct referral ask

If day 1 got no issues (or the issues were fixed):

Subject: One favor on [Project name]

> Glad the [deliverable] is working. If you know one person who needs the same result, reply with their name or forward this note. I will treat the intro the same way I treated yours.

That is the whole email. One ask. No long story. No "no pressure." The forward option removes the work of writing an intro for them.

After send: log the time. If they name someone, reply within the hour with a short thank-you and the next step. If silence, do not send a second referral ask.

## Day 30 — Soft rebook check

Still quiet or they already referred:

Subject: Quick check on [Project name]

> Checking in 30 days later. Is the [deliverable] still doing what you needed, or is there a next piece that would help?
>
> If timing is off, no problem. Reply "later" and I will note it. Otherwise I am free the week of [specific week].

Binary plus a concrete week. People reply faster when the next available slot is already named.

After send: log the time. If they say later, park with the month they name. If they want the next piece, send a one-paragraph scope and price the same day. Do not open a new discovery call unless the scope is unclear.

## Exact timing windows

- Day 1: same day the work is accepted, before 3pm, weekday only.
- Day 7: Tuesday or Wednesday morning before 11am.
- Day 30: any weekday before 11am.
- Never Friday afternoon. The thread dies over the weekend.
- If day 7 falls on a Friday, send Thursday instead.

## What you do when they reply

- Names a referral: thank them the same hour. Send a short note to the new person that references the original client by name. Log the referral as closed and start a new proposal row.
- Wants the next piece: send one paragraph of scope + price + start date. Restart the three-touch clock only after the new proposal is sent.
- "Later": write the month. One note that month. Status = parked.
- Silence after day 30: mark closed. Do not send a fourth email.
- Problem with the original work: fix it inside the 7-day window. After that, treat it as a new request with a new price.

## Concrete scripts for the three most common replies

**1. "I will keep you in mind / will refer if I think of someone"**

> Appreciate it. If a name comes up, just forward this note or reply with their email. No extra work on your side.

Do not chase again. The ball is with them.

**2. "We might need something similar in Q2 / later this year"**

> Got it. I will note [month / quarter]. Reply then or I will send one short check-in that month. No other messages until then.

Park the row. One note only.

**3. "Can you also do [new thing]?"**

> Yes. That is a separate piece. Scope is [one sentence]. Price is [number]. Start date available [week]. Want me to send the one-pager?

Do not fold it into the old project. New log line. New price.

## Extra concrete steps that stop finished projects from disappearing

1. Write the exact deliverable sentence in the log the day the final file leaves. Without it the day-30 line has nothing specific to point at.
2. Keep the three emails in the same note as the Monday reset. One open, both available.
3. Cap open post-project rows at five. Older ones past day 30 are closed or parked.
4. After any paid next engagement, add the client to next Monday’s onboarding row the same day money lands.
5. Never send a fourth email because you felt the relationship was "warm." Three is the cap.
6. At the weekly reset, add one line: "Post-project rows past day 7." If none, write none.
7. Screenshot or copy the final acceptance email into the log. Memory of "they were happy" is not a date.
8. If the client is a repeat, skip day 1 and go straight to the referral ask on day 7 of the newest project.
9. When they name a referral, send the intro the same hour. Delayed intros kill the warm connection.
10. Keep the three scripts in the same note so you never search for them under pressure.
