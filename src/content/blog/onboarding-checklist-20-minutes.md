A new client pays. Then you spend two hours digging through old threads for the welcome email, inventing an intake form, and hoping you remember the kickoff agenda before the week collapses.

That is the failure mode. The process lives in your head. Thursday erases it.

Run this checklist the day payment clears. Twenty minutes. Same steps every time. If you only have twelve, stop after step 4.

## The 20-minute onboarding checklist

1. **Send the welcome email (4 minutes).** Subject: Welcome — [project name]. Three lines: payment received, what happens next, the one thing you need from them today. Link or attach the intake.
2. **Send the intake form (3 minutes).** Five questions max. Name, most important outcome, what they already tried, who else decides, anything else. Deadline: 3 days.
3. **Book the kickoff (4 minutes).** 30–45 minutes in the next three business days. Title: [Client] kickoff. Invite them now. The hold exists before you close the laptop.
4. **Write the private note (4 minutes).** Client name, start date, first-week goal from the intake or payment note, next check-in date. If it is not written, it dies.
5. **Set the day-7 check-in (5 minutes).** Put it on the same list as your weekly reset. Not “sometime.” A date.

That is the whole sequence. No new tools. Copy the welcome and the intake once. Reuse them.

Fill this before you leave the laptop:

```
Client: ________

Intake sent: yes / no — link: ________

Scope line logged: yes / no
```

## Exact welcome email

> **Subject:** Welcome — [project name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Payment received. Welcome.
>
> Next step is the short intake form (link). Once that is back I will send the kickoff agenda and the first-week checklist.
>
> Kickoff is on the calendar for [day/time]. Reply if that slot does not work.
>
> This start covers [exact deliverable]. Anything outside that is a separate quote.
>
> [Your name]

Send it before you do anything else. The form and the calendar hold do the rest of the work.

## Exact intake fields

Five fields. Phone-friendly. No account required.

- Most important outcome from this engagement?
- What have you already tried that did not stick?
- Who else needs to be in the loop on decisions?
- What would make this feel like a clear win in 90 days?
- Anything else I should know before kickoff?

Deadline in the form or the email: three business days. If it is not back by then, the day-7 check-in becomes a poke, not a progress call.

## Kickoff agenda (paste into the invite)

- Confirm the outcome they wrote on the intake (2 min)
- Walk the first deliverable and the date it ships (5 min)
- Name who decides and who gets copied (3 min)
- Agree the day-7 check-in and the channel (2 min)
- Anything out of scope this week (2 min)

The invite exists before you write the agenda. The agenda is five bullets. Do not redesign it per client.

## Private note (the part that dies in your head)

```
Client: ________
Start: ________
First-week goal: ________
Next check-in: ________
Who decides: ________
Open risk: ________
Amount paid: $________
Out of scope: ________
```

Write it in the same place you keep the weekly reset. If the note is not there, the client is not onboarded.

## Day-7 hold

Put a 15-minute block on the calendar the same day you send the welcome. Title: [Client] day-7. The block is the reminder. The email is one line:

> Quick check — did the first deliverable land as expected, or is something blocking it?

If they have not returned the intake, the same block becomes:

> Still need the intake before we can move the first deliverable. Here is the link again.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the leak

- Paste the original scope line into the private note the day payment clears. Clients forget what they signed.
- If intake is not back in 48 hours, send one nudge the same day. Do not wait for day 7.
- After kickoff, send the one-paragraph summary within 60 minutes. Do not let it sit until tomorrow.
- Add the client to next Monday’s weekly reset “Still open” row so week two is not silence.
- Keep the welcome, intake, and kickoff templates in the same folder as the weekly reset and the scope reply. One open, all available.
- Cap the first-week touches at four. More becomes a blast and the habit dies.
- If any day is missed, send the missed touch the same day you notice it. Do not restart the whole list.

## Day-by-day first week (run the list, do not invent)

**Day 0 (payment day)**  
- Welcome sent  
- Intake link sent with 3-day deadline  
- Kickoff hold on calendar  
- Private note written  
- Day-7 block set  
- Scope sentence in welcome  

**Day 2**  
- Confirm intake received or send the 48-hour nudge  
- Fill-in: “Still need the intake before kickoff so we use the time well. Link again: [link]. Deadline stays [date].”  

**Day 3–4**  
- Review intake, write 2 clarifying questions  
- Kickoff held or rescheduled with new date written  
- Kickoff summary sent within 60 minutes: “Here is what we covered: [outcome], first deliverable due [date], day-7 check on [date]. Reply if I missed anything.”  

**Day 6**  
- Mid-point status line: “Quick status: [what moved]. Next piece due [date]. Anything blocked on your side?”  

**Day 7**  
- Day-7 check-in sent  
- Client added to weekly reset “Still open” so week two stays quiet-free  

If any day is late, send the late version the same day. Never skip because you are behind.

## Exact lines that protect the cash

Add these three before you close the laptop on day 0:

1. Scope line in welcome: "This start covers [exact thing]. Anything outside that is a separate quote."
2. Private note amount: write the dollar figure and "paid in full / deposit only". If deposit, write remaining date next to it.
3. Pre-written 48-hour nudge on calendar:

> Hi [Name] — still need the intake so kickoff is useful. Link again: [link]. Deadline stays [date]. Reply if something is blocking it.
