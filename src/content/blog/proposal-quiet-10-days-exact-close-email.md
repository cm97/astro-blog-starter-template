You sent the proposal. Day 3 bump went out. Day 7 check went out. Now it is day 10 and the thread is still silent. The start date you named is slipping. Your calendar still holds the block. Every morning you open the inbox expecting a yes that does not arrive.

Silence past day 10 is not a maybe. It is an open file with no next date. Leaving it open trains you to wait. Closing it frees the hours for the clients who already paid.

## The 4-minute log before you send the close-out

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________
Last touch: ________
Start date offered: ________
Price: $________
Day 10 close due: ________
Status after send: closed / reopened / paid
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The close-out only works when the dates are already written.

## Exact close-out email — send today

Subject: Closing the loop on [Project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> I have not heard back on the proposal from [date] for [one-line scope] at [price].
>
> I am releasing the [start date] so the calendar stays clear.
>
> If timing changes later, reply and I will reopen it.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No “let me know if you have questions.” The release is the close. The reopen line keeps the door open without leaving the file active.

Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status closed in the log.
2. Delete the hold on the start date from your calendar.
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Closed [Name] day 10.”
4. Do not check the thread for 48 hours. If they reply, treat it as a new proposal and restart the clock.
5. If they pay after the close, send the deposit confirmation and open the onboarding checklist the same hour.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet file from living in your head

- Cap open proposals at five. Anything past day 10 without a reply is closed or parked. A sixth means you are rewriting the week.
- Copy the close-out into the same note as the day-3 and day-7 templates. One open, all three available.
- After you send, set a 48-hour no-check rule. Looking early turns the close into another wait.
- If they reply “still thinking,” send one line: “Understood. The [start date] is released. Reply by [specific day] if you want it reopened.” Do not leave it open.
- Never discount in the close-out. The original price stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every closed proposal older than 14 days out of the active list. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.

## Fill-in after every close-out

```
Close sent: ________ (date/time)
Calendar hold removed: yes / no
Reply within 48h: yes / no
If yes: new proposal / paid / still quiet
Next action: onboarding opened / closed for good
```

## Why this works when “I’ll check back next week” fails

“I’ll check back next week” keeps the file open and the hours blocked. The close-out names the end. You stop carrying the maybe into Monday. The clients who want the work reply. The ones who do not free the calendar.
