The hardest part of running a small business isn't the work. It's keeping track of what the work is.

Most solo and small-team businesses I know operate in one of two modes: either there's a real planning system that takes 45 minutes on Sunday night and slowly gets abandoned over time, or there's no system at all and priorities live entirely in someone's head.

Both have the same failure mode: things fall through on busy weeks.

Here's a weekly reset that takes 20 minutes, works the same way every week, and doesn't depend on you having energy when you run it.

## Why weekly resets fail

Planning systems usually die for one of three reasons:

**They're too long.** If the weekly review takes an hour, it competes with everything else demanding that hour. One busy week skips it. Then two. Then it's a thing you used to do.

**They require too much decision-making.** If every week involves deciding which format to use, which priorities matter, and what counts as "done," the system is just giving you more to think about.

**They live somewhere you don't check.** A weekly review doc in a folder you open once a month isn't a system — it's a filing cabinet.

## The 20-minute reset

The goal is to answer three questions:

1. **What's actually on my plate right now?** (not what I think is there — what's actually there)
2. **What's the one thing that has to happen this week?**
3. **What almost slipped through last week?**

That's it. Three questions, one pass through your inboxes and task list, one clear priority.

Here's how to structure the 20 minutes:

**Minutes 1–5: Clear**  
Scan every inbox — email, voice, notes app, sticky notes, whatever you use. Move anything actionable into your task system. Archive the rest. This isn't processing, just moving.

**Minutes 5–12: Review**  
Look at everything in your task system. Mark anything that's actually done as done. Note anything that slipped. Identify what's overdue.

**Minutes 12–17: Prioritize**  
Pick the single most important thing that has to happen this week. Write it at the top of your week. Everything else is secondary — this one doesn't get bumped.

**Minutes 17–20: Plan**  
Write down the three or four other things you intend to do this week. Not a full schedule — just a short list that tells you what you committed to. You'll check it Thursday.

## Why this works when everything else doesn't

The reason this holds up on busy weeks is that it doesn't require you to feel like doing it.

A 20-minute routine with a fixed format is short enough that you can run it when you're tired, when you're behind, and when the week already looks bad. Those are exactly the weeks where skipping it costs the most.

The format also keeps it consistent. You're not deciding each week what the review covers — you're running the same checklist in the same order. Consistent process produces consistent results.

## The one thing you need to make it stick

A pre-built checklist somewhere you see it at the start of the week.

Not a document you have to open. Not a system you have to remember to check. A checklist that is already waiting for you when Monday starts — already formatted, already in the right place, ready to run.

That's the difference between a practice you intend to have and a practice you actually run.
