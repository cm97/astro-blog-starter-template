The leak is not the sales call. It is the week after they pay, and the month after you deliver.

You send the files. They say thanks. Then nobody books the next piece, because the next piece was never on a checklist. You remember them when cash dips. By then the check-in feels awkward, so you skip it.

Retention is five timed touches. Same words. Same calendar. Not a CRM.

## Run this the day they pay

Do not wait for a kickoff mood. The first 48 hours decide whether they feel handled or forgotten.

1. Send the welcome in the first hour. One sentence on what starts, one date for the first deliverable, one question only they can answer.
2. Attach or link the intake. Do not rebuild the questions from the last thread.
3. Put the kickoff on the calendar before you close the laptop. If they have not picked a time, offer two slots.
4. Write the next date on your client list: what they get, when they get it, who owes the reply.


Welcome, paste and fill:

> Paid and on the calendar. First piece is [deliverable] on [date]. One thing I need from you before then: [single ask]. Reply with that and we are moving.

## While the work is open

One pulse a week. Not a status novel.

Friday, five minutes, every active client:

- What shipped this week
- What is next and the date
- One question: anything you want adjusted before I keep going?

Paste:

> This week: [one line]. Next: [deliverable] on [date]. Anything you want changed before I lock that?

If they do not reply, do not invent a new email. Resend the same three lines on Tuesday with "Still good to lock [date]?"

## The day you hand it off

Delivery without a next step is how a good project dies.

Send three blocks in one email:

1. What they have (file names, links)
2. What "done" means (the scope you already agreed)
3. The next piece, with a price and a start date, or a check-in date if they are not buying yet

Paste:

> [Deliverable] is in [link]. Scope we closed: [one line]. Next piece, if you want it: [offer] at [price], start [date]. If not now, I will check in on [date two weeks out]. One line either way is enough.

## After delivery — four touches, already written

Put these on the calendar the day you deliver. If they are not on a date, they will not happen.

**Day 14 — tighten or book**

> Two weeks after [deliverable]. Anything you want tightened, or should we book the next piece?

**Day 30 — proof**

> Quick ask: one or two sentences on what changed after [deliverable]? I will not edit your words. If it is easier to skip, skip it.

**Day 60 — referral, one name**

> Know one person dealing with [same problem]? A name is enough. I will not pitch them until you say I can.

**Day 90 — quiet list**

> Still on my list. Want a 15-minute look at [next problem], or should I close the file?

One question each. An easy out. No "just circling back."

## Quarterly, 15 minutes

Open the client list. Mark anyone with no touch in 90 days.

For each name, send the day-90 line or remove them from active. Do not write a custom re-engagement essay. The point is the date, not the prose.

```
Quiet client: ________
Last touch: ________
Send day-90 line: yes / close file
```

## What still breaks if this only lives in your head

- The welcome waits until you "have time," and the first 48 hours are already gone
- Delivery emails stop at the link, so the next sale never gets a date
- Day 14, 30, 60, and 90 live in memory and lose to the client who is emailing today
