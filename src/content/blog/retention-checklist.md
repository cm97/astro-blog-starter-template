## Why the quiet happens

1. The welcome waits for a free hour. The first 48 hours are already gone.
2. Delivery stops at the link. No next price, no next date.
3. Day 14, 30, 60, and 90 live in your head and lose to whoever emailed today.

A client who felt handled on day one and got a dated next step is the one who replies. A client who got a file and silence is a cold lead you already did the hard part for.

## The day they pay — 20 minutes

Do these in order. Do not wait for a kickoff mood. Time box the whole block at 20 minutes. If it takes longer, you are redesigning onboarding instead of running it.

1. **Send the welcome in the first hour.** One sentence on what starts, one date for the first deliverable, one question only they can answer. Time box: 4 minutes. Put the send on a calendar hold if you cannot do it in the hour.
2. **Attach the intake you already use.** Do not rebuild the questions from the last thread. If you do not have a form, send five fields: outcome, deadline, who approves, what is out of scope, where files live. Time box: 3 minutes.
3. **Put kickoff on the calendar before you close the laptop.** Offer two slots if they have not picked. A kickoff that is "sometime next week" is not a kickoff. Block 25 minutes. Time box: 3 minutes.
4. **Write the next date on the client list.** What they get, when they get it, who owes the reply. Same place as the weekly reset. Time box: 4 minutes.
5. **Name what is out.** One line in the welcome so week two does not start with a free extra. Time box: 2 minutes.
6. **Schedule the four later touches now.** Day 14, 30, 60, 90 as calendar holds with the exact line already in the description. Time box: 4 minutes.

Fill this before you send the welcome:

```
Client: ________
Paid: ________ on ________
First deliverable: ________ by ________
Kickoff: ________ (two slots offered: ________ / ________)
Out of scope: ________
Waiting on them for: ________
Day 14 hold: ________
Day 30 hold: ________
Day 60 hold: ________
Day 90 hold: ________
```

Welcome, paste and fill:

> Paid and on the calendar. First piece is [deliverable] on [date]. Out of this round: [one line]. One thing I need from you before then: [single ask]. Reply with that and we are moving.

If they do not reply to the single ask by the next morning, resend the same note with the subject "Need this before [date]." Do not write a new essay. Time box the resend at 2 minutes.

## While the work is open — Friday, 5 minutes

One pulse a week per active client. Not a status novel. Put the Friday block on the calendar the same day they pay.

- What shipped this week
- What is next and the date
- One question: anything you want adjusted before I keep going?

Paste:

> This week: [one line]. Next: [deliverable] on [date]. Anything you want changed before I lock that?

If they do not reply, do not invent a new email. Tuesday, same thread:

> Still good to lock [date]?

A scope ask that lands in that reply does not get a yes in the same message. Reply: "Got it. I will check what we locked and come back with two options." Price it, or park it. Do not squeeze it into Friday's five minutes. Log the ask on the same card as the weekly reset.

Friday log:

```
Client: ________
Shipped: ________
Next + date: ________
Reply needed?: yes / no
Scope ask?: priced / parked / none
```

## The day you hand it off

Delivery without a next step is how a good project dies. Send three blocks in one email. Time box: 8 minutes. Do this the same hour the files leave, not the next day.

1. What they have (file names, links)
2. What "done" means (the scope you already agreed)
3. The next piece, with a price and a start date, or a check-in date if they are not buying yet

Paste:

> [Deliverable] is in [link]. Scope we closed: [one line]. Next piece, if you want it: [offer] at [price], start [date]. If not now, I will check in on [date two weeks out]. One line either way is enough.

Then put the four dates below on the calendar before you close the thread. If they are not on a date, they will not happen. Confirm the day-14 hold is still there.

Hand-off card:

```
Delivered: ________ on ________
Next offer: ________ at $________ start ________
Or check-in date: ________
Day 14 still set?: yes / moved to ________
```

## After delivery — four touches, already written

**Day 14 — tighten or book**

> Two weeks after [deliverable]. Anything you want tightened, or should we book the next piece?

**Day 30 — proof**

> Quick ask: one or two sentences on what changed after [deliverable]? I will not edit your words. If it is easier to skip, skip it.

**Day 60 — referral, one name**

> Know one person dealing with [same problem]? A name is enough. I will not pitch them until you say I can.

**Day 90 — quiet list**

> Still on my list. Want a 15-minute look at [next problem], or should I close the file?

One question each. An easy out. No "just circling back." Send from the calendar hold. If the hold was skipped, send the same line late. Do not rewrite it.

If they reply yes to the next piece, send the same welcome the same day. Do not start the new work on a maybe. Restate price, start date, and what is out. Put the new day-14 on the calendar before you close the laptop.

## Quarterly, 15 minutes

Open the client list. Mark anyone with no touch in 90 days. Do this on the first Monday of the quarter, same slot as the weekly reset.

For each name, send the day-90 line or remove them from active. Do not write a custom re-engagement essay. The point is the date, not the prose.

```
Quiet client: ________
Last touch: ________
Send day-90 line: yes / close file
Next name: ________
```

A closed file is usable. An open silence is not a pipeline. Cap the block at 15 minutes. If more names remain, they wait until next quarter.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet

- Book the day-they-pay 20 minutes as a hold the moment payment clears. Unscheduled welcomes get pushed to tomorrow.
- Paste the out-of-scope line into the welcome every time. Blank out-of-scope becomes free work.
- After hand-off, add one row to the weekly reset: "Open next-offer waiting on reply." If none, write none.
- If day 14 is missed, send it on day 16 with the same words. Late is better than rewritten.
- On the next Monday reset, list every client with a touch due this week. Cap at the three you will actually send.
