None of these show up as one bad day. They show up as money you cannot point at: a new client who goes quiet in week two, a proposal that never gets a day-6 note, a Monday plan that is dead by Thursday. You are the system. When you get slammed, the system stops.

Here are the three signs, and the 20-minute list for each. Run one today. Do not start a redesign.

## 1. You are the only one who knows where anything is

Not because you are controlling. Because the client file is a memory. Access, price, next step, and what is out of scope live in your head. Thursday wipes Thursday.

20-minute client note. One page. Fill it the hour they pay.

- Name:
- Amount paid and date:
- Next step and date:
- Who decides:
- What is out of scope:
- Where the files live (one link):

If any line is blank, you will rebuild it from memory the next time they email. That is the whole sign.

## 2. Every new client means starting from scratch

Onboarding is a reconstruction exercise: welcome email rewritten, intake invented, kickoff with no agenda, no 48-hour check. The first week is improvised. Week two they go quiet and you think it was the work.

Same-day start line. 20 minutes after payment clears. Five items, in order.

1. Welcome email. Subject: You are in — start date [date]. Say what you need, when you reply, and the first deliverable date.
2. Intake, five fields only: goal, deadline, access, who decides, what is out of scope.
3. Kickoff on the calendar. 25 minutes. Agenda already written: outcome, constraints, first deliverable, next check-in.
4. 48-hour follow-up on the calendar: "Confirm I have everything from intake." One paragraph.
5. The one-page client note from sign 1.

Paste this welcome line. Swap the brackets. Do not rewrite it.

> Hi [Name] — payment received. We start [date]. I need [access or file] by [date]. I reply [window]. First thing you get is [deliverable] on [date]. Kickoff is [time]. Out of scope for this start: [one line].

## 3. Follow-up lives in your head, not on a date

Nothing forgets like a busy brain. A lead only hears from you when you remember. A client only gets a check-in when you feel guilty. You never see the money leave.

One bump, then a close. Same thread. No new novel.

Day 3, after a quote:

> Checking this landed. Same offer: [one-line outcome] at $[amount]. Reply yes and I send the invoice, or no and I close the file.

Day 6, still quiet:

> Closing this on my side Friday unless I hear otherwise. Offer still stands at $[amount] if you want it later — reply yes and I reopen the file.

Log it the minute you send the proposal, or the follow-up dies:

```
Name:
Sent:
Price:
Day-3 date:
Day-6 date:
Status: waiting / yes / no / parked
```

Two bumps is a chase. One bump plus a close-the-file note is a system. The Follow-Up Email Templates ($19) are the five ready-to-send versions of this. The Digital System keeps those next to onboarding so a yes on Friday still has a welcome note on Monday.

## The Thursday test

Friday or Monday, 20 minutes, same chair.

1. Inbox to zero decisions. Reply, archive, or kill. No later pile.
2. Three priorities. Kill one. Keep two.
3. Open the follow-up log. Send the one note that is due today.
4. Write tomorrow's first task. One line.

If you skip it, signs 1–3 come back by Thursday. The Weekly Reset Checklist ($15) is that list alone. Most people who already need onboarding and follow-ups should skip the single pack and take the system.
