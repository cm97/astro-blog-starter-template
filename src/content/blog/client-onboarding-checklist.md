Onboarding is where most one-person businesses quietly bleed time. Not because they don't care — because every new client becomes a mental reconstruction exercise: what did I send last time? What did I forget? What did I promise in the first call?

This is the checklist I actually run. It is built to survive a slammed week, not a calm one.

## Before the client signs

1. **Confirm the scope in writing.** One short email or message: what you're doing, what you're not, and what "done" looks like. If it's not written, it's a future argument.
2. **Collect the basics once.** Name, contact, preferred channel, timezone, any deadlines. Put it in one place — not five tabs.
3. **Set the first milestone.** A date, a deliverable, a check-in. Vague starts become vague finishes.

## In the first 48 hours

4. **Send the welcome pack.** What happens next, how to reach you, what you need from them. No mystery, no "I'll figure it out."
5. **Schedule the kickoff.** Even 15 minutes. A calendar hold beats a floating intention.
6. **Log the first follow-up.** The moment you say "I'll check in Thursday," it goes on a list — not in your head.

## The part people skip

7. **Close the loop.** When the milestone is done, confirm it in writing. "Done — here's what you have." Closure is what turns a transaction into a repeat client.
