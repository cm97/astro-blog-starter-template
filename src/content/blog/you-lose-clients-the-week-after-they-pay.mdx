The invoice clears. Then silence.

You felt the sale land and went back to the inbox. No welcome. No next date. No mid-week note. The client paid and got a void. That is where one-off projects stay one-off.

This is not a delivery problem. It is the missing week after payment. Run the same seven days every time. Do not decide whether they "seem fine." The checklist decides.

## Why the week after payment leaks cash

You already spent the expensive part: the call, the proposal, the follow-up that got the yes. Replacing that client means doing it again. Keeping them means three short touches in seven days, plus one internal card the same hour they pay.

Most solo operators have a get-the-yes sequence and no keep-the-yes sequence. The second one is where retainers die. The hour after payment is the only hour that is still empty. Use it.

## Day 0 — same day the payment hits (10 minutes)

Send this before you close the laptop. One email. Do not attach a novel.

Fill-in:

```
Subject: You are in — here is the first week

Hi [Name],

Payment landed. First deliverable: [one thing]. You will have it by [date].
Next note from me: [day, time].
One thing I need from you by [date]: [file, login, or decision].
Out of scope for this start: [one line].

Reply to this if that date does not work.
```

Rules:

1. One deliverable, not a menu.
2. A date they will hear from you, even if the work is not done.
3. One ask so they reply. A reply is the first lock-in.
4. One out-of-scope line. If it is blank, the extra request lands in your lap on day 2.

If you cannot name the next date, you do not have onboarding. You have a paid task sitting in your head.

Paste clock for day 0, in order:

1. Send the welcome (4 minutes).
2. Put the kickoff or first delivery date on the calendar (1 minute).
3. Write the internal card below (3 minutes).
4. Stop. Do not start the work in the same sitting unless the deliverable is a file you already have.

## Day 1 — internal card, not another email (5 minutes)

Write this where you already keep client notes. If it only lives in the payment receipt, Thursday will erase it.

```
Client: ________
Paid: ________
First deliverable: ________ by ________
Their homework: ________ due ________
Next touch: Day 2 if no reply, else Day 3, ________
Who owes a reply: me / them
Out of scope: ________
```

## Day 2 — only if the welcome got no reply (3 minutes)

A paid client who does not answer the welcome is not "busy in a good way." They did not lock the date. One bump. Then wait for day 3.

Subject: Need one reply to hold [date]

> Hi [Name] — payment is in. I still need [file / login / decision] by [date] to hit [deliverable date]. Reply yes if that date holds, or send the file. If I do not hear back, the date moves to [new date].

Do not add a second offer. Do not apologize for asking. One bump is a date. Two bumps on day 2 is a chase.

## Day 3 — status, even if nothing is finished (5 minutes)

Silence after payment feels like abandonment. Send two sentences.

> Still on track for [deliverable] on [date]. No decision needed from you unless [their homework] is stuck.

Do not apologize for not being done. Do not add a new scope item in this note.

If they still have not sent the file, repeat the date-move line from day 2. Do not invent a new reason.

## Day 5 — only if they owe you something

If their homework is late, one line:

> Still need [file / login / decision] to hit [date]. If I do not have it by [tomorrow], the date moves to [new date].

Name the slip. Do not absorb it for free.

If they already sent everything, skip this email. A status note with nothing to decide trains them to ignore you.

## Day 7 — one win and one question (8 minutes)

Send a short progress share and make them reply.

> Here is what is done: [one concrete result]. Next piece is [next deliverable] on [date]. One question: do you want [option A] or [option B] on that piece?

Two options. Not an open "thoughts?" A reply is the retention signal. No reply by day 9 means they go on the Monday follow-up list, not into a new novel.

Day 9 line, if still quiet:

> Closing the open question on [A or B]. I will proceed with [A] on [date] unless you reply with B before then.

Then do the work. An unanswered question is not a pause button.

## What you do not do this week

- Do not wait for them to ask what happens next.
- Do not rebuild the welcome email from old threads.
- Do not start extra work they mentioned in the payment thread until it is a written line item with a price or a no.
- Do not skip day 3 because the work "is going fine." Fine is how they go quiet.
- Do not discount the next piece to get a reply. Silence is a date problem, not a price problem.

Time box for the whole week of touches: under 30 minutes. If it takes longer, you are writing a status report, not locking the client.

## Wednesday tie-in

On the weekly reset, this client is one line: next deliverable and next date. If you cannot name both, they are drifting. Drift is how a paid project never becomes a second invoice.

Monday card for this client only:

```
Client: ________
Next deliverable: ________
Next date: ________
Touch due today: welcome / day 2 / day 3 / day 7 / none
```
