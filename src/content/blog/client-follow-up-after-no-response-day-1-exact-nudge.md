You hit send on the proposal or the intake request. Twenty-four hours later the thread is silent. You open the email to check, rewrite a longer version in your head, then close the tab because you do not know what to say that does not sound desperate.

That silence is not a rejection. It is a missing next action. Without a day-1 line written in advance, the follow-up dies the first time delivery gets loud.

## The 90-second log before you send the day-1 nudge

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
What you sent: proposal / intake / invoice
Sent date and time: ________
One-line scope or ask: ________
Price or next step: ________
Day-1 due: ________ (today)
Status after send: still quiet / replied / closed
Next touch if no reply: day 3
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The nudge only works when the original ask is already written.

## Exact day-1 nudge email — send today

Subject: Re: [Project or ask]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Checking this landed. Still holding [start date or next step] for [one-line scope] at [price or “no charge for the intake”].
>
> Reply yes and I send the next piece, or tell me to release it.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. Binary. Same thread. Do not add a new attachment or a longer explanation.

Phone SMS version (under 160 chars):

> Checking the [proposal/intake] landed. Still holding [date] for [scope]. Reply yes or release?

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status “day 1 sent” in the log.
2. Set the day-3 calendar hold or reminder the same minute. Unscheduled day-3 dies.
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Day 1 sent [Name].”
4. Do not check the thread for 24 hours. If they reply, treat it as a yes or a new date and update the log.
5. If they pay or return the intake, open the onboarding checklist the same hour.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet file from living in your head

- Cap open proposals and intakes at five total. A sixth means you are rewriting the week instead of sending the nudge.
- Never discount in the day-1 line. The original price stays the record.
- If day 1 lands on a Friday, send it Thursday morning. Do not slide it to Monday and lose the window.
- Keep the template in the same note as the weekly reset. One open, both available.
- After any yes, the follow-up sequence ends. The onboarding sequence starts the same day.
- On the next weekly reset, move every item older than day 3 without a reply into the day-3 or park column. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.
- Write the exact start date or deadline you offered into the email before you send. Guessing it from memory creates a soft nudge that does not free the calendar.
- Phone-only: dictate the three lines into Notes, paste, send, mark done. The calendar hold still has to exist.

## Fill-in after every send

```
Touch sent: ________ (date/time)
Channel: email / SMS
Reply: yes / new date / release / silent
Decision logged: ________
Next action: day 3 / onboarding / closed
```

## Why this works when “just checking in” fails

“Just checking in” trains them that silence is fine and trains you that you will rewrite the message every time. The binary day-1 line makes the choice visible and time-bound. You stop rebuilding the follow-up from memory the morning after you hit send.

## What still breaks when the nudge only lives in a note

You can paste it today. It works until a slammed Thursday. Then the thread sits open, the original scope sentence is gone, and by Friday you have already decided the lead is dead because sending felt harder than doing delivery.

## Phone-only day-1 (under 2 minutes)

When you are not at a desk. Same binary. No new app.

1. Open the original thread or Notes where the log lives.
2. Dictate or paste the SMS version. Fill the brackets. Send.
3. Immediately set a phone reminder for day 3 titled “[Name] day-3 or release.”
4. Mark the log “day 1 sent” and close the thread. Do not refresh it.

Phone log you keep at the top of Notes:

```
[Name] day-1 sent [time]
Scope: ________ Price: ________
Day-3 reminder: set
```

## If they reply “busy” or “still thinking”

Do not rewrite the original offer. Use one of these and log the new date.

> Understood. I will hold [start date] until [specific day 3–5 days out]. Reply by then with yes or release.

Or for true busy:

> No problem. Next touch is [day 3 date]. Reply before then if the start date needs to move.

Both keep the original price and scope. Both free your calendar. Do not open a new thread.

## What to do if day 1 is already late

If more than 36 hours have passed and you never sent day 1, send the same line today anyway. Then set day 3 from this moment, not from the original send. Late is better than never. The log still records the real send time.
