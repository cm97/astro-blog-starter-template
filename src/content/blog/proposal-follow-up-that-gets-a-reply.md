You sent the proposal. Then silence.

The deal did not die. You stopped chasing it.

Most freelancers and solo operators send one proposal, wait, then either ghost or send a long “just checking in” that sounds desperate. Neither works. The ones who close more send short, specific follow-ups on a fixed cadence.

Here is the exact sequence that gets a reply without the awkwardness.

## The 4-minute log the minute you hit send

Copy this before you close the tab. Blank fields mean the follow-up does not exist yet.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________ (date / time)
Start date you named: ________
Deliverable named: ________
Price named: $________
Day 3 due: ________
Day 7 due: ________
Day 14 due: ________
Status: waiting / replied / closed
```

Four minutes. Do it while the proposal is still open. Thursday will erase it if you wait.

## Day 3 — One line, one ask

Do not recap the whole proposal. Do not ask if they have questions. Send before 11am, mid-week. Not Friday.

> Still want me to hold the [start date], or should I release it?

That is it. One sentence. The date creates urgency. The “or release it” gives them an easy out so they reply.

Subject: Re: [Project name] proposal

Send it. Log the date. Move on. Do not rewrite it.

## Day 7 — Specific next step

If day 3 got nothing:

> Quick one: do you want the [deliverable] locked for [date], or should I drop the hold?

Same idea. Still one line. Still a binary choice. People reply to binary choices.

Same window: before 11am. Same subject: Re: [Project name] proposal

## Day 14 — Close the loop or kill it

If still quiet:

> Closing the loop on the [project] proposal. If the timing is off, no problem — reply “later” and I’ll note it. Otherwise I’ll release the date.

Subject: Closing the file on [Project name]

This one either gets a reply or cleanly kills the lead so it stops living in your head.

## Exact timing windows

- Day 3: Tuesday or Wednesday morning, before 11am.
- Day 7: same, mid-week only.
- Day 14: any weekday before 11am.
- Never Friday. Never after 3pm. The reply rate drops and the thread dies over the weekend.

If a due date lands on Friday or Monday, move it to the nearest Tuesday or Wednesday morning. Do not skip.

## What you do when they reply

- Yes: send the invoice the same hour. Welcome note with the start date and the one thing you need from them. Mark the log closed.
- Later: write the month they named. One note that month. Not a drip. Status = parked.
- Price question: restate what is in and what is out. Do not cut the number. One line: "The [price] covers [exact deliverable]. Outside that is a separate quote. Still want the hold?"
- Scope question: "Original scope is [in]. [New ask] is out unless we add it at [price]. Which do you want?"
- No: mark closed. Do not argue. Do not send a fourth email.

If you cannot name the next date after a yes, the client is not onboarded. That is a separate checklist.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the ghost

- Paste the original start date into the log the same minute you send the proposal. Without the date the day-3 line has nothing to hold.
- Keep the three lines in the same note as the Monday reset. One open, both available.
- Cap at three emails. A fourth becomes a novel and they mute you.
- If day 3 falls on a Friday, send Thursday instead. Do not slide it to Monday and lose the window.
- After any yes, add the client to next Monday’s “Still open” so the day-7 onboarding touch does not live only in your head.
- Log the reply time and the next action in the same block. Blank next action means the thread dies again.

Fill-in after any reply:

```
Reply received: ________ (date/time)
Decision: yes / later / price / no
Next action: invoice sent / parked until ________ / closed
Onboarding note started: yes / no
```

## Why this works when “just checking in” fails

“Just checking in” asks them to do work (remember the proposal, decide, reply with context). These lines ask for one word. The date makes the decision time-bound. You stop rebuilding the follow-up from memory every time.
