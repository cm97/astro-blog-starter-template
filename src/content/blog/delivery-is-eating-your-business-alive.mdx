You closed the deal. The invoice is paid. The work starts.

Then the rest of the business quietly stops.

No follow-ups on the open proposals. No weekly priorities. No intake for the next lead. No check-in with the client who finished last month. You are heads-down in delivery and the pipeline is already drying up.

This is the trap. Delivery feels productive. Running the business feels optional. Until the current projects end and there is nothing left behind them.

## The real cost in one week

Every week spent only on delivery is a week the next revenue is not being created. The discovery calls that never got booked. The proposals that sat unanswered. The past clients who never heard from you again.

You are not "too busy to sell." You are running a business with no operating system for the non-delivery work. So when the calendar fills with client work, everything else vanishes.

Most freelancers treat planning, follow-up, and onboarding as things they will "get to later." Later never comes. The system has to be short enough and automatic enough that it still happens on the weeks delivery is heavy.

## Three things that cannot wait for a light week

**1. Follow-ups that run without motivation**  
Day 3, day 7, day 14. Same sequence every time. The deal does not die because you were busy delivering for someone else.

**2. Onboarding that does not restart from zero**  
Every new client hits the same sequence: intake, kickoff, welcome, first milestone. No blank page. No "I think I did this last time."

**3. A weekly reset that survives Thursday**  
Three priorities. Mid-week check. Friday close. Twenty minutes Monday. The plan does not die just because a client asked for revisions.

These are not productivity hacks. They are the minimum viable operating system for a solo business that still needs to generate the next client while serving the current ones.

## Exact follow-up lines you send while delivery is on fire

Copy the log the minute a proposal goes out. Blank fields mean the follow-up does not exist.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________
Start date offered: ________
Day 3 due: ________
Day 7 due: ________
Day 14 due: ________
Status: waiting / replied / closed
```

Block 8 minutes on the calendar for each due date. Label them. If the date lands on Friday, move it to Thursday morning.

**Day 3 (before 11am, mid-week)**  
> Still want me to hold the [start date], or should I release it?

Subject: Re: [Project name] proposal

**Day 7 (same window)**  
> Quick one: do you want the [deliverable] locked for [date], or should I drop the hold?

**Day 14**  
> Closing the loop on the [project] proposal. If the timing is off, reply "later" and I'll note it. Otherwise I'll release the date.

Subject: Closing the file on [Project name]

Cap at three. A fourth becomes a novel. Mark closed and move on.

## What you do the same hour they say yes

- Invoice or deposit request goes out immediately.
- Welcome note: start date, the one thing you need from them, next check-in date.
- Add them to Monday's "Still open" list so onboarding does not live only in your head.

If you cannot name the next date after a yes, you do not have a running client. You have a paid task sitting in memory. That is the onboarding checklist, not another email.

## The 20-minute Monday reset that still runs when delivery is heavy

Do this before email. Same window every week.

1. Clear anything under 2 minutes.
2. Write three priorities. Not ten. If a fourth appears, one of the three leaves.
3. List who is waiting on you and who you are waiting on (open proposals past day 7).
4. Schedule the day-3, day-7, and day-14 notes that are due. If it is not on a calendar block, it is not a follow-up.
5. Write tomorrow's first move in one line.

Fill-in for quiet leads:

```
Name: ________
Last touch: ________
Next touch: day 3 / 7 / 14 on ________
Close date if no reply: ________
```

If this list lives only in your head, the next client stays imaginary while you deliver for the current ones.

## Why templates alone still fail

You have the words. They live in a random folder or an old note. When Monday is already full of client work, the sequence never starts. The Day 3 never gets sent. The Day 7 becomes a guilt novel on Day 11. The pipeline is a pile of open maybes.

The words have to sit next to the weekly reset and the onboarding checklist so a slammed week still has the sequence.
