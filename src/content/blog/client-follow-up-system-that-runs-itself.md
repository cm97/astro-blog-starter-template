You know the follow-up should go out. The proposal went quiet. The new client has not sent the intake. The past client has not heard from you in three months.

It does not go out because the week got loud. Delivery ate the morning. By Thursday the list lives only in your head, and heads drop things.

The fix is not more willpower. It is a system that still runs when you are slammed.

## The 20-minute weekly follow-up scan

Do this every Monday (or the first 20 minutes of your week). Same order every time. Timer on. Stop at 20.

1. Open the list of open proposals older than 5 days. Send the day-5/7 template if none exists.
2. Check new clients from the last 7 days. Confirm intake arrived. If not, send the intake nudge.
3. Scan active clients. Anyone quiet more than 7 days gets the mid-project check-in.
4. One past client from last quarter gets the re-engagement note.
5. Close the loop on anything older than 21 days with the break-up email.

That is the entire process. Five actions. Twenty minutes. The emails are already written.

Fill this log before you start so the scan has a record:

```
Date: ________
Proposals >5 days, no reply: ________ ________ ________
Intakes missing: ________
Quiet mid-project (>7 days): ________ ________
Past client re-engage: ________
Break-ups sent: ________
Next scan date: ________
```

If any field is blank after the 20 minutes, the scan was incomplete. Do not open new work until the five actions are done.

## Exact emails that do the work

Copy these. Fill the blanks. Send. Do not rewrite.

**Day 5–7 proposal follow-up**
Subject: Re: [Project] proposal

Hi [Name],

Following up on the proposal I sent [day] for [project].

Is this still something you want to move forward with, or should I take it off my plate?

Happy to adjust scope or timeline if anything changed.

[Your name]

**Intake missing after deposit**
Subject: Quick note before we start

Hi [Name],

Got the deposit. Ready to start on [date].

The intake form is the one thing I need from you so week one stays on track. Link is below. Takes about 8 minutes.

[link]

[Your name]

**Quiet mid-project**
Subject: Quick check-in — [project]

Hi [Name],

Quick check-in on [project]. We are at [stage] and on track from my side.

Anything you want to flag before [next date]?

[Your name]

**Re-engagement for past client**
Subject: Checking in after [project]

Hi [Name],

It has been a while since we wrapped [project]. Curious if anything new came up that matches what we did before.

Happy to hop on a short call this week if useful.

[Your name]

**Break-up / close the loop (day 21+)**
Subject: Closing the loop on [project]

Hi [Name],

I have not heard back on the [proposal / next step] from [date]. I am closing this out on my side so the calendar stays clear.

If timing changes later, reply and I will reopen it.

[Your name]

## Timing rules that keep replies coming

- Send all follow-ups before 11am, Tuesday–Thursday only.
- Never Friday. The thread dies over the weekend.
- After you send, write the next check date in the log the same minute.
- Cap the scan at three names per category. More than that is noise; the rest wait until next Monday.
- If a reply lands mid-week, update the log in 60 seconds. Do not rewrite the email.

## Phone-only version (15 minutes from your phone)

When you are not at a desk. Same five actions. Smaller screen.

1. Open Notes. Paste the log template above. Fill the blanks from memory or last week's note.
2. Open email or the app you use. Copy one of the exact emails. Fill the name. Send to the first three names that match.
3. Mark the log with the time sent.
4. Set a phone reminder for next Monday titled "Follow-up scan — 20 min".
5. Close the apps. Do not check replies for 30 minutes.

Exact phone log after sends:

```
Phone scan: ________
Sent: ________ to ________ at ________
Next reminder set: yes
```

## Extra exact lines that stop the stall

- Proposal still quiet after the day-5: "Closing the loop Friday unless I hear otherwise. Want me to hold the [date] start?"
- Intake still missing on day 3: "Still need the intake so week one stays on track. Link again: [link]. Deadline is [date]."
- Mid-project quiet more than 10 days: "Flagging [project] is quiet. Next piece is due [date]. Anything blocked on your side?"
- Past client re-engage when they said "later": "You mentioned [thing] last time. Still on the radar, or should I close the note?"
- Break-up when they asked for a call that never happened: "Still want the short call, or should I close this so the calendar stays clear?"
