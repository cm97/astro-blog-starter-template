They paid. Onboarding ran. First piece delivered. Then the thread goes quiet. No complaint. No second invoice. Just silence. That is the retention leak most solo operators never notice until the next client is also gone.

These two short check-ins close it. Same words every time. No call required.

## The 10-minute retention window

Run this the day the calendar hits Day 14 and again on Day 30. Stop at ten minutes even if you have more clients. The emails that leave the same day are the ones that still get replies.

1. **Open the private note (2 min).** Confirm the original scope line and the next unpaid deliverable or the project end date.
2. **Day-14 email (3 min).** One subject, four lines. Status + one question.
3. **Log the reply date (1 min).** Same note. Next touch is Day 30 or the date they name.
4. **Day-30 email (3 min).** Same shape. Ask for the next step or the referral in one sentence.
5. **Close the tab (1 min).** If no reply by the end of the next business day, mark status quiet and move on. Do not invent a third touch.

Fill this before you open the email draft:

```
Client: ________
Paid on: ________
Original scope (one line): ________
Last deliverable sent: ________
Next unpaid item or end date: ________
Day-14 sent: ________ (time)
Day-30 sent: ________ (time)
Reply logged: yes / no
```

## Exact Day-30 check-in (paste)

Subject: [Project] — still on track?

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thirty days in. [One sentence of what shipped]. Original scope was [one line].
>
> Want me to lock the next piece at [price / date], or is this a good point to wrap and send the final files?
>
> [Your name]

If the project is already finished, swap the second line for:

> Want a short referral note I can send to anyone who asks how we worked together, or should I close the file?

## What you do when they reply

- **Still on track / yes:** reply with the next date and the invoice link the same hour. Log the new due date.
- **Shifted / pause:** one line: “Understood. Holding until [specific day]. Reply then and I either restart or close.” Do not offer a free extension.
- **Quiet:** wait one business day. If still silent, mark quiet and stop. The next touch is the monthly reset, not a new novel.
- **Scope ask inside the reply:** answer with the two-option line from the scope post. Do not start the extra work in this thread.

## Extra lines that stop the silence

- Send both emails from the same address that handled onboarding. A new thread from a different account gets ignored.
- Put the Day-14 and Day-30 holds on the calendar the same day the first payment clears. Future-you will not remember the dates.
- Keep the original scope sentence in the private note. If you cannot paste it in one line, do not send the check-in yet.
- If they paid by invoice, start the Day-14 clock the day the payment notification arrives, not the day the bank cleared.
- One question only. A second question turns the email into a form they never finish.
- If the next unpaid item is already late, name the new date in the same email. Do not ask them to suggest one.
- Log the send time in the note before you close the tab. The weekly reset finds the date or the client disappears.
- Cap the whole sequence at two touches. A third email after silence is how you train them to ignore you.
