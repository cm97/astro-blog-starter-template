"Remote" doesn't mean "anywhere."

Many remote jobs only hire people in one country, or in a few time zones. You can write a great application and still get turned down because of where you live.

Here is how to find the jobs that are open to you, without reading every listing to the bottom.

## Read the location line first

Every good listing says where the company can hire. Look for it before you read anything else.

- **"Worldwide" or "Anywhere"** means they can hire you wherever you live.
- **A country name** ("USA", "UK", "Canada") usually means you need the right to work there. Their payroll, tax and benefits are set up for that country.
- **A region or time zone** ("Europe", "Americas", "UTC-5 to UTC+1") means they want overlap with a team's working hours.

If the line says "USA only," believe it. Applying anyway rarely works.

## Roles that hire worldwide most often

Some kinds of work travel across borders easily. Look first in:

- **Software development**, especially open-source-heavy teams.
- **Customer support**, where companies want people awake when customers are.
- **Design and writing**, where the work is reviewed, not supervised.
- **Marketing and sales roles at small companies**, which often hire contractors.

Contract and freelance roles hire across borders more often than full-time ones. The company pays an invoice, not a salary.
