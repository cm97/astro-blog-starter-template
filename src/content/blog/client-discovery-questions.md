You quoted a price. They went quiet. You cut the number. They still went quiet. The price was not the problem. You pitched a deliverable before you knew what a win looked like for them.

That is how solo operators lose a week. The call feels good. The proposal is written from memory. Then Thursday hits and you are rebuilding the intake from scratch for the next person. Ask the questions first. Write the quote from the answers. If they cannot answer, do not send a proposal.

Run this in one sitting. Twenty minutes. Phone notes are enough. Do not add a CRM for this.

## Ask these before you name a price

Send them in the email that books the call, or ask them live. Same order every time.

1. **What has to be true in 30 days for this to be a win?** Not "what do you want built." The outcome. If they name a tool instead of a result, ask again.
2. **What did you already try that failed?** This is the landmine list. It also tells you if they have paid someone and felt burned.
3. **Who else has to say yes?** If a partner, boss, or spouse decides, you are not selling to the person on the call. Ask for that name before you write anything.
4. **What is the budget range?** Say "range." Do not say "how much are you willing to spend." If they will not give a range, you do not have a buyer yet.
5. **What happens if this is not done in 30 days?** If the answer is "nothing, really," stop. That is a tire-kicker.
6. **What does done look like in 90 days?** One sentence you can paste into the proposal.
7. **What is the real deadline?** "Someday" is not a date. "Before the launch on the 18th" is.
8. **What would make you walk away?** Get the deal-breaker now, not after you have started.

If question 1 has no answer, end the call. Say you will wait until they know the win. Do not discount your way into a vague job.

## Words you can paste

Booking email, after they ask for a quote:

> Before I send a number, four answers so the quote matches the job: what has to be true in 30 days, what you already tried, who else signs off, and the budget range. If any of those are still open, say so and I will wait.

On the call, when they jump to price:

> I can price it once I know the win. What has to be true in 30 days, and what happens if it slips?

When the budget is a shrug:

> I work in ranges. If this is under $500, it is a checklist, not a build. If it is $2k to $5k, I will scope the full onboarding. Which band are we in?

When they go quiet after the proposal, day 6, one note only:

> Floating the proposal back up. If the timing moved, tell me and I will check back then. If something in the scope is wrong, I would rather fix that than guess. A one-line reply is enough.

Then stop. A third chase costs more than the job.
