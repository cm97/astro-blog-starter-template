You quote a price. They ghost. You lower it. They still ghost. The problem was never the price — it was that you never asked what they actually needed.

Most freelancers pitch from memory: "I do websites, I do branding, I do whatever you need." That's not a pitch. That's a shrug with a dollar sign on it.

Here's the fix. Before you quote anything, ask these five questions. They take two minutes. They save you from building the wrong thing for the wrong person.

## The five questions

1. **What's the one thing that has to be true for this project to be a win?**
   Not "what do you want." What has to be true. Forces them to name the outcome, not the deliverable.

2. **What have you already tried that didn't work?**
   Tells you where the landmines are. Also tells you if they've burned money on the wrong solution before — which means they'll be skeptical of yours.

3. **Who else is involved in the decision?**
   If it's not just them, you're not selling to them. You're selling to a committee. Adjust your pitch accordingly.

4. **What's your budget range for this?**
   Ask it straight. "What's your budget range?" Not "how much are you willing to spend." Range implies flexibility. The second phrasing implies a ceiling.

5. **What happens if this doesn't get done in the next 30 days?**
   This is the pain question. If the answer is "nothing, really," you don't have a client. You have a tire-kicker.

## Why this matters

These questions do two things. They filter out people who aren't serious, and they give you the ammunition to write a proposal that actually lands.
