They opened the proposal. You saw the open notification or the link click. Then nothing. No reply. No questions. Just silence.

That open is not a yes. It is an open file with a timestamp. Leaving it sitting trains you to check the inbox every hour instead of moving the other work. The 48-hour window is when a short nudge still lands as helpful instead of chasing.

## The 3-minute log before you send the nudge

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________
Opened: ________ (date/time)
Start date offered: ________
Price: $________
48-hour nudge due: ________
Status after send: still open / replied / closed
```

If you do not have the open time, do not invent it. Use the send time plus 48 hours as the due.

## Exact 48-hour nudge — send today

Subject: Quick check on [Project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Saw you opened the proposal for [one-line scope] at [price].
>
> One line is enough: want me to hold the [start date], or should I release it?
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No new pitch. No “let me know if you have questions.” The binary keeps the calendar clean.

Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Saw you opened the [project] proposal. Hold the [start date] or release it? One line is enough.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status as nudged in the log.
2. Set the next check for day 7 if no reply.
3. Do not open the thread again until the next scheduled touch.
4. If they reply yes, send the deposit link the same hour and open the onboarding checklist.
5. If they say still thinking, reply once with a hard date: “Understood. Reply by [specific day] to keep the hold. Otherwise I release the date.”

## Extra concrete steps that stop the opened-then-quiet file from living in your head

- Write the open time into the log the hour you see it. Guessing later turns the nudge into a soft check-in.
- Cap open proposals at five. Anything past the 48-hour nudge without a reply moves to the day-7 list or gets closed.
- Copy the 48-hour nudge into the same note as the day-3, day-7, and day-10 templates. One open, all available.
- After you send, set a no-check rule until the next date. Looking early turns the nudge into another wait.
- Never discount in the 48-hour nudge. The original price stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every opened-but-quiet proposal older than 7 days out of the active list. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.
- Put a 3-minute calendar block titled “48h nudge: [Name]” the night before the due. The block is the trigger.
- After the send, screenshot or copy the sent time into the log. “I think I sent it” is not a closed loop.
- If three 48-hour nudges land the same morning, send all three before 11am. Do not spread them.
- Keep a “opened, no reply” note separate from the active list. One weekly scan of that note is enough.
- When a reply arrives, update the start date or close the file the same hour. Do not let a yes sit overnight.
- If they ask for a call after the nudge, answer: “Happy to talk. First step is a short written yes on the original scope and price so we both know what we are discussing.”
- At Friday close, count how many 48-hour nudges you sent that week. Write the number on the Monday still-open line.
- Pre-write the subject and body in the calendar note the day the proposal first goes out. The 3-minute block only needs a paste and send.
- Never send the nudge from your phone while standing in a line. Sit, open the log, fill the three-minute block, then send.
- After any paid yes that arrives post-nudge, open the onboarding checklist the same hour. The follow-up sequence ends when money lands.
- Cap the reopen window at seven days after the nudge. After that a reply starts a fresh proposal, not a continuation.

## Exact replies for the answers you actually get

**Still thinking / need more time:**
> Understood. Reply by [next Friday] to keep the [start date] hold. Otherwise I release it.

**Can we hop on a call?**
> Happy to. First send a one-line yes on the original scope and price so we know what the call is about. Then pick a time.

**Price is high / can you do less?**
> Scope and start date stay the same. Price is [original]. Reply yes if you want a fresh proposal with a new date.

**I need to check with [partner/boss]:**
> Got it. Reply by [next Friday] with yes or a new date so the calendar stays clean.

**They pay:**
Open the onboarding checklist the same hour. Welcome, intake, kickoff hold. Do not celebrate first.
