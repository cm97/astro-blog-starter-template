You sent the proposal. Day 3 bump went out. Still nothing. Now it is day 7. The start date you named is still on the calendar. Every morning you check the thread and rewrite a softer version in your head. Silence at day 7 is not a maybe. It is a file that needs one exact next message so the date stays real or gets released.

Leaving it open without a send trains you to wait. The day-7 nudge names the next step. Clients who want the work reply. The rest free the hours.

## The 3-minute log before you send the day-7 nudge

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent: ________
Day 3 sent: ________
Start date offered: ________
Price: $________
Day 7 due: ________
Status after send: replied / still quiet / closed
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The nudge only works when the dates are already written.

## Exact day-7 nudge email — send today

Subject: Still holding [start date] for [Project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Checking in on the proposal from [date] for [one-line scope] at [price].
>
> I am holding the [start date] through end of day Friday. After that the slot releases.
>
> One line is enough if you want it or want it released.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No “let me know if you have questions.” The hold date is the next step. The release line closes the open loop without a hard close yet.

Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Checking in on [project] proposal. Holding [start date] through Friday. One line if you want it or want it released.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status “day 7 sent” in the log.
2. Keep the calendar hold for now. Do not delete it yet.
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Day 7 sent [Name].”
4. Do not check the thread for 48 hours. If they reply, treat it as a yes or a new date and update the log.
5. If they pay, send the deposit confirmation and open the onboarding checklist the same hour.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet file from living in your head

- Cap open proposals at five. Anything past day 7 without a reply gets this nudge or gets parked. A sixth means you are rewriting the week.
- Copy the day-7 nudge into the same note as the day-3 and day-10 templates. One open, all three available.

- If they reply “still thinking,” send one line: “Understood. The [start date] releases Friday. Reply by then if you want it held.” Do not leave it open past the named day.
- Never discount in the nudge. The original price stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every proposal older than day 10 without a reply out of the active list. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.
- Write the exact start date you offered into the email before you send. Guessing it from memory creates a soft nudge that does not free the calendar.

- After the send, screenshot or copy the sent time into the log. “I think I sent it” is not a closed loop.
- If three day-7 nudges land the same morning, send all three before 11am. Do not spread them. One sitting, three files moved.
- Keep a “parked past day 7” note separate from the active list. One weekly scan of that note is enough.
- When a reply arrives, update the start date or close the file the same hour. Do not let a yes sit overnight.
- If they ask for a call, answer: “Happy to talk. First step is a short written yes on the original scope and price so we both know what we are discussing.” Do not jump on a call with an open maybe.
- At Friday close, count how many day-7 nudges you sent that week. Write the number on the Monday still-open line. Zero open ghosts is the goal.
- Pre-write the day-7 subject and body in the calendar note the day the proposal first goes out. The 4-minute block only needs a paste and send.
- Never send the nudge from your phone while standing in a line. Sit, open the log, fill the three-minute block, then send. The log is the gate.

- Keep the five most common objection replies in the same note so you never search under pressure after a late reply.
