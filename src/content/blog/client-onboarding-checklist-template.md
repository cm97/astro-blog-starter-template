A new client signs. You feel good. Then thirty minutes later you're staring at a blank email wondering exactly what you're supposed to send first.

This happens because most coaches and consultants have an onboarding process that lives in their head, not in a document. You've done it a dozen times. You know the steps. But knowing the steps and having a checklist that runs itself are completely different things.

This is that checklist. Copy it, fill in the variable fields for your business, and the next time a client signs, you run the list instead of rebuilding it.

## What a client onboarding checklist actually needs to contain

Most onboarding checklists fail because they stop at the obvious steps — "send welcome email" — without telling you what the welcome email actually says, when to send the intake form, or how to handle the 48-hour window before a kickoff call.

A checklist that works has three layers:

**The trigger.** What starts the onboarding? For most service businesses it's: contract signed + deposit received. That combination is the green light. Everything before it is pre-sales; everything after it is onboarding.

**The steps.** In order, with time estimates. Not "send documents" but "send welcome email (10 min) → send intake form link (2 min) → set 48-hour follow-up reminder if intake not returned (1 min)."

**The variables.** Every template has three to five blank fields: client name, service type, start date, key contact, billing schedule. Fill those in once per client and the rest of the checklist runs on its own.

## The client onboarding checklist template

### Within 1 hour of signing

- [ ] Send welcome email (template below — fill in: name, service, what happens next)
- [ ] Create shared folder or project space (name format: `YYYY-MM ClientLastName`)
- [ ] Send intake form link with 3-day deadline
- [ ] Set internal reminder: intake form follow-up in 48 hours if not returned
- [ ] Create internal client note (fields: name, email, phone, service, start date, billing)

### Before the kickoff call

- [ ] Review completed intake form — note two or three things to confirm on the call
- [ ] Prepare kickoff agenda (see template below)
- [ ] Confirm the call time via calendar invite — include a one-paragraph agenda in the invite description
- [ ] Set up any tools you'll share access to (task manager, shared doc, project board)

### During or immediately after kickoff

- [ ] Send kickoff summary (what you covered, next steps, who owns what, next check-in date)
- [ ] Add client to any recurring communication (weekly update, report, etc.)
- [ ] Set internal milestone reminders for the first 30 days

### Two weeks in

- [ ] Send a brief check-in: "How are things feeling so far? Anything you need from me?"
- [ ] Review progress against what you agreed on the kickoff call
- [ ] Note any scope questions that have come up — address them explicitly rather than letting them drift

## The welcome email template

The welcome email does four things: confirms the relationship, sets tone, tells them what happens next, and gives them a point of contact if anything comes up. It does not need to be long.

> **Subject:** Welcome — here's what happens next
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Really glad to be working together on [service/project].
>
> Here's what the next few days look like:
>
> **Today or tomorrow:** You'll receive a short intake form. It takes about five minutes and helps me make sure our kickoff call is useful rather than just introductory. Please return it by [date, 3 days from now].
>
> **[Kickoff call date/time]:** We'll spend [30/60] minutes covering [main agenda items]. I'll send a calendar invite with the details.
>
> If anything comes up before then, reply here or reach me at [phone/email].
>
> Looking forward to it.
>
> [Your name]

That's it. No long preamble, no list of everything you're excited about. Just what they need to know right now.

## The intake form template

Five questions. No more. The intake form is not a discovery call — it's pre-work so the discovery call isn't wasted on basics.

1. What's the most important thing you want to accomplish in the first 30 days?
2. What have you already tried that didn't work?
3. Who else is involved in decisions about this?
4. What would make you feel like this was a great investment six months from now?
5. Is there anything else I should know before we talk?

Send it as a simple form (Google Forms, Typeform, whatever you already use) with a clear deadline. If it's not returned within 48 hours, follow up once by email: "Just checking in on the intake form — I need it before our kickoff so we can use the time well. Here's the link again: [link]."

## The kickoff agenda template

A kickoff call without an agenda turns into a 45-minute conversation where nothing is decided. A kickoff with an agenda takes 30 minutes and leaves with clear next steps.

**Kickoff agenda (30-minute call)**

1. **Confirm the goal** (5 min) — What does success look like at the end of this engagement?
2. **Review the intake form** (10 min) — Go through their answers, ask clarifying questions, surface anything that changes the plan
3. **Set the rhythm** (5 min) — How often will you communicate? What's the best channel? Who's the main point of contact on their side?
4. **First 30 days** (5 min) — What are the three things that need to happen in the first month?
5. **Questions** (5 min) — What do they need from you right now?

Send a one-paragraph summary within an hour of the call: what you agreed on, who owns what, and what the next check-in looks like.

## Why most onboarding breaks down

The checklist above is not complicated. Most coaches and consultants could write a version of it themselves in an afternoon.

The reason it doesn't exist in most businesses is the same reason any process document doesn't exist: it feels like administrative work that competes with client work, and client work always wins.

The result is a process that exists only in memory — which means it only runs well when you're not under pressure. The weeks when you're busy are exactly the weeks when the onboarding falls apart.

A document doesn't make you more capable. It makes you less dependent on being at your best every time a new client starts. The checklist runs the same whether you have one client or five, whether it's a slow week or a slammed one.

## The internal client note

One more piece that most onboarding checklists skip: a single internal document per client that captures everything you'll need in a moment of pressure.

Not a CRM profile. Just a note with five fields:

- Full name + preferred name
- Email + best phone
- Service / engagement type
- Start date + end date (if applicable)
- Billing: amount, schedule, method

That's it. When you need to reference something about this client in six months, that note is the place you look. If you have fifteen clients, you have fifteen notes. They live in the same folder. You can find any client in 10 seconds.
