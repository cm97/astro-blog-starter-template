They said yes on the call. You sent the invoice. Then the deposit sits unpaid while the start date you named keeps getting closer.

You do not want to chase money. You also do not want to start work with nothing in the bank. The gap lives in your head until Thursday and the slot is gone.

These three lines close it without a novel. Run them on a fixed cadence so the unpaid invoice does not turn into free work or a dead deal.

## The 3-minute log the minute they say yes

Copy this before you leave the call or close the proposal tab.

```
Name: ________
Yes date: ________
Invoice sent: ________ (date / time)
Deposit amount: $________
Start date named: ________
Day 2 due: ________
Day 5 due: ________
Day 8 due: ________
Status: waiting / paid / closed
Channel: email / other
Last touch sent: ________
Next action: ________
```

Three minutes. Do it while the yes is still fresh. Memory of “they said they would send it” is not a date.

Put three 6-minute blocks on the calendar labeled “Deposit: [name] day 2/5/8”. If the date lands on Friday, move it to Thursday morning. Do not skip.

## Day 2 — Confirm the invoice landed

Send on day 2, mid-week morning before 11am. Not Friday. Not after 3pm.

> Invoice for the [project] deposit is in your inbox. Still good to lock the [start date], or should I release it?

One sentence. The date creates the deadline. The “or release it” gives them an easy reply instead of silence.

Subject: Re: [Project name] deposit

After send: update log with time sent. Status stays waiting. Do not check for 24 hours.

## Day 5 — Binary hold or release

Still unpaid:

> Quick one: do you want me to hold the [start date] for the [project], or release it so I can book someone else?

Still one line. Still a binary. People answer yes/no faster than they explain why the transfer is late.

Same window: before 11am, mid-week. Same subject: Re: [Project name] deposit

After send: log the time. If they say the payment is processing, reply with the exact date you will check again and restate the hold. Do not start work.

## Day 8 — Close the loop

Still quiet or unpaid:

> Closing the loop on the [project] deposit. If the timing is off, no problem — reply “later” and I’ll note it. Otherwise I release the date today.

Subject: Closing the file on [Project name]

This either gets the money, a real date, or a clean no so the slot stops living in your head.

After send: set status to closed or parked. Delete the remaining calendar blocks. Do not send a fourth email. Do not start the work.

## Exact subject lines that do not look like collection spam

- Day 2: Re: [Project name] deposit
- Day 5: Re: [Project name] deposit
- Day 8: Closing the file on [Project name]

Keep the Re: even if it is a new thread. It reads as continuation of the yes.

## Exact timing windows that raise reply rate

- Day 2: Tuesday or Wednesday morning, before 11am.
- Day 5: same, mid-week only.
- Day 8: any weekday before 11am.
- Never Friday. Never after 3pm. The reply rate drops and the thread dies over the weekend.
- If day 2 falls on a Friday, send Thursday instead. Do not slide it to Monday and lose the window.
- If you miss the window, send the next morning before 11am. Do not wait for a “better” day.

## What you do when they reply

- Paid: send the welcome note with the start date and the one thing you need from them the same hour. Mark the log closed. Add them to next Monday’s onboarding row. Do not start delivery until the money is in.
- Later / next week: write the exact date they named. One note that day. Status = parked. Do not hold the original start date.
- Payment processing: ask for the expected clearance date. Restate: “I’ll hold the [date] until [clearance day]. Reply by then or I release it.” Log it.
- Price or scope change: new invoice, new date. The original yes stays the record. Restart the three-touch clock only after the new invoice is sent.
- No / never mind: mark closed. Do not argue. Do not send a fourth email.

If you cannot name the next date after a paid yes, the client is not onboarded. That is a separate checklist.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the unpaid yes from dying

1. Paste the original start date into the log the same minute they say yes. Without the date the day-2 line has nothing to hold.
2. Keep the three lines in the same note as the Monday reset. One open, both available.
3. Cap at three emails. A fourth becomes collection noise and they mute you.
4. After any paid yes, open the onboarding checklist the same day. The deposit sequence ends when money lands; the onboarding sequence starts.
5. On Day 5, if they say it is processing, answer in one paragraph with the clearance date and restate the hold. Do not open a new scope talk.
6. After Day 8, do not send a fourth email because you felt guilty on a quiet Friday.
7. At the next weekly reset, add a row: "Open deposits past Day 5." If none, write none.
8. Screenshot or copy the invoice send time into the log. Memory of “I sent it last week” is not a date.
9. If the start date you named has already passed by Day 5, replace it with the next available start you can actually hold before you send the line.
10. Set a phone reminder for the 6-minute block the night before. The calendar alone dies when the week is on fire.
11. If they reply with a question instead of yes/no, answer in one short paragraph, then restate the binary close in the same email. Do not leave the decision open-ended.
12. Write the next touch date on the log the second the current email leaves. Blank next-touch is how the cadence dies.
13. Cap open deposit rows at three. Older ones past Day 8 are closed or parked. More than three is noise, not a pipeline.
14. Never start the work on a “they said they paid” without the money in the account. The sequence exists so you do not have to decide that on the spot.

```
Touch sent: Day 2 / 5 / 8 — time: ________
Reply received: ________ (date/time) or none
Decision: paid / later / processing / no / silent
Next action: welcome note / park until ________ / closed / answer + restate binary
Onboarding started: yes / no
```

## Why these work when “just checking on the invoice” fails

“Just checking in on the invoice” asks them to remember, decide, and explain. These ask for one word. The date makes the decision time-bound. You stop rebuilding the chase from memory every time a yes goes quiet on the money.
