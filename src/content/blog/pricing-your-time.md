Pricing is where most solo operators either undercharge out of fear or overcharge out of spite. Neither works long-term.

## The three numbers

1. **Your floor:** what you need to cover costs + a living wage. Below this, you're donating time.
2. **Your market rate:** what similar work sells for. Research it. Don't guess.
3. **Your value rate:** what the outcome is worth to the client. This is where the real money is.

## The method

- List every task you do for a typical client.
- Time each one honestly (not optimistically).
- Multiply by your hourly rate.
- Add a buffer for the stuff you always forget (revisions, emails, the "quick question" that takes an hour).
- Round up to a clean number.

## The trap

Most people price the task. Price the outcome instead. "I'll set up your onboarding system" is a task. "You'll never lose a client to a slow follow-up again" is an outcome. Outcomes cost more.
