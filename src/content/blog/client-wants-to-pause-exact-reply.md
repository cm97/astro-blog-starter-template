The client emails on Thursday: “Can we pause this for a few weeks? Things got busy on our side.”

You either say “sure, let me know when you are ready” and lose them, or you push and sound desperate. Both leave the project living only in your head. Next month you are starting from zero while the paid work sits unfinished.

The pause is not the problem. The open-ended reply is. Lock a restart date, a single next step, and a calendar hold so the file does not vanish.

## The 8-step pause sequence (15 minutes total)

1. **Reply the same day (2 minutes).** Do not wait for a quiet moment. Same thread. Short. Silence reads as agreement to an open-ended hold.
2. **Name the pause window and the restart date (1 minute).** “Pause until [date]” not “until you are ready.” You pick the date. They only correct it.
3. **Confirm what is already done and what stays open (1 minute).** One sentence each. They need to see the work is real, not vapor.
4. **Set the single next action and who owns it (1 minute).** Usually a short check-in email from you on the restart date. Not “let me know.”
5. **Log the hold next to the weekly reset (2 minutes).** Name, pause date, restart date, open item, dollar amount still open. If it is not written, it is gone by next Thursday.
6. **Put the check-in on the calendar (1 minute).** Same slot as the Monday reset. Title: [Client] pause check. Recurring until it closes.
7. **Send the confirmation (2 minutes).** Exact lines below. No sales pitch. No new scope.
8. **Close the tab (1 minute).** Do not keep the draft open. The list owns it now.

That is the whole move. Copy the lines. Reuse them every time the same email arrives.

Fill this before you hit send:

```
Client: ________
Pause requested: ________ (date)
Restart date offered: ________
Work already delivered: ________
Open item: ________
Paid amount still open: ________
Next action owner: you / them
Check-in date: ________
```

## Exact pause reply

> **Subject:** Re: [project name] — pause until [date]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Understood. We will pause until [specific date, 2–4 weeks out]. Everything delivered so far stays as-is: [one-line list]. The open item is [exact next step].
>
> I will email you on [restart date] to confirm we pick it back up or extend the pause. Reply “extend” by then if the date needs to move.
>
> [Your name]

Send it the same day. Do not add a sales line. Do not invent new scope.

## If they name no date

Offer one:

> Got it. I can hold the current state until [date two weeks from today]. I will check in that morning. Reply before then if you need a different window.

You pick the date. They only correct it.

## If they want an open-ended pause

Hold firm on a date:

> I can pause until [date]. After that the current deliverables stay as delivered and we close the open item or book a short restart call. Reply if that window works.

No third option of “whenever.” Open-ended is how the paid work dies in a folder.

## Restart check email (day of the date)

> Hi [Name],
>
> Checking in on the pause we set for [project]. Ready to pick up [open item] this week, or do you need to extend to [new date]?
>
> Reply with the date that works. If I do not hear back by [two days later] I will mark the open item closed and we can restart fresh when you are ready.

Send it even if you already know the answer. The calendar owns the send, not your memory.
