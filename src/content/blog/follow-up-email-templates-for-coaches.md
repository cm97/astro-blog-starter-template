You finish a discovery call with a coaching prospect. They say they will review the proposal and get back to you. Three days later the inbox is still empty.

Or a current client goes quiet mid-engagement. You know you should check in, but the words do not come easily. The fear of sounding pushy keeps the email unwritten.

Coaches live on relationships. Silence costs revenue. These five follow-up email templates are written for the specific moments coaches face: after a proposal, during a quiet engagement, for past clients, and at project close. Each one is short, specific, and asks one clear question so the reply is easy.

## Why coach follow-ups fail

Most follow-ups die for three reasons.

1. They are too vague. "Just checking in" gives the reader nothing to answer.
2. They are too long. A novel in the inbox gets archived.
3. They have no next step. The reader has to invent what to do.

The templates below fix all three. Copy, fill the blanks, send.

## Template 1: Proposal follow-up (day 5–7)

Use this when a prospect received your coaching proposal and has not replied.

**Subject:** Re: [Program or package name] proposal

> Hi [Name],
>
> Following up on the proposal I sent [day] for [specific outcome or program].
>
> Is this still the right fit for you right now, or has timing shifted?
>
> Happy to adjust the length or focus if something has changed on your end.
>
> [Your name]

Why it works: It names the exact thing you sent. It asks a binary question. It offers flexibility without discounting. Send it Tuesday or Wednesday morning.

## Template 2: Quiet client mid-engagement

Use this when a current coaching client has not replied in 7+ days and the next session or deliverable is approaching.

**Subject:** Quick check — [client name or program]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Checking in on [current focus or milestone]. On my side we are still on track for [next date or deliverable].
>
> Anything you want to flag before our next session, or anything you need from me this week?
>
> [Your name]

Why it works: It leads with status, not a demand. The questions can be answered in one sentence. It keeps the relationship warm without adding pressure.

## Template 3: Re-engagement for a past client

Use this for a client you worked with months ago who has gone quiet. One per week maximum.

**Subject:** Checking back in — [their name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> We worked together on [specific result or program] back in [month]. Things moved quickly on my end so I did not circle back the way I intended.
>
> Is [the goal you discussed] still something you are working on, or has it sorted itself out?
>
> Either way, no pressure.
>
> [Your name]

Why it works: It references real history. It takes ownership of the silence. It gives a clear out so the reply feels safe.

## Template 4: Referral ask after a successful engagement

Send this two to four weeks after a coaching engagement ends and the client has seen results.

**Subject:** One quick ask

> Hi [Name],
>
> Glad we wrapped [specific outcome]. Nice to see [concrete result they got].
>
> One quick ask: do you know anyone else dealing with [the same problem]? A name and a short intro email is enough.
>
> No worries if no one comes to mind.
>
> [Your name]

Why it works: It leads with the result they experienced. The ask is minimal. The out is genuine.

## Template 5: Project or package wrap-up

Send the day the engagement ends.

**Subject:** [Program name] — wrapped

> Hi [Name],
>
> [Program or package] is complete on my side.
>
> [One or two sentences stating the specific result: "You now have the 90-day plan and the weekly check-in rhythm in place."]
>
> Final notes and any remaining files are attached. Let me know if anything needs adjusting.
>
> If you want to continue or start the next piece, reply with a date and I will hold the spot.
>
> [Your name]

Why it works: It confirms the work is done. It restates the outcome in concrete terms. It opens the door for the next engagement without a hard sell.

## How to run these without the process dying

The templates only work if they get sent. Put the send dates on your weekly reset. One proposal follow-up and one quiet-client check per week is enough. Do not batch five at once.

Log each send the minute it leaves your outbox:

```
Name: ________
What you sent: proposal / check-in / re-engage
Sent date: ________
Next touch due: ________
Status: waiting / replied / closed
```

When a reply arrives, act the same day. Yes means send the next step or invoice. Not now means note the month they named. No means close the file.
