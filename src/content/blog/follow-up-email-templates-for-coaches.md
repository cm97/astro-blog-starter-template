A discovery call ends. The prospect says they'll think about it. You wait three days, then two weeks. The follow-up never leaves your drafts because the words feel awkward and the week already filled up.

Coaches lose more potential clients to silence than to a hard no. The same happens mid-package when a client goes quiet between sessions. The fix is not better motivation. It is five short emails you already have written, so the send still happens when the calendar is full.

These templates are for coaches and solo service providers. They stay under six lines. They ask one clear question. They give an easy out so the other person can reply without writing a novel.

## Why coaches skip the follow-up

Most coaches already know they should follow up. The block is the blank page plus the fear of looking pushy. When the process lives only in your head, a slammed week deletes the follow-up. A dated note on the calendar plus a ready email removes the decision.

Log these five fields the minute a proposal or session ends. If any line is blank, the follow-up does not exist yet.

```
Name: ________
What you sent or discussed: ________
Date sent: ________
One thing they said they wanted: ________
Next follow-up date: ________
```

Time box: three minutes. Do it before you close the tab.

## Template 1: Proposal or discovery call went quiet (day 5–7)

Send on a Tuesday or Wednesday morning. Subject stays boring so it does not look like a newsletter.

**Subject:** Re: [package or program name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Following up on the proposal I sent [day] for [specific outcome or package].
>
> Is this still something you want to move forward with, or should I take it off my plate?
>
> Happy to adjust timing if anything has changed.
>
> [Your name]

One question. No new pitch. If you named a start date, add one line: "I am holding [date] until [close date]."

## Template 2: Post-session check-in (48 hours)

Use after a paid session when you want the action items to stick without turning into a second coaching call by email.

**Subject:** Quick check on [one action item]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Quick check on the [specific action] we set on [day]. How did it go?
>
> Reply with one line — even "still working on it" is useful. Next session stays [date].
>
> [Your name]

Send it before problems appear. A check-in at 48 hours is maintenance. The same email sent after silence is damage control.

## Template 3: Client quiet mid-package (day 7)

When a client has not replied after a session or a shared resource and the next session is more than a week away.

**Subject:** Checking in before [next session date]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Just checking in before our [date] session. Anything you want to flag, or are we still on track for [one goal]?
>
> Reply if something shifted. Otherwise I'll see you then.
>
> [Your name]

## Template 4: Package or retainer renewal (2 weeks before end)

Send while the current package is still active. Waiting until the last session makes the ask feel like a surprise.

**Subject:** What comes after [current package]

> Hi [Name],
>
> We're two weeks from the end of [package]. If you want to continue with [next option], reply and I'll send the invoice.
>
> If timing is not right, no problem — just say so and I'll close the file.
>
> [Your name]

## Template 5: Past client check-in (90 days)

Use for someone whose package ended and you have not heard from them. One touch, not a drip.

**Subject:** Checking back in — [their name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> We wrapped [package] back in [month]. Is [the problem you worked on] still something you're working on, or has it sorted itself out?
>
> Either way, no pressure.
>
> [Your name]

## The 20-minute Monday scan that keeps them going out

Run this every Monday before your first client session:

1. Open the log. Send any note that is due today.
2. Cap it at two sends. More than that becomes a blast.
3. Write the next date for each open thread before you close the note.
4. If a proposal has been quiet for 14 days, send Template 1 and set a close date.
5. Stop at 20 minutes. Unsent notes move to next Monday, not into guilt.

If the due date lands on a Friday or Monday, move it to Tuesday morning. Do not skip it because the week started busy.
