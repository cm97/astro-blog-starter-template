You sent the invoice with a clear due date. The next morning the bank is still empty and the thread is quiet. You open the email app, rewrite a softer version in your head, and close it again. That first quiet day is where most late invoices start living in your head instead of getting a next step.

The day-1 reminder is not a chase. It is the message that keeps the original due date real. Clients who intend to pay reply or pay. The rest get a clean next date instead of an open maybe.

## The 3-minute log before you send the day-1 reminder

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Invoice sent: ________
Amount: $________
Original due: ________
Day 1 due: ________
Status after send: paid / replied / still quiet
Next action if quiet: day 7 nudge
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The reminder only works when the amount and dates are already written.

## Exact day-1 invoice reminder email — send today

Subject: Invoice [number] for [Project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Quick check on invoice [number] from [date] for [one-line scope] at [amount]. Original due date was yesterday.
>
> Still holding the original amount through end of day Friday. One line is enough if it is paid or if there is a blocker.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No “let me know if you have questions.” The hold date is the next step. Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Quick check on invoice [number] for [amount]. Holding original through Friday. One line if paid or if there is a blocker.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status “day 1 invoice sent” in the log.
2. Keep the amount on the open list.
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Day 1 invoice sent [Name].”
4. Do not check the thread for 48 hours. If they reply or pay, update the log the same hour.
5. If they pay, mark paid and send the receipt confirmation the same hour.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet invoice from living in your head

- Cap open invoices at five. Anything past day 1 without a payment or reply gets this reminder or gets parked. A sixth means you are rewriting the week.
- Copy the day-1 reminder into the same note as the day-7 and day-14 templates. One open, all three available.
- After you send, set a 48-hour no-check rule. Looking early turns the reminder into another wait.
- If they reply “still processing,” send one line: “Understood. Original amount holds through Friday. Reply by then with confirmation or the blocker.” Do not leave it open past the named day.
- Never discount in the reminder. The original amount stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every invoice older than day 14 without payment out of the active list. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.
- Write the exact amount and original due date into the email before you send. Guessing it from memory creates a soft reminder that does not free the space.
- Put a 4-minute calendar block titled “Day 1 invoice: [Name]” the night before. The block is the trigger. Without it the email never leaves.
- After the send, screenshot or copy the sent time into the log. “I think I sent it” is not a closed loop.
- If three day-1 reminders land the same morning, send all three before 11am. Do not spread them. One sitting, three files moved.
- Keep a “parked past day 1” note separate from the active list. One weekly scan of that note is enough.
- When a payment arrives, update the log the same hour. Do not let a paid invoice sit overnight.
- If they ask for a call, answer: “Happy to talk. First step is confirmation the original invoice is paid or the exact blocker in writing so we both know what we are discussing.” Do not jump on a call with an open maybe.
- At Friday close, count how many day-1 reminders you sent that week. Write the number on the Monday still-open line. Zero open ghosts is the goal.
- Pre-write the day-1 subject and body in the calendar note the day the invoice first goes out. The 4-minute block only needs a paste and send.
- Never send the reminder from your phone while standing in a line. Sit, open the log, fill the three-minute block, then send. The log is the gate.
- After any payment, mark it paid the same hour. The follow-up sequence ends when money lands.
- Cap the hold window at the named Friday. After that the original due date is no longer the only record without a new note.
- If the original due date has already passed by more than 7 days, move straight to the day-7 nudge.
- Keep the five most common payment-blocker replies in the same note so you never search under pressure after a late reply.

## Exact replies for the day-1 invoice replies you actually get

When they finally answer, do not invent a new email. Paste one of these.

**Still processing / need more time:**
> Understood. Original amount holds through end of day Friday. Reply by then with confirmation or the blocker. Otherwise the file moves to the next step.

**Can we hop on a call?**
> Happy to. First send a one-line confirmation the invoice is paid or the exact blocker in writing so we know what the call is about. Then pick a time.

**Can you do less / payment plan:**
> Amount and original invoice stay the same. Reply with yes on the full amount or the exact blocker. No partials in this message.

**I need to check with [partner/boss/accounting]:**
> Got it. The hold stays through Friday. Reply by then with confirmation or release so the open list stays clean.

**Send more info:**
> The invoice is the info. One line is enough: paid or the blocker?

**They pay:**
Mark paid the same hour. Send the receipt confirmation. Do not celebrate first.

## Why this works when “I’ll check the payment later” fails

“I’ll check later” keeps the file open and the cash blocked in your head. The day-1 reminder names the end of the hold. You stop carrying the maybe into Monday. The clients who intend to pay reply or pay. The ones who do not free the space.
