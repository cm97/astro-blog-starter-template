The project went well. The client said thank you. You meant to ask for a testimonial, then the next job started, and now it's been two months and asking feels strange.

Most testimonials are lost in that gap, not in the asking. Clients who were happy are usually willing. They just need a short, easy request at the right moment.

## When to ask

Ask within a week of the final delivery, while the result is still fresh for them. The best moment is right after they say something kind. If a client replies "this is perfect, thank you," that is your opening. Reply to that same email.

If you missed that window, the 30-day check-in is your second chance. Ask how the work is holding up, then ask for the testimonial in the same note.

## The exact email

Subject: A quick favour about [project name]

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm glad [deliverable] is working for you. Would you be up for writing two or three sentences about working together? I'd like to share it on my site so other [type of client] know what to expect.
>
> If it helps, these three questions make it quick:
>
> 1. What was going on before we started?
> 2. What did we do together?
> 3. What's different now?
>
> A few lines is plenty. If you'd rather not, no problem at all.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

Why it works: it names a small, specific ask ("two or three sentences"), it explains where the words will go, and it gives them a structure. The three questions turn "great to work with!" into a short before-and-after story, which is the kind a future client actually reads.

## Make it easy to say yes

- **Offer to draft it.** Some clients would rather approve than write. Add: "Or, if it's easier, I can draft something from our emails and you can edit it."
- **Ask permission to use their name.** Get a clear yes for their name, role and company before you publish. Never put words in a client's mouth or post a quote they didn't approve.
- **Send one reminder, then stop.** If there's no reply in a week, send one line: "No pressure on the testimonial. If you have two minutes this week, I'd still love it." Then let it go.
