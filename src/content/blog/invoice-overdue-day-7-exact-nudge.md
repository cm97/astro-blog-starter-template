You sent the invoice. Day 1 reminder went out. Still nothing. Now it is day 7 past due. The amount is sitting in your head every morning while you open the bank app and rewrite a softer version. Silence on an overdue invoice is not a maybe. It is cash that needs one exact next message so the due date stays real or the file gets closed.

Leaving it open without a send trains you to wait. The day-7 nudge names the next step. Clients who intend to pay reply or pay. The rest free the mental space.

## The 3-minute log before you send the day-7 invoice nudge

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Invoice sent: ________
Amount: $________
Original due: ________
Day 1 reminder sent: ________
Day 7 due: ________
Status after send: paid / replied / still quiet / closed
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The nudge only works when the amount and dates are already written.

## Exact day-7 invoice nudge email — send today

Subject: Invoice [number] for [Project name] still open

> Hi [Name],
>
> Checking in on invoice [number] from [date] for [one-line scope] at [amount]. Original due date was [due date].
>
> Still holding the original amount through end of day Friday. After that the file moves to the next step.
>
> One line is enough if it is paid or if there is a blocker.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No “let me know if you have questions.” The hold date is the next step. The next-step line closes the open loop without a hard close yet.

Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Checking in on invoice [number] for [amount]. Holding original through Friday. One line if paid or if there is a blocker.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status “day 7 invoice sent” in the log.
2. Keep the amount on the open list for now. Do not delete it yet.
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Day 7 invoice sent [Name].”
4. Do not check the thread for 48 hours. If they reply or pay, update the log the same hour.
5. If they pay, mark paid and send the receipt confirmation the same hour.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the quiet invoice from living in your head

- Cap open invoices at five. Anything past day 7 without a payment or reply gets this nudge or gets parked. A sixth means you are rewriting the week.
- Copy the day-7 invoice nudge into the same note as the day-1 and day-14 templates. One open, all three available.
- After you send, set a 48-hour no-check rule. Looking early turns the nudge into another wait.
- If they reply “still processing,” send one line: “Understood. Original amount holds through Friday. Reply by then with confirmation or the blocker.” Do not leave it open past the named day.
- Never discount in the nudge. The original amount stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every invoice older than day 14 without payment out of the active list. The list is for cash still open, not for ghosts.
- Write the exact amount and original due date into the email before you send. Guessing it from memory creates a soft nudge that does not free the space.
- Put a 4-minute calendar block titled “Day 7 invoice: [Name]” the night before. The block is the trigger. Without it the email never leaves.
- After the send, screenshot or copy the sent time into the log. “I think I sent it” is not a closed loop.
- If three day-7 invoice nudges land the same morning, send all three before 11am. Do not spread them. One sitting, three files moved.
- Keep a “parked past day 7” note separate from the active list. One weekly scan of that note is enough.
- When a payment arrives, update the log the same hour. Do not let a paid invoice sit overnight.
- If they ask for a call, answer: “Happy to talk. First step is confirmation the original invoice is paid or the exact blocker in writing so we both know what we are discussing.” Do not jump on a call with an open maybe.
- At Friday close, count how many day-7 invoice nudges you sent that week. Write the number on the Monday still-open line. Zero open ghosts is the goal.
- Pre-write the day-7 subject and body in the calendar note the day the invoice first goes out. The 4-minute block only needs a paste and send.
- Never send the nudge from your phone while standing in a line. Sit, open the log, fill the three-minute block, then send. The log is the gate.
- After any payment, mark it paid the same hour. The follow-up sequence ends when money lands.
- Cap the hold window at the named Friday. After that the original due date is no longer the only record without a new note.
- If the original due date has already passed by more than 14 days, move straight to the day-14 close.
- Keep the five most common payment-blocker replies in the same note so you never search under pressure after a late reply.

## Exact replies for the day-7 invoice replies you actually get

When they finally answer, do not invent a new email. Paste one of these.

**Still processing / need more time:**
> Understood. Original amount holds through end of day Friday. Reply by then with confirmation or the blocker. Otherwise the file moves to the next step.

**Can we hop on a call?**
> Happy to. First send a one-line confirmation the invoice is paid or the exact blocker in writing so we know what the call is about. Then pick a time.

**Can you do less / payment plan:**
> Amount and original invoice stay the same. Reply with yes on the full amount or the exact blocker. No partials in this message.

**I need to check with [partner/boss/accounting]:**
> Got it. The hold stays through Friday. Reply by then with confirmation or release so the open list stays clean.

**Send more info:**
> The invoice is the info. One line is enough: paid or the blocker?

**They pay:**
Mark paid the same hour. Send the receipt confirmation. Do not celebrate first.
