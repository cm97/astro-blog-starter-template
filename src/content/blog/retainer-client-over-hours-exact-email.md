The retainer says 10 hours a month. It's the 20th and you're already at 12. The client just sent three more "quick" requests.

You don't want to sound petty about two hours. So you keep going. By the end of the month you've worked 16 hours for the price of 10, and next month starts the same way.

A retainer only works if both sides can see the hours. Most overruns aren't the client taking advantage. They just don't know where the hours went, because nobody told them.

The fix is two short emails: one before you hit the limit, and one with options when you do.

## First: know where you are

You can't flag an overrun you haven't noticed. Keep one line per client, updated at the end of each work session:

```
Client: ________
Retainer: ___ hours / month, $____
Hours used so far this month: ___
Date I'll hit 80%: ________
What's still in the queue: ________
```

A spreadsheet or a note is fine. What matters is that you update it the same day, not at month end.

## Email 1: the heads-up at 80%

Send this when you've used about 80% of the month's hours. Not when you're already over.

> Subject: [Month] retainer: 8 of 10 hours used
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Quick heads-up so nothing surprises you. We've used 8 of this month's 10 hours, mostly on [main work].
>
> Still in the queue: [request 1], [request 2], [request 3]. That's about [X] hours.
>
> Which ones matter most this month? I'll do those first and move the rest to [next month].
>
> [Your name]

This email does most of the work. The client sees the hours before they're gone, and picks what matters. Most clients reply with a priority list, and the overrun never happens.

## Email 2: when the month is full

If the hours run out and requests keep coming, send this before you start the extra work:

> Subject: [Month] retainer hours are used up
>
> Hi [name],
>
> This month's 10 hours are now used. For [the new request], we can:
>
> 1. Start it on [first day of next month] as part of next month's hours, or
> 2. Do it now as extra hours at [rate] per hour (about [X] hours, so [total]), or
> 3. Move up to a bigger retainer, [hours] hours a month for [price], from next month.
>
> Which works best for you?
>
> [Your name]

Three options, all a yes. None of them is "no".

## Why this works

- **Numbers, not feelings.** "8 of 10 hours" is a fact. "You're asking for a lot" is a complaint.
- **The heads-up comes first.** Asking about money after the work is done feels like a surprise bill. Asking at 80% feels like good service.
- **The client chooses.** You're not deciding to work for free or refusing to help. They pick the option that fits their budget.
- **Option 3 is a real offer.** If they go over every month, the retainer is just too small. Saying so is helpful, not pushy.

## If they go over every month

Two months in a row over the limit means the retainer doesn't match the work. Don't keep absorbing it. Send this at the start of the next month:

> Hi [name],
>
> Looking back, the last two months came to about [hours] hours each, against a 10-hour retainer. To keep things simple and make sure your work always gets done, I'd suggest moving to [hours] hours a month at [price] from [date].
>
> If you'd rather keep 10 hours, we can agree on which kinds of requests come first, so the rest wait for the next month.
>
> [Your name]

To price the bigger retainer, multiply the hours by your hourly floor (the lowest rate that covers your costs and what you need to take home). The [pricing post](/blog/pricing-your-time-without-guessing) walks through working that out.

## If they say "I thought this was included"

> I get it. The retainer covers [hours] hours a month of [type of work]. This month we've used them on [list]. The new request is extra on top, so here are the options for it.

Then repeat the three options. Keep it short. Don't defend every hour.

## Put the rules in writing next time

Most retainer arguments come from things nobody wrote down. Add these lines to your retainer agreement:

> - Includes [hours] hours a month of [type of work].
> - Unused hours [do / don't] roll over to the next month.
> - I'll let you know when we reach 80% of the month's hours.
> - Extra hours are [rate] per hour, agreed before I start.

The "80%" line matters most. It tells the client to expect the heads-up, so when it arrives it feels like the process working, not a complaint.
