A kickoff call without an agenda becomes a friendly chat. Everyone leaves feeling good. Nobody leaves knowing who does what by when.

A kickoff call with an agenda takes 30 minutes. It ends with decisions on paper. The client sees how the work will run before the work starts.

This post gives you two agendas, the prep list, the questions worth asking, and the summary email to send afterwards.

## What a kickoff call is for

The kickoff call is not a sales call. The client already said yes. It is not a discovery call either. You already know roughly what they need.

A kickoff call has three jobs:

1. **Agree what "done" looks like.** In words you can both point back to later.
2. **Agree how you will work together.** Channel, rhythm, who decides.
3. **Agree the first steps.** With owners and dates.

If you leave with those three things written down, the call worked.

## Before the call: a 10-minute prep list

- [ ] Read the intake form answers. Note two or three things to confirm.
- [ ] Re-read the scope you agreed. Write it in one sentence.
- [ ] Draft the first three milestones with rough dates.
- [ ] Put the agenda in the calendar invite, so they see it before the call.
- [ ] Open a blank summary doc. You will fill it in live.

If the client has not sent the intake form, ask for the three most important answers by reply before the call. The [client intake form template](/blog/client-intake-form-template/) lists the questions worth asking.

## The 30-minute kickoff agenda

Use this for most coaching clients, small projects and solo consulting work.

1. **Welcome and goal (5 min).** "By the end of this, what would make you say it was worth it?" Write their answer down word for word.
2. **Intake review (10 min).** Go through their answers. Ask about anything vague. Flag anything that changes the plan.
3. **How we work (5 min).** Main channel. Reply times. How often you update them. Who on their side makes decisions.
4. **First 30 days (5 min).** The first three milestones and dates. Who owns each one.
5. **Questions and next step (5 min).** What do they need from you right now? Confirm the next check-in date before you hang up.

## The 60-minute kickoff agenda

Use this for bigger projects, teams with more than one decision-maker, or work longer than three months.

1. **Introductions and roles (5 min).** Who is on the call. Who signs off on work. Who just needs to be kept informed.
2. **Goal and success measures (10 min).** What done looks like. How you will both know it is working.
3. **Scope walkthrough (10 min).** What is included, in plain language. What is not included. How extra requests get quoted.
4. **Intake review and risks (10 min).** Their answers, plus anything that could slow the work down. Missing access, a busy season, a holiday.
5. **Working rhythm (10 min).** Channel, update schedule, meeting cadence, file sharing, approvals.
6. **Timeline and first milestones (10 min).** The first 30 days in detail. The rest in outline.
7. **Questions and confirm next step (5 min).** Read back the decisions. Book the next call.

## Questions worth asking on a kickoff call

You don't need all of these. Pick the ones that fit the client.

- What would make this a clear success six months from now?
- What has been tried before, and what got in the way?
- Who else needs to approve work before it is final?
- How do you prefer updates: email, a shared doc, a quick call?
- What is your busiest time of the month? When should I avoid sending things for review?
- Is there anything that would make you want to stop working together?

The last question feels awkward. Ask it anyway. The answer tells you what to protect.

## What to say about scope on the call

Say it once, out loud, early. Keep it friendly.

> "Just so we are on the same page: what we agreed covers [scope]. If something comes up outside that, I'll send you a quick quote before I start, so there are no surprises."

That one sentence makes every later conversation about extra work easier. The [scope creep script](/blog/scope-creep-script/) has the exact reply for when the first extra request arrives.

## The kickoff summary email

Send it within an hour of the call. It turns the conversation into a record you can both point to.

> **Subject:** Kickoff summary: [project or client name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for the call today. Here is what we agreed.
>
> **The goal:** [their words, from the start of the call]
>
> **How we will work:**
> - Updates every [weekday] by [channel]
> - Check-in calls [frequency]. Next one: [date]
> - Requests go to [email or tool]
>
> **First 30 days:**
> - [Milestone 1]: [owner], by [date]
> - [Milestone 2]: [owner], by [date]
> - [Milestone 3]: [owner], by [date]
>
> **What I need from you:** [anything outstanding, with a date]
>
> If I have missed anything, reply and I will update it.
>
> [Your name]

Keep the client's own words for the goal. When the project gets busy, that line reminds you both why it started.

## Common kickoff call mistakes

- **No agenda in the invite.** The client arrives unprepared and the call drifts.
- **Re-selling.** They already bought. Spend the time on how the work will run.
- **Leaving dates vague.** "Soon" and "next week or so" become missed deadlines.
- **No summary email.** If it is not written down, each side remembers a different call.
- **No next date booked.** The second call is easiest to book while you are both on the first.

## Where the kickoff call fits

The kickoff call is the second step of onboarding. The [client welcome email](/blog/client-welcome-email-template/) comes first and books it. After the call, the week-one check-ins in the [client onboarding checklist](/blog/client-onboarding-checklist-template/) keep the momentum going.

Save this agenda once. Paste it into every kickoff invite. The call runs the same way whether it is your first client this year or your fifth this month.
