They love the proposal. Then the message lands: “Can we do a payment plan?” or “Is it possible to split this over two months?”

You need the cash this month. They need the work. Saying flat no kills the deal. Saying yes to open-ended installments turns you into their bank and guarantees the second half arrives late or never.

You need one reply that keeps the deposit rule, gives them a real path, and still lets you start only when the money that protects the project is in.

## The 60-second cash check before you reply

Open the same note you use for open proposals. Add one line.

```
Name: ________
Full price: $________
Deposit rule: 50% up front / other: ________
Asked for: installments / pay later / split
Reply sent: ________
Decision: hold / deposit + final / two fixed payments
Next touch: ________
```

If you do not already have a written deposit rule, write one now. 50% before any work starts is the default that keeps most solo operators solvent.

## Exact reply that protects cash without killing the deal

Send this the same day. Subject: Re: [Project name] — payment options

> I can do two fixed payments: 50% ($X) before we start, and the remaining 50% ($Y) on [date or milestone]. Once the first payment clears I send the kickoff and we begin. Does that work, or do you want to stay with the original full-payment plan?

One paragraph. Binary. The date or milestone makes the second half concrete. “Once the first payment clears” is the gate. You do not start on a promise.

Do not offer three or four payments. Do not offer “pay when you can.” Do not apologize for the structure.

## What you do when they reply

- Yes to the two payments: send the invoice for the first 50% the same hour. Subject: Invoice — [Project] deposit. After it clears, run the normal onboarding sequence and put the second payment date in the Monday scan.
- Stay with full payment: reply “Understood. Full amount due before start. Invoice attached.” Same gate.
- Can you do smaller / three payments / after delivery: repeat the binary once. “I can hold the two fixed payments (50% now, 50% on [date]), or full payment up front. Which works for you?” Do not negotiate the percentage in the same thread.
- Silence after 48 hours: no second softener. Mark the proposal “payment structure pending.” Next touch is the normal day-7 or day-14 follow-up you already use for quiet proposals.

## Exact invoice and log fields

When they accept the two-payment version, the first invoice is three lines.

```
Project: ________
Payment 1 of 2 (start deposit): $________
Due: upon receipt / by ________
```

After it clears, log:

```
Deposit cleared: ________ (date)
Second payment due: ________
Kickoff sent: ________
```

Never start the work, send the welcome email, or open the shared folder until the first payment is in the account.

## Timing that keeps cash clean

- Reply the same day before 3pm. After that it looks like you are deciding whether you can afford to be flexible.
- Never offer a payment plan on Friday afternoon. The thread dies and Monday you have already mentally started the project on zero cash.
- Cap open “payment structure pending” rows at two. More than two means the deposit rule is not written down or you are chasing the wrong prospects.
- If they ask for a plan after the work has already started, the answer is no. Scope already delivered has a different conversation and a different invoice.

## Extra concrete steps that stop “we’ll pay the rest later” from becoming unpaid work

1. Write the deposit rule in the proposal itself before you send it. “50% due before work begins, balance on [milestone or date].”
2. Keep the payment-plan reply in the same note as the open-proposal list. One open, both available.
3. After any deposit clears, add one line to the next weekly reset: “Deposit in for [name]. Second payment due [date].”
4. If the second payment is late by 48 hours, send the exact past-due line you already use for invoices. Do not continue delivery past the missed date without a written extension.
5. Never start on a verbal “yes we’ll pay half now.” The invoice and the cleared payment are the record.
6. At wrap-up, the final invoice restates both payments and any change orders. No surprises.
7. If you cannot name a firm second-payment date in under 30 seconds, the request is bigger than a simple split. Offer the two fixed payments or full up front only.
8. Screenshot the original proposal payment terms into the project note the day the deposit clears. Memory is not a cash rule.

## Why this works when “sure, we can figure something out” fails

“Sure, we can figure something out” trains them that the price is flexible and trains you that cash arrives whenever it arrives. The binary two-payment reply makes the structure visible, time-bound, and gated on real money before you spend time. You stop rebuilding the cash rule from memory every time a prospect asks for flexibility.
