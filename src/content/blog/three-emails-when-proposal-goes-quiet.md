The proposal left. Then silence.

You check the thread. No reply. You rewrite the same email three times in your head and never send it. That is where the close dies — not in the proposal, in the quiet days after.

Send these three. Same words every time. No blank screen. Calendar them the day you hit send so Thursday cannot invent a new version.

## What you log the minute the proposal goes out

Open the note you already use for the week. Fill this block in under four minutes. Blank fields mean the follow-up does not exist yet.

```
Name: ________
Proposal sent date and time: ________
Channel: email
One-line scope: ________
Price: ________
Start date offered: ________
Day 3 due: ________
Day 7 due: ________
Day 14 due: ________
Status: waiting / replied / closed
Next action if no reply: send day 3 / park / close
```

Put three 8-minute blocks on the calendar labeled "Proposal: [name] day 3/7/14". If the date lands on Friday or Monday, move it to Tuesday morning. Do not skip.

## Day 3 — the light poke

Send on calendar day 3, before 11am. Not when you feel ready.

> Subject: Re: [proposal name]
>
> Quick check — did the proposal land, or should I resend the link?
>
> Happy to walk the one part that usually needs a decision: [scope / start date / price].
>
> One line is enough.

Fill-in before you send: "I am holding [start date] until [Day 14]." Add it only if you actually named a start date.

Do not add a paragraph about how busy you are. Do not attach a new PDF. Time box: four minutes including finding the thread.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Quick check — proposal land, or resend? Holding [start date] until [Day 14]. One line enough.

## Day 7 — the date hold

If Day 3 got nothing, send this. It creates a decision instead of another check-in.

> Subject: Re: [proposal name]
>
> Still want me to hold the [start date], or should I release the slot?
>
> Either answer is fine. I just need to know so I can book the next client.

Silence after this is data. Do not invent a new pitch. Do not cut the price.

Short phone version:

> Still holding [start date] or release the slot? Need to know to book the next one.

## Day 14 — the close-out

Send it even if you already decided they are gone. Closing the file in writing keeps you from reopening it out of guilt next month.

> Subject: Closing the file — [proposal name]
>
> I am closing this proposal on my side so it does not sit in limbo.
>
> If the timing changes, reply to this thread and I will reopen it. Otherwise no further notes from me.
>
> Thanks for looking.

Then mark the row closed. Remove it from this week's priorities. A closed no is usable. An open silence is not.

Phone version:

> Closing the file on [proposal]. Reply if timing changes, otherwise no further notes.

## What you do if they answer

- **Yes / start:** send the invoice or deposit request the same day. Welcome note with the start date, the one thing you need from them, and the day they will hear from you next.
- **Not now:** write the month they named on the log. One note that month. Not a weekly drip.
- **Price question:** restate what is in and what is out. Do not cut the number to get a reply.
- **Scope change:** new line, new price, new date. The original proposal stays the record.
- **No / close:** mark closed. Do not argue.

If you cannot name the next date after a yes, you do not have a running client yet. You have a payment waiting on a process that still lives in your head. That is the onboarding checklist, not another follow-up.

Reply log fill-in:

```
Reply received: ________ (date/time)
Decision: yes / later / price / scope / no
Next action: invoice sent / parked until ________ / new quote / closed
Onboarding note started: yes / no
```

## Extra concrete steps that stop the leak

1. Block the three send times the day the proposal leaves. Unscheduled follow-ups die.
2. Copy the log into the same note as your Monday reset. One place.
3. On Day 7, if they ask a question, answer in one paragraph and restate the close date. Do not open a new scope conversation.
4. After Day 14, do not send a fourth email because you felt guilty on a quiet Friday.
5. At the next weekly reset, add a row: "Open proposals past Day 7." If none, write none.
6. If Day 3 lands on a delivery day, send the poke before you open any other file. Four minutes max.
7. After any yes, add the client to next Monday's "Still open" so the onboarding day-2 or day-7 does not live only in your head.
8. Cap open proposal rows at five. Anything past Day 14 is closed or parked before you write the three weekly priorities.
9. Screenshot the log or copy it into the same note as the weekly reset the same hour you send the proposal. Blank next action means the thread dies again.
10. Never send on Friday or after 3pm. Move those windows to Tuesday or Wednesday morning.
11. If they reply with a price objection, restate the in-scope line and the price. Do not invent a discount in the reply.
12. Log the reply time and the next action in the same block. The system is the log plus the three lines, not a new draft.

## Timing windows that actually get replies

- Day 3: Tuesday or Wednesday morning, before 11am.
- Day 7: same, mid-week only.
- Day 14: any weekday before 11am.
- Never Friday. Never after 3pm. The reply rate drops and the thread dies over the weekend.

If a due date lands on Friday or Monday, move it to the nearest Tuesday or Wednesday morning. Do not skip the touch.

## Multi-proposal scan (when more than one is quiet)

Do this inside the Monday reset, not as a separate task.

1. Open the list of proposals sent in the last 14 days.
2. Write one line per quiet proposal: sent date + start date offered + cash amount.
3. Rank by cash at risk (largest remaining fee first).
4. Cap the day at two sends (day 3 or day 7). The rest wait until Wednesday.
5. Log every send on the same card so Thursday cannot invent a different version.

Fill-in for the scan:

```
Quiet proposals this Monday:
1) ________ — sent ________ — start ________ — $________ — send day 3/7 on ________
2) ________ — sent ________ — start ________ — $________ — send day 3/7 on ________
Cash-at-risk order: ________
Sends today (max 2): ________
```

If the scan takes more than four minutes you are rewriting history. Use the dates already on the calendar.
