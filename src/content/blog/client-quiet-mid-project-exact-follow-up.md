Work is underway. You sent a draft, asked for one asset, or need a yes on the next step. Then nothing. The project sits. You wait because another email feels pushy and you already delivered the last piece.

That silence is where retainers die and invoices stay unpaid. The next step lives in your head. When the week is slammed, the follow-up never leaves.

You do not need a longer message. You need two fixed lines and a date on the calendar.

## The 3-minute log the minute you notice the quiet

Copy this before you close the tab. Blank fields mean the follow-up does not exist yet.

```
Client: ________
Last thing you sent: ________ (date)
What you need from them: ________
Day 3 due: ________
Day 7 due: ________
Status: waiting / replied / paused
```

Three minutes. Do it the same day you notice. Thursday will erase it if you wait.

Put two 5-minute blocks on the calendar labeled “Mid-project: [name] day 3/7”. If the date lands on Friday, move it to Thursday morning. Do not skip.

## Day 3 — Restate the one thing you need

Send before 11am mid-week. Same thread. One sentence.

> Need [specific item or decision] to keep [next deliverable] on [date]. Reply with it or a new date and I will lock the rest.

Subject: Re: [project name]

Log the send. Move on. Do not add a paragraph about how busy you know they are.

## Day 7 — Binary choice

If day 3 got silence:

> Quick one: send [specific item] so I can finish [next deliverable], or should I pause the project until you are ready?

Still one line. Still a binary. People reply to yes/no faster than open-ended questions.

Same window: before 11am, mid-week. Same subject.

## Exact timing that raises replies

- Day 3: Tuesday or Wednesday morning, before 11am.
- Day 7: same, mid-week only.
- Never Friday. Never after 3pm. Replies drop and the thread dies over the weekend.
- If day 3 falls on a Friday, send Thursday instead.

## What you do when they reply

- They send the item: mark the log closed the same hour. Put the next deliverable date on the weekly reset card. Do not open a new scope conversation.
- They name a new date: write it on the same row. One note. Status = waiting on date.
- They ask a question: answer in one paragraph and restate the original ask and date. Do not cut scope.
- No reply after day 7: mark paused. One line on the next Monday reset: “Paused — waiting on [client].” Do not send a third email this week.

## Multi-client scan (when more than one is quiet)

Do this inside the same 20-minute Monday reset, not as a separate task.

1. Open the list of open projects.
2. Write one line per quiet client: last touch date + the exact item still needed.
3. Rank by cash at risk (unpaid invoice or largest remaining fee first).
4. Cap the day at two day-3 or day-7 sends. The rest wait until Wednesday or next Monday.
5. Log every send on the same card so Thursday cannot invent a different version.

Fill-in for the scan:

```
Quiet clients this Monday:
1) ________ — last touch ________ — need ________ — send day 3/7 on ________
2) ________ — last touch ________ — need ________ — send day 3/7 on ________
Cash-at-risk order: ________
Sends today (max 2): ________
```

If the scan takes more than four minutes you are rewriting history. Use the dates already on the calendar.

## Pause recovery (when they come back after day 7)

They reply weeks later. Do not restart the whole project from memory.

1. Open the original log row.
2. Restate the last agreed next deliverable and the date that was on the table.
3. Send one line:

> Good to hear from you. Last open item was [specific]. Still the right next piece, or has the priority shifted? Reply with a date and I will lock it.

4. Put the new date on the weekly reset card the same day.
5. If scope has drifted, do not absorb it in the reply. Park it: “Got the new ask. I will check what we locked and come back with two options.”

Recovery fill-in:

```
Client: ________
Original need: ________
New date or shift: ________
Scope still same? yes / park for quote
Next reset row: ________
```

## Extra steps that stop the stall

1. Paste the exact thing you need into the log the same minute you notice the quiet. Without the specific ask the day-3 line has nothing to hold.
2. Keep the two lines in the same note as the Monday reset and the weekly status email. One open, all available.
3. Cap at two emails. A third becomes a novel and they mute the thread.
4. After any reply, add the client to next Monday’s “Still open” so the next touch does not live only in your head.
5. On day 7, if they say pause, write the pause date. Do not keep working on speculation.
6. At the next weekly reset, scan for open mid-project rows older than 7 days. If none, write none.
7. Never send the day-3 or day-7 line from a blank screen. Copy the template, swap the brackets, send.

## Why these work when “just checking in” fails

“Just checking in” asks them to remember what you need, find the old thread, and write context. These name the exact item and give a binary. You stop rebuilding the follow-up from memory every time a client goes quiet mid-project.
