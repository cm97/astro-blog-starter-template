A client says they are in. Then nothing. You rewrite the same email three times, rebuild the intake from memory, and the week dies in your inbox.

These are the five templates people ask for after that happens. Copy the lines. Run them in order. If you want the files already filled, the Digital System is the $49 checkout.

## 1. Day-6 proposal follow-up

Send this on day 6. Not day 2. Not a new novel.

Subject: Still good for [service]?

Hi [name] — checking the proposal I sent [date]. If it is a yes, reply YES and I send the intake today. If timing moved, reply LATER and a date. If it is a no, reply NO and I close the file. Either way I stop guessing.

Time box: 4 minutes. One send. No second rewrite.

## 2. Quiet-lead poke

Use this when they opened the email and still did not answer.

Subject: Closing this Friday

Hi [name] — I am closing open proposals Friday at 4. If you still want [service] starting [date], reply YES before then. If not, no reply needed.

That line does the deciding for them.

## 3. Intake that starts the job

Do not start work on a verbal yes. Start on these five fields:

- Name and the person who approves
- Service they bought, in their words
- Start date and the first deliverable
- How they want updates (email, text, one weekly note)
- What is out of scope

If field 5 is blank, the job will sprawl. Ask it before kickoff.

## 4. Scope reply when they add work

Client: can you also do X?

You: X is outside the signed scope. I can add it as a separate line at [price] and start after you reply YES. The current job stays on the original date.

Do not say yes in the same breath you explain the price.

## 5. Monday reset so Thursday does not wipe the list

20 minutes. Before email.

1. Clear anything under 2 minutes.
2. Write three priorities. Not ten.
3. List who is waiting on you and who you are waiting on.
4. Schedule the day-6 follow-ups that are due.
5. Write tomorrow's first move in one line.

If this list lives only in your head, the ghosted client stays ghosted.
