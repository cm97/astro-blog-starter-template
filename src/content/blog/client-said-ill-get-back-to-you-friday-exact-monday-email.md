They ended the call with "I'll get back to you by Friday."

Friday came and went. No email. No text. You spent the weekend wondering if you should nudge or wait.

Now it is Monday. The longer you wait the more the deal feels dead. You need the exact short email that brings the decision back without sounding desperate or restarting the whole pitch.

## The 60-second log before you send

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Add one line.

```
Name: ________
Call date: ________
Promised reply: Friday
Days past promise: ________
Monday email sent: ________
Status: waiting / closed
Next check: ________
```

If you cannot name the call date, do not send yet. That date is the reason the email is fair.

## Exact Monday email that reopens the decision

Send this before noon on Monday. Subject: Quick check on [Project / Proposal name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> You mentioned you'd let me know by Friday on the [proposal / next step]. I haven't seen a note yet, so I'm checking in once.
>
> Happy to answer any last questions or hold the slot if you need more time. Otherwise I'll assume timing shifted and close it on my side.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No new pitch. No discount. No "just circling back." The Friday promise is the only fact you need.

Do not add a second paragraph. Do not attach the proposal again. Do not send it after 2pm.

## What you do when they reply (or don't)

- They reply and want to move forward: send the next concrete step the same day (invoice, kickoff date, or intake form). Log status as reopen. Do not restart the old silence clock.
- They reply with a no or "not this month": thank them in one line, mark closed, set a 60-day check only if it fits your business.
- Silence after this Monday email: wait 5 full days, then send the clean close from the second-follow-up post. Do not invent a third soft version.
- They reply weeks later: treat it as a fresh start. Do not reference the old Friday.

## Timing that keeps it professional

- Send only on the first Monday after the promised Friday.
- Weekday morning only. Never after 2pm or on Friday.
- Cap open "waiting on Friday reply" rows at two. More than two means the Monday scan is not running.
- If they were a past client, use the same words but change the subject to "Quick check on next steps."

## Extra concrete steps that stop the open-thread pile-up

1. Write the promised reply date in the log the moment the call ends. Without that date the Monday email has no anchor.
2. Keep this template in the same note as the Monday scan. One open, both available.
3. After you send the Monday email, move the row out of the "active call" list the same hour. The scan only shows live items.
4. If three of these land in the same week, look at the original call. Vague next steps create silence.
5. Never reopen a closed row without a new reply from them. Memory of "they seemed interested" is not a reason to email again.
6. At the weekly reset, count how many Friday-promise emails you sent. Zero is a signal you are still carrying dead threads.
7. Screenshot or paste the call date into the project note the day it happens. The Monday email only works when the timeline is visible.
8. If you cannot decide whether to wait until Tuesday, default to Monday morning. The exact day matters less than having one.

## Why this works when "I'll just wait" fails

Waiting trains you that silence after a Friday promise is normal and trains the client that your follow-ups can be ignored. The Monday email frees the mental slot for the next live lead and gives them a low-pressure way back in if they actually want it.
