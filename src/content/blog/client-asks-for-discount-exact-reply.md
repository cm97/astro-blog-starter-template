## Why cutting the price loses the job

You lower the number. They still go quiet. Or they say yes and then ask for three extras that were never in the original scope. The discount becomes the new floor and every future client hears about it.

The reply that keeps the client and the margin is two options only: take the price as written, or change the scope so the number fits. No third option of "I can do 15% off if you decide today."

## The 5-minute reply the minute they ask

Do these in order. Do not send a lower number in the first reply.

1. **Pause in one line.** Reply: "Got it. Let me confirm what is in the current scope so I can give you a clear answer." Time box: 1 minute. Do not negotiate in that first note.
2. **Open the original proposal.** Highlight the deliverables and the price. Write both down before you write the email. If the scope is fuzzy, that is the bug to fix next time.
3. **Name what is in and what is out.** One sentence each. Clients forget what they asked for three days ago.
4. **Send two options only.** Keep the price and the full scope, or reduce the scope so the number drops. No invented middle discount.
5. **Ask for a written choice.** "Reply with the option you want and I lock the date."
6. **Log it.** Same place you log proposals. Price asked, options sent, status.
7. **Set the day-2 bump.** If they go quiet, one line. Do not drop the price to restart the conversation.

Fill this before you send the reply:

```
Client: ________
Original price: ________
Original scope in one line: ________
What they asked: ________ (e.g. "20% off")
Option A: full scope at original price
Option B: reduced scope at lower price — new line: ________
Status: waiting / chose A / chose B / closed
```

## The script

When they ask for a discount:

> I can do the work at [original price] with the scope we already locked: [one-line scope]. If that number needs to come down, I can remove [specific deliverable] and the price becomes [lower number]. Which do you want?

No apology. No "I understand budgets are tight." One restatement, two options, one question.

If they push with "everyone else is cheaper":

> Other quotes may cover different work. This one covers [scope] for [price]. I can keep that, or we cut [item] and drop to [new price]. Which is the better fit?

If they name a specific number they want:

> That number works if we drop [deliverable]. Scope becomes [shorter line] for [their number]. Original full scope stays [original price]. Reply with the one you want and I send the updated note.

If they go quiet after the two options:

> Checking which option you want for [project]. Full scope at [price], or reduced scope at [lower]. Reply and I hold the [start date], or I release it.

## Exact timing that protects the margin

- Send the two-option reply the same day they ask. Not after you sleep on it.
- Do not drop the number in the first reply even if the ask feels small.
- Bump at 48 hours if no choice. One line. Then close the file if still quiet by day 5.
- Never invent a "decide today" discount. It trains the next ask.
