You are ready to build. You just need their logo files. Or the homepage copy. Or feedback on round one. Or a login to their hosting.

They said "this week" two weeks ago. Your calendar still has their launch date on it. When it slips, they will remember the date you gave them, not the files they never sent.

This is not a ghosting problem. They still reply to other things. The project is stalled on **them**, and nobody has said so out loud.

The fix is one email that does three jobs. It names the exact missing item. It moves the deadline in writing. It gives them a choice they can answer in one line.

## Before you write: the 2-minute stall check

Fill this in first. If you can't fill a line, that is the reason the project is stuck.

```
Client: ________
Exact item(s) I'm waiting on: ________
Date I first asked: ________
Original delivery date: ________
Work days lost so far: ________
What I can still do without it: ________
```

"Feedback" is not an item. "Yes or no on the homepage layout, plus changes to the menu" is an item. Vague requests get vague silence.

## The reply: name it, move the date, offer a choice

Send this in the same thread where you first asked. Mid-week, before 11am.

> Subject: Re: [project name] — waiting on [item] to keep going
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Quick status. I'm ready for the next step, but I'm still waiting on:
>
> - [item 1, e.g. final logo files (SVG or PNG)]
> - [item 2, e.g. your notes on the round 1 draft]
>
> Since these were due on [date], the [deliverable] date moves from [old date] to [new date]. Every extra day I wait moves it one more day.
>
> Two ways to keep it moving:
>
> 1. Send the items by [day], and I'll hold [new date].
> 2. If they'll take longer, I can [carry on with placeholders / finish the parts that don't need them] and swap them in later.
>
> Which works better for you?
>
> [Your name]

That's it. No apology. No "I know you're busy." No long recap of the project.

## Why this version works

- **The list is the ask.** They don't have to dig through old threads to work out what you need. A bulleted item is easier to forward to the person on their team who has it.
- **The date moves in writing.** You are not punishing them. You are writing down what is already true. If launch slips later, this email is the record.
- **"Day for day" is fair and simple.** One extra day of waiting means one extra day on delivery. Most clients accept that without arguing.
- **The choice gives them an easy yes.** "Use placeholders" is often the answer they wanted. They just didn't know it was allowed.

## When the stall is feedback on a draft

Feedback stalls are different. The client has the work. They are stuck on what to say, or waiting for someone else to look.

Make it smaller. Ask for a decision, not an essay.

> Hi [name],
>
> To keep [project] on track, I just need one of these by [day]:
>
> - "Looks good, go ahead", or
> - Your top 3 changes, in any format (a quick list is fine).
>
> If I don't hear back by [day], I'll [move ahead with the current version / hold the next step] and we can adjust in the next round.

Only use "move ahead with the current version" if your contract or proposal allows it. If it doesn't, use "hold the next step" instead. Never treat silence as approval unless they agreed to that in writing.

## When they reply

- **They send everything.** Confirm the new date in one line. Put it on your calendar the same hour.
- **They send some of it.** Thank them, and list only what's still missing. Same date rule.
- **They pick placeholders.** Write down which pieces are placeholders. Add "swap in real [item]" as its own task, so it doesn't vanish at launch.
- **They ask to push it back.** Say yes, and agree a restart date. The project is paused, not dead. Write the restart date somewhere you'll see it on that Monday.

## When they still don't reply

Wait one week. Then send one line in the same thread:

> Hi [name], should I pause [project] until you have [item]? Happy to pick it back up whenever you're ready.

That's the last chase. Mark the project paused, write the date, and stop working on it. Don't keep building on guesses. That's how a stalled project turns into free rework.

## Stop the next stall before it starts

Most stalls are set up at kickoff. Two small changes help:

1. **Put a "what I need from you" list in your onboarding email.** Every file, login and decision, each with a date. Our [client intake form template](/blog/client-intake-form-template) has a starting list.
2. **Add one line to your proposal:** "Delivery dates assume materials and feedback arrive by the dates listed. Delays on either side move the timeline by the same number of days." Then the reply above is just you following what you both agreed.
