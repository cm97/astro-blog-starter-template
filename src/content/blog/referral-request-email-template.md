Your happiest clients would refer you. Most of them never do. Not because they don't want to. Because nobody asked them, at the right time, in a way that made it easy.

Asking for a referral feels awkward. So it gets put off. Then the project ends, the client moves on, and the moment passes.

This post fixes that. When to ask, three emails to copy, the one follow-up to send, and what to do when someone actually refers you.

## Why clients don't refer you on their own

Clients are busy. Recommending you is a favor. It sits at the bottom of their list unless three things are true.

1. **They know you want referrals.** Many assume you are already full.
2. **They know who to refer.** "Anyone you know" gives them nothing to picture.
3. **It takes them one minute.** A forwardable line beats "let me think about it".

A good referral request email does all three.

## When to ask for a referral

Timing matters more than wording. Ask when the client has just felt the value of your work.

- **Right after a clear win.** A milestone hit, a problem solved, a thank-you email from them.
- **Two to four weeks after a project ends.** They have seen results. They still remember working with you.
- **At a regular review with ongoing clients.** Once things are going well, not in month one.

Don't ask in the first week. Don't ask in the same email as an invoice. Don't ask when something has just gone wrong.

## Template 1: After a clear win

Short, warm, specific. Send it the same day they say thanks.

> **Subject:** Glad that worked
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Really glad [the specific result] landed well.
>
> Quick question. Do you know anyone else who is dealing with [the problem you solved]? I have room for [one or two] new clients next month and would love to help someone you trust.
>
> If someone comes to mind, feel free to forward this email or share my details: [email or booking link].
>
> No pressure at all. Thanks again for a great [project / few months].
>
> [Your name]

## Template 2: After the project ends

Send this two to four weeks after the final delivery.

> **Subject:** Checking in, and a small ask
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> It has been a few weeks since we wrapped up [project]. How is [the result] holding up?
>
> I'm taking on a small number of new [clients/projects] this quarter. The people I help best are [one-line description, for example: solo consultants who need their onboarding sorted before a busy season].
>
> If anyone like that comes to mind, I would really appreciate an introduction. Here is a line you can forward:
>
> *"[Your name] helped me with [result]. Worth a chat if you are dealing with [problem]: [link]."*
>
> Either way, I hope [their next goal] goes well.
>
> [Your name]

The forwardable line does the work. Your client doesn't have to write anything. They copy, paste and send.

## Template 3: For ongoing or retainer clients

Use this after a good review call, not out of the blue.

> **Subject:** Thank you, and one question
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for the time on today's call. It's been good to see [what has improved] over the last [few months].
>
> One question. Is there anyone in your network who might need the same kind of help? I would look after them the same way I look after you.
>
> If so, an intro by email is perfect. Just copy me in.
>
> [Your name]

## The one follow-up

If they don't reply, send one short follow-up a week later. Then stop.

> Hi [Name], just bumping this in case it got buried. No worries at all if nobody comes to mind. Thanks again for [project].

One follow-up is polite. Two starts to feel like pressure. The relationship matters more than the referral.

## How to thank someone who refers you

When a client sends someone your way, thank them that day. Whether or not the referral turns into work.

> Hi [Name], [Referred person] just got in touch and mentioned you. Thank you. That means a lot. I'll take good care of them.

If the referral becomes a client, tell your original client. People like knowing their recommendation worked out.

## What to avoid

- **Asking everyone at once.** A bulk email to your whole client list feels like a campaign. Ask one person at a time.
- **Being vague about who you help.** "Anyone who needs help" gives them nobody to picture.
- **Making it a condition.** Never tie a referral to a discount they didn't ask for, or to finishing the work.
- **Asking after a problem.** Fix the problem first. Ask later, if at all.

## Make it a habit, not a one-off

Referral requests fail when they depend on remembering. Add one line to your project wrap-up routine: "Day 14 after delivery: referral email." The [client retention checklist](/blog/retention-checklist/) has a day-60 referral step for ongoing clients.

The referral request is one of five follow-ups worth keeping ready. The others cover a proposal that went quiet, a mid-project check-in, a lead that went silent, and the project wrap-up. All five are in [follow-up email after no response](/blog/follow-up-email-after-no-response/).

Write your version of these emails once. The next time a client says thanks, you reply within the hour.
