You open the laptop already late. By 9:15 the inbox has eaten the morning. By noon you have done three hours of work that does not invoice.

That is not a discipline problem. The day has no spine. Everything is one pile. Time blocking only works if the blocks protect cash, not if they decorate the calendar.

Use three blocks. Same windows. Same order. If a day blows up, keep the names and shrink the minutes. Do not invent a fourth block.

## Block 1 — Paid work (90 minutes, before inbox)

This is the only block that ships something a client is waiting on. Writing, building, the next deliverable. No email. No Slack. Phone in another room.

If you only have 45 minutes, do the first deliverable only. Do not "check one thing" first. Checking is Block 2.

Fill-in, before you open mail:

```
Today's paid block:
Client: ________
One deliverable: ________
Done looks like: ________
Stop at: ________
```

If you cannot name the deliverable, you do not have a block. You have a hope.

## Block 2 — Admin and replies (45 minutes, after the paid block)

Batch it. Do not let it leak into Block 1.

Sort first, then write:

1. **Today** — money, a blocked client, or a promise with a date this week
2. **Wait** — anything that can sit 48 hours
3. **Delete** — FYIs you will not act on this week

Then send two notes you have been avoiding. Use the lines. Do not draft from a blank screen.

Quiet proposal:

> Still want me to hold the [date] start, or should I release it? One line is enough.

Finished work, no check-in:

> Checking in after [deliverable]. Anything you want tightened, or should we book the next piece?

Fill-in:

```
Follow-ups that leave today:
1) Name: ________ — one sentence: ________
2) Name: ________ — one sentence: ________
Invoice or contract still open: ________
```

## Block 3 — The 20-minute weekly reset (same slot every week)

Friday afternoon or Monday morning. Pick one and lock it. This is the block that keeps Thursday from erasing the plan.

Write three lines only:

1. What actually shipped
2. What got pushed, and why
3. What you promised that is still open

Then the next week's three. Not five. Not ten. Three. If a fourth appears, one of the three leaves. Put each on the calendar as a real block.

```
This week's three:
1) Deliver: ________ by ________
2) Follow up: ________ (last touch ________)
3) Unblock money: ________
Open client with no next date: ________ — date I will set: ________
Tomorrow's first move: ________
```

A client with no next date is drifting. Drift is how a paid project never becomes a second invoice. Name the date or take them off "active" until you do.

Wednesday, five minutes, on the calendar: still the right three? If a fire ate one, replace it. Do not add a fourth. Send any follow-up that is now overdue. One sentence each.

## What still breaks if the blocks only live in your head

- Block 1 starts after the inbox, so it never starts
- Follow-ups wait for a calm hour that does not exist
- The weekly three get rewritten every time you remember a new fire
