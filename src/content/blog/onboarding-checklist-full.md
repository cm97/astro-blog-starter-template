Most client relationships are won or lost in the first 48 hours after they pay.

Not in the delivery. Not in the final presentation. In what happens — or doesn't happen — right after the contract is signed.

The client just made a financial decision. They're looking for signs that it was the right one. A slow response, a missing intake form, a vague "we'll talk soon" — these are signals that they made a mistake. A fast, organized, professional welcome tells them the opposite.

## What most onboarding looks like

A client pays. The provider sends a quick "thanks, talk soon!" email. The kickoff call gets scheduled for two weeks out. The intake form never gets sent.

By the time the call happens, the client's excitement has cooled. Their trust has dropped. They're already wondering if this was worth the money.

## What good onboarding looks like

Within one hour of payment: a welcome email that tells them exactly what happens next. The intake form sent immediately — and not scheduled until it's filled out. A kickoff call in the calendar within the week, with an agenda attached.

That's it. The bar is not high. Most providers just don't do it.

## The checklist

The full Buzzyfly onboarding checklist covers four phases:

- **Before the kickoff call** — the 7 things to complete within 24 hours of payment, including what to look for in the intake form before you ever get on a call
- **The kickoff call** — the exact questions to ask, what you must confirm before the call ends, and the one thing you should never leave without
- **After the call** — the 24-hour summary, how to set up the client folder, and when to send the first project touch
- **First week touchpoints** — the Day 3, Day 7, and Day 10 emails that keep the relationship warm while the work is getting started

It also includes red flags to watch for before the call — patterns that predict a difficult client before the project starts.
