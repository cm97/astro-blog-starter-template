Monday is not the problem. The first hour is.

You sit down already late. Three messages. One fire. No written plan. So you rebuild the week from memory, again. That hour is where the money leaks: the proposal that needed a poke, the client with no next date, the invoice you meant to send Friday.

Do this instead. Twenty minutes. Same order every time. If you only have twelve, stop after step 4.

## The rule when you are already behind

Do not design a better week. Rescue the next 48 hours.

A behind-Monday reset is not a clean-Monday reset. You do not write twelve priorities. You do not reorganize folders. You name three moves that protect cash and stop drift, then you stop.

If the week is already on fire, the reset still happens. You just cut the list, not the habit.

## Minute 0–4 — Sort the inbound pile. Do not work it

Open email and DMs. Sort only. Do not write the replies yet.

Three buckets:

- **Today** — money, a blocked client, or a promise with a date this week
- **Wait** — anything that can sit 48 hours without exploding
- **Delete** — newsletters, FYIs, and anything you will not act on this week

If a thread takes more than 30 seconds to classify, it goes in Wait. Classification is the job. Replies come later.

Fill this before you leave the inbox:

```
Today (max 5 threads):
1) ________
2) ________
3) ________
Wait until Wednesday: ________
Deleted / ignored: ________
```

## Minute 4–8 — Name three priorities, not twelve

Write exactly three lines:

1. One client delivery that is due or late
2. One follow-up that is aging (proposal, quiet lead, or finished project)
3. One admin item that unblocks money (invoice, contract, or kickoff that is sitting)

Everything else waits until those three move. If a fourth item shows up, one of the three has to leave. Put each of the three on the calendar as a real block, even if the block is 25 minutes.

Fill-in:

```
This week's three (behind-Monday version):
1) Deliver: ________ by ________  block: ________
2) Follow up: ________ (sent ________, last touch ________)  block: ________
3) Unblock money: ________  block: ________
Killed so these three can live: ________
```

Cash-protect check before you leave this block:

- Is one of the three an invoice or a paid next date? If not, swap one in.
- Is the follow-up older than 7 days? If yes, it stays.
- Can you name the dollar amount or the next date on paper? If not, it is not a priority yet.

## Minute 8–14 — Touch every open client once

One line each: next deliverable and next date. If you cannot name the next date, that client is drifting.

Scan the list, not the inbox. For each open client write:

- Next thing they get
- Date they get it
- Who owes whom a reply

If a client has no next date, they become today's follow-up or they come off "active" until you put a date back. Drift is how finished work never turns into a second project.

Fill-in, one card per client:

```
Client: ________
Next deliverable: ________
Next date: ________
Who owes a reply: me / them
If no date: follow up today / mark paused
Paid amount still open: ________
```

Cap this at the clients who paid or who have a proposal out. Old leads with no date and no reply in 21 days are not an open client. They are a follow-up or a closed row.

## Minute 14–19 — Send the two follow-ups you have been avoiding

Usually it is one quiet proposal and one finished project with no check-in. Use the lines. Do not draft from a blank screen.

Proposal, no reply (day 5–7):

> Still want me to hold the [date] start, or should I release it? One line is enough.

Finished work, no check-in:

> Checking in two weeks after [deliverable]. Anything you want tightened, or should we book the next piece?

Invoice that never left:

> Invoice for [deliverable] is attached. Due [date]. Reply if the address is wrong.

Send two. Do not perfect them. Two sent beats one rewritten.

If you only have one overdue thread, send that one and write the second as tomorrow's first line. Do not invent a third.

Log the sends so Thursday cannot invent a different version:

```
Follow-up 1: ________ sent [time] — next touch if no reply: ________
Follow-up 2: ________ sent [time] — next touch if no reply: ________
```

## Minute 19–20 — Stop

Write tomorrow's first move in one line. Close the list.

```
Tomorrow first move: ________
Time box: ________
Done when: ________
```

If you keep going you will spend the morning organizing instead of shipping. The reset worked if the three priorities exist on paper and two follow-ups left the building.

## Wednesday, 5 minutes

Look at the three lines. Still the right three? If a fire ate one, replace it. Do not add a fourth.

Send any follow-up that is now overdue. One sentence each.

A Wednesday check that is not on the calendar never happens. Put a 5-minute block next to Monday the same week you write the list.

```
Priority still true? yes / replace with ________
Overdue follow-up sent? yes / no — which: ________
Tomorrow first move still right? ________
```

## Friday, 5 minutes if Monday was ugly

Do not rebuild the week. Close it.

1. Mark which of the three actually moved.
2. Note the one that slid. It is next Monday's line 1 unless you kill it.
3. Send one close-out or one invoice if either is still open.

That is the whole Friday. Same place, same card. If Friday gets skipped, Monday starts from the last written three, not from memory.

```
Moved: ________
Slid (next Monday line 1 unless killed): ________
Invoice or close-out sent: ________
```

## Extra cash-protect moves when the week is already late

- If any open invoice is older than 7 days, it becomes priority 3 before any new delivery.
- If a proposal is older than 10 days with no reply, the day-2 bump goes out the same day you notice it. Do not wait for the "right" moment.
- After you send the two follow-ups, write the next touch date on the same card. Thursday cannot invent a different version.
- Cap the open-client scan at paid clients plus proposals out. Leads with no date and no reply in 21 days are closed rows, not open work.
- If the three priorities do not include one money move, swap one in before you leave the 8-minute block.
- Put the three calendar blocks before you leave the 8-minute window. A priority without a block is a wish.
- Screenshot or copy the three lines into the same note as last week’s Friday close so Monday never starts from a blank page.
- If you only have 12 minutes total, stop after the three priorities and the two follow-ups. Skip the open-client scan. Cash first.
- After any paid reply lands, open the onboarding checklist the same day. The reset ends when money moves; onboarding starts.
- At the end of the 20 minutes write one number: open invoices older than 7 days. If the number is not zero, priority 3 is already chosen.
- Phone reminder the night before for the 20-minute block. Calendar alone dies when the week is on fire.
- Cap open proposal rows at five. Anything past Day 14 is closed or parked before you write the three.
- If a client texts a new ask during the reset, do not answer it. Log it as a scope line and finish the three first.
- After the two follow-ups leave, close the email tab for 30 minutes. The reset is done; delivery starts.
