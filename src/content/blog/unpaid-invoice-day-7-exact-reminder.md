The invoice went out. Day 1 polite note. Still nothing on day 7. The money is sitting in their account instead of yours. Every day it stays open is a day you cannot reinvest or pay yourself. Silence past day 7 is not busy. It is a problem that needs the exact reminder now.

Leaving it soft trains them that late is fine. The day-7 reminder names the due date, restates the amount, and asks for the payment or a firm date.

## The 2-minute log before you send the day-7 reminder

Open the same note you use for the Monday scan. Fill this block.

```
Name: ________
Invoice number: ________
Amount due: $________
Original due: ________
Last touch: ________
Day 7 reminder due: ________
Status after send: paid / promised / escalated
```

If any field is blank, do not send. The reminder only works when the numbers are already written.

## Exact day-7 invoice reminder — send today

Subject: Invoice [number] still open — [amount]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Invoice [number] for [one-line project] is past the due date of [original due] and still open for [amount].
>
> Please send payment today or reply with the exact date it will clear.
>
> [Your name]

One paragraph. No apology. No “just checking in.” The amount and the due date are the reminder. The ask is binary: pay or name the date.

Send it before 11am on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday. Never after 3pm.

Phone-paste version (under 160 chars):

> Invoice [number] for [amount] is past due [date]. Please pay today or reply with the clear date.

## What you do the minute it leaves

1. Mark the status “day 7 sent” in the log.
2. Set a calendar block for day 14 if it is still open.
3. Write the next Monday scan row: “Day 7 reminder sent [Name] [amount].”
4. Do not check the thread for 48 hours. If they reply with a date, log it and confirm in one line.
5. If they pay, mark paid and send the receipt the same hour.

## Extra concrete steps that stop the open invoice from living in your head

- Cap open invoices older than 14 days at two. Anything past day 14 without a payment or date is escalated or written off. A third means you are financing their business.
- Copy the day-7 reminder into the same note as the day-1 and day-14 templates. One open, all three available.
- After you send, set a 48-hour no-check rule. Looking early turns the reminder into another wait.
- If they reply “next week,” answer: “Understood. Confirm the exact date it will clear so I can note it.” Do not leave it vague.
- Never discount in the reminder. The original amount stays the record.
- On the next weekly reset, move every invoice older than 14 days to the “cash still open” line. The list is for money still due, not for hopes.
- Write the exact amount and due date into the reminder before you send. Guessing it from memory creates a soft ask that does not get paid.
- Put a 5-minute calendar block titled “Day 7 invoice: [Name]” the night before. The block is the trigger. Without it the email never leaves.
- After the send, screenshot or copy the sent time into the log. “I think I sent it” is not a chased invoice.
- If three day-7 reminders land the same morning, send all three before 11am. Do not spread them. One sitting, three files chased.
- Keep a “past day 14” note separate from the active list. One monthly scan of that note is enough. Do not let them re-enter the Monday reset as soft maybes.
- When a payment arrives after the reminder, mark it paid the same hour and send the receipt. The follow-up sequence ends when money lands.
- Cap the promised-date window at 7 days. After that the original terms are no longer valid. A new invoice or written plan is required.
- If the original due date has already passed by more than 14 days, do not offer to wait again. The reminder simply names the amount still open.
- Keep the three most common delay replies in the same note so you never search under pressure after a late reply.
