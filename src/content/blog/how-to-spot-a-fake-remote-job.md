You find a remote job that looks perfect. Good pay. Flexible hours. No interview, just a quick chat on a messaging app.

That last part is the problem.

Fake remote jobs copy the look of real ones. They want your money, your bank details or your ID. Here is how to tell them apart before you hand anything over.

## Seven warning signs

1. **They ask you to pay first.** A fee for training, equipment, a background check or "onboarding." Real employers don't charge you to work for them.
2. **They send you a check to buy equipment.** You deposit it, buy gear from "their vendor," and the check bounces a week later. The money you spent is gone.
3. **The interview happens only by text.** No video call. No phone call. Just a chat app and a stranger with a company logo as their picture.
4. **The offer comes before any real interview.** Real hiring takes a few steps. An offer after ten minutes of chat is a script.
5. **They want bank details or ID before an offer letter.** Payroll forms come after you sign. Not before.
6. **The email comes from a free address.** A recruiter at a real company writes from the company's domain, not a free mail account.
7. **The pay is far above the work.** Data entry for a senior engineer's salary is a lure, not a job.

## Checks to run before you apply

- **Find the job on the company's own careers page.** If it isn't there, ask why.
- **Look up the recruiter.** A real recruiter has a work history you can find.
- **Search the company name plus the word "scam."** Other people may have been there first.
- **Never move the conversation off the hiring platform** until you have checked the company is real.

If something feels off, skip it. Another job will come along. Your bank details won't come back.
