A new client pays. You feel the win for about twenty minutes. Then you open a blank email and start writing the welcome from scratch again.

That is the part that breaks. The sequence lives in your head. When the week is busy the welcome is late, the intake nudge never goes out, and week two is silence.

Here is the exact email sequence that runs the first ten days. Four emails. Fill the blanks. Send them on the clock.

## What the sequence actually does

It does three jobs:

1. Confirms they are in and tells them what happens next.
2. Gets the information you need before the kickoff so the call is useful.
3. Keeps a light touch going so they do not wonder if you forgot them.

It does not sell. It does not explain your whole process. It moves the start forward.

## Email 1 — Welcome (send within one hour of payment)

Subject: Welcome — here is what happens next

> Hi [Name],
>
> Payment received. We start [start date].
>
> Here is the next step: I sent a short intake form. It takes about five minutes. Please return it by [date, three days from now] so the kickoff is useful.
>
> Kickoff is [day/time]. I will send the calendar invite with the agenda.
>
> One thing I need from you before then: [access, file, or decision-maker name].
>
> Reply here if anything is unclear. I answer weekdays before [time].
>
> [Your name]

Why it works: It confirms payment, names the start date, gives one clear action, and sets the reply window. No long story.

## Email 2 — Intake nudge (48 hours if the form is not back)

Subject: Still need the intake before kickoff

> Hi [Name],
>
> Quick check — I still need the intake form before our kickoff so we can use the time well.
>
> Link again: [link]. Deadline is still [date].
>
> If the time is tight, just answer the first three questions and we will fill the rest on the call.
>
> [Your name]

Send this only if the form is missing. Do not rewrite it. The same note every time removes the decision.

## Email 3 — Kickoff summary (within 60 minutes of the call)

Subject: Recap from today — [project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Recap from the kickoff:
>
> Goal: [one outcome].
> You own: [item].
> I own: [item].
> Next check-in: [date/time].
> Out of scope for this start: [one line].
>
> First piece is due [date].
>
> Anything missing from that list, reply and I will add it.
>
> [Your name]

This email is the written version of what you just agreed. It stops the “I thought we said…” conversation later.

## Email 4 — Day-7 check-in

Subject: Week one check — [project name]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Quick status after the first week:
>
> What moved: [one sentence].
> Next piece: [deliverable] on [date].
> Anything blocked on your side?
>
> [Your name]

One question. Easy to answer. It keeps the client from going quiet and gives you a clean log of progress.

## The fill-in card you write once

Copy this into the client note the hour payment clears. Fill it before you send Email 1.

```
Client: ________
Paid: ________ on ________
Start date: ________
Intake link: ________
Intake due: ________
Kickoff: ________
You need from them: ________
Out of scope: ________
Day-7 hold: ________
```

If any line is blank, the sequence is not ready.
