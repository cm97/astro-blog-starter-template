# Buzzyfly product research and build standard

The owner's rule: nothing gets built until the research proves people want it, it works for real buyers, and it will still work as the market changes. These rules apply every time.

## 1. Originality first
- Do not pick a crowded category and copy it (Notion templates, generic planners, prompt packs, generic CRM sheets). If anyone can pick it, it is a weak choice.
- Start from an unmet need: a problem people pay to solve where existing products fail them. Name what the existing products get wrong.
- The product must be something a competitor, or a person typing a prompt into an AI, cannot easily copy.

## 2. Three time horizons, every time
For every idea, check all three:
- **Worked:** the past 3–5 years, with revenue or adoption evidence.
- **Working:** now, with current data and the date of every figure.
- **Will work:** the next 2–5 years. Check what is coming:
  - AI built into Excel, Google Sheets and Notion
  - AI agents doing the task for the user
  - search traffic lost to AI answers
  - price collapse and marketplace flooding
  - subscription and consumer law (auto-renewal, cancellation)
  - payment-platform rules
  - support load

## 3. Filter out what flunks later
Drop an idea if it looks good now but has a known bottleneck later:
- the AI tools users already have will do it free
- it only survives on SEO
- its price is collapsing
- delivery depends on a manual step
- it brings heavy support load or refund and chargeback risk
- a law or platform change kills it
Write down which bottleneck killed each dropped idea.

## 4. Judge sources, don't just collect them
Rank evidence. Higher beats lower:
1. Revenue or adoption data: open dashboards, filings, marketplace sales counts.
2. Large surveys and government data.
3. Official docs: platform, law, regulator.
4. Credible journalism.
5. Vendor or course-seller claims. Flag them as unverified and never rely on them alone.
6. Opinion or hype. Exclude it.

Never stop at one source because it sounds good. Cross-check every key claim against a second, independent source.

## 5. Real buyers, including the ones who struggle
- Buying has to be one click to Stripe and an instant download on the thank-you page. No account and no forms.
- The product has to work for neurodivergent buyers and people who are overwhelmed:
  - few choices at a time
  - one clear first step
  - no reliance on memory
  - every color signal paired with words
  - plain language
- It has to open on a phone, Mac, Windows and Google Sheets, with no unzipping, macros or passwords.
- Follow Microsoft's spreadsheet accessibility rules: a title in A1, no merged cells, descriptive tab names.

## 6. Before building
- Full specs written: who it's for, what it does, how it's delivered, and what is in month 1, month 2 and so on if it's a subscription.
- Demand evidence in hand, from the research or from the free tool's waitlist.
- Delivery path tested end to end: Stripe payment, webhook, download.
- Load the matching skill and use the connected tools (Stripe, GitHub, Cloudflare). If a tool needs connecting, ask the owner once, naming exactly which one.
- No PDFs, no demo or sample data, no blog posts as products. Products are software, apps, or working Excel workbooks.
- No fake urgency, testimonials, stats or income promises (see CLAUDE.md).
