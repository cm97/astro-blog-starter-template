# Follow-Up System
### The complete follow-up cadence for leads, proposals, and quiet clients.

---

## The core rule

Most sales happen on follow-up 4, 5, or 6. Most service providers stop at 1 or 2. The money is in the follow-through — not the pitch.

---

## SECTION 1 — Lead follow-up (after first contact or inquiry)

Use this when someone expressed interest but hasn't moved forward.

| Day | Action | Template |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Initial reply or proposal sent | `templates/proposal.md` |
| Day 3 | Gentle check-in: "Just making sure this didn't get buried" | `templates/check-in-email.md` (Variation A) |
| Day 7 | Value drop: share something useful, no ask | `templates/check-in-email.md` (Variation B) |
| Day 14 | The breakup: close the thread on your end | `templates/close-out.md` |

- [ ] Log each follow-up in your tracking doc with date and response (yes / no / no reply)
- [ ] If they reply at any point — restart the sequence from their response, don't continue the chain
- [ ] If they say "not now" — add to quarterly re-engagement list
- [ ] If they say "no" — thank them, ask if there's anyone they'd refer

---

## SECTION 2 — Proposal follow-up (after sending a proposal)

Use this when you've sent a formal proposal and are waiting on a decision.

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | Proposal sent. Confirm receipt by email. |
| Day 3 | "Did you get a chance to look this over?" (3 sentences max) |
| Day 7 | Add one line of value: "One thing I forgot to mention..." |
| Day 12 | The decision prompt: "I have a slot opening up — wanted to check in before I fill it" |
| Day 18 | Final check: "I'm going to close this on my end — totally fine if timing's off" |

**What to do if they say:**
- "Too expensive" → Ask what budget they have. Offer a smaller first engagement.
- "Still deciding" → Ask what information would help them decide. Give a specific deadline.
- "We went another direction" → Ask what tipped it. No emotion. Just data.
- Nothing → Send the Day 18 email and move on.

---

## SECTION 3 — Active client follow-up (keeping projects moving)

Use this when a project stalls mid-engagement.

- [ ] If you haven't heard from an active client in 5 days — send a one-line check-in
- [ ] If they haven't sent requested assets in 7 days — send a polite nudge with a specific deadline
- [ ] If they haven't responded in 14 days — send a project status email with two options: "continue as planned" or "put this on hold"
- [ ] If a deliverable is late on YOUR end — tell them 24 hours before the deadline, not after

**The project-stall email:**
> Subject: [Project Name] — quick check-in
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I haven't heard back on [specific item] and want to make sure we stay on track for [milestone/date].
>
> If you can get me [what you need] by [specific date], we're still good. If the timeline has shifted, just let me know and we'll adjust.
>
> [Your name]

---

## SECTION 4 — Post-project follow-up (after delivery)

Use this to protect the relationship after the work is done.

| Timeline | Action |
|---|---|
| Day of delivery | Send final files + thank-you email |
| +2 weeks | "How's it going?" check-in (no ask) |
| +30 days | Ask for a testimonial or review |
| +60 days | Referral ask: "Is there anyone you'd point my way?" |
| +90 days | Check-in: "Anything come up that I can help with?" |

- [ ] Add every completed client to your quarterly re-engagement list
- [ ] Set a calendar reminder for the 30-day and 90-day check-ins before you close the project

---

## SECTION 5 — Re-engagement (cold leads and past clients)

Use this quarterly for anyone who went quiet or said "not now."

**The re-engagement email:**
> Subject: Still on your radar?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We spoke [timeframe] ago about [brief context]. I've been working on [relevant thing] since then and thought of you.
>
> No pressure — just wanted to reconnect. If there's anything I can help with, I'm here.
>
> [Your name]

- [ ] Send to no more than 10 people per quarter — keep it personal
- [ ] Do not mass email. One by one.
- [ ] Log responses. Track what language gets replies.

## The rule

Send the breakup email. It works more often than you think. The people who ghost feel guilt — and a polite close often breaks the silence. Even if it doesn't, you get clarity and move on. Both are wins.
