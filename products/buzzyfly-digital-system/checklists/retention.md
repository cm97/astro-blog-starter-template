# Client Retention Checklist
### How to keep clients, get referrals, and build a business that doesn't start from zero every month.

---

## The math

Acquiring a new client costs 5–7x more than keeping an existing one. A retained client who refers one person per year is worth 3–4x their contract value. Retention is not a nice-to-have — it's the highest-ROI activity in your business.

---

## PHASE 1 — During the project (active retention)

- [ ] Send a progress update every Friday — even if nothing changed. One sentence is enough.
- [ ] Deliver one small win in the first 2 weeks, before the big deliverable. Remove their anxiety early.
- [ ] Ask one question per week they didn't expect. Shows you're thinking, not just executing.
- [ ] Never let 7 days pass without contact during an active project.
- [ ] If something is delayed, tell them before the deadline — not after.
- [ ] Acknowledge their input. "Good call on X — that made the work better."

**The Friday update email:**
> Subject: [Project Name] — week [#] update
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Quick update on where we are:
>
> - [What got done this week]
> - [What's next]
> - [Anything you need from them, if anything]
>
> On track for [milestone/date]. Let me know if anything comes up.
>
> [Your name]

---

## PHASE 2 — At delivery (the most important moment)

This is when clients form their lasting impression. Most providers deliver the work and disappear. Don't do that.

- [ ] Deliver on or before the deadline — never late without advance notice
- [ ] Send a clean, organized final package — not raw files in an email
- [ ] Include a short "how to use this" note with the delivery
- [ ] Thank them — specifically — for something about working with them
- [ ] Set their expectation for what happens next (even if "next" is nothing)

**The delivery email:**
> Subject: [Project Name] — final delivery
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Here's everything from [project name]:
>
> [Clean list of deliverables with links or attachments]
>
> [One sentence on how to use what you've given them]
>
> It's been a genuine pleasure working on this. [Specific thing you appreciated about working with them.]
>
> I'll check in in a couple of weeks to see how it's landing. In the meantime, if anything comes up, I'm here.
>
> [Your name]

---

## PHASE 3 — Post-delivery (relationship maintenance)

| Timing | Action |
|---|---|
| +2 weeks | "How's it going?" — no ask, no pitch. Just genuine curiosity. |
| +30 days | Ask for a testimonial or review. Strike while they're happy. |
| +60 days | Referral ask: "Is there anyone you'd point my way?" |
| +90 days | "Anything new I can help with?" — one line, low pressure |
| Quarterly | Add to re-engagement list; check in with something useful |

**The testimonial ask (30 days after delivery):**
> Subject: Quick favor?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Hope [the thing you delivered] is going well. I'd really appreciate a quick testimonial or review if you're open to it — even a sentence or two.
>
> Here's a link: [link]
>
> Or just reply here with a few words and I'll handle the formatting.
>
> Thank you either way.
>
> [Your name]

**The referral ask (60 days after delivery):**
> Subject: Anyone come to mind?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I've really enjoyed the work we did together and I'm looking to take on a couple more projects like that.
>
> Is there anyone in your network who might be dealing with a similar challenge? Even a quick introduction would mean a lot.
>
> No pressure at all — just thought I'd ask.
>
> [Your name]

---

## PHASE 4 — Building a referral system

- [ ] Ask for referrals at the right moment: 30–60 days after delivery, when the result is fresh
- [ ] Make it easy: "Someone who does X, similar to what you do" is easier to answer than a generic ask
- [ ] Acknowledge every referral — even if it doesn't close
- [ ] Consider a referral thank-you: a handwritten note, a gift card, a discount on their next project
- [ ] Track referral sources — after 6 months, you'll know exactly who to invest in

---

## PHASE 5 — The quarterly retention review

Run this every 90 days.

- [ ] List every client from the last 12 months
- [ ] For each: did you do a post-delivery check-in? A 30-day? A 60-day?
- [ ] Who hasn't heard from you in 90+ days?
- [ ] Send re-engagement emails to anyone who's gone quiet
- [ ] Which clients are most likely to come back? Prioritize those.

---

## The rule

Retention is not a campaign you run once. It's a habit you build into your calendar. One email a month to past clients keeps you top of mind without being annoying. When they have a problem — and they will — you're the first person they think of.
