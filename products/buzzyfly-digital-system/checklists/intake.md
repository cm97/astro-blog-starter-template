# Client Intake Checklist
### Run this before every discovery call. No exceptions.

---

## The purpose

The intake process filters out bad-fit clients before you spend an hour on a call. It also gives you everything you need to walk in prepared, ask smart questions, and close on the spot if it's right.

A client who won't fill out an intake form is telling you something important. Listen.

---

## STEP 1 — Send the intake form

Send this within 1 hour of the initial inquiry. The faster you respond, the higher the conversion rate.

**The send message:**
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for reaching out. Before we get on a call, I'd love to get some context so I can make the most of your time.
>
> Can you fill this out? Takes about 5 minutes: [link or paste questions below]
>
> Once I have this, I'll send over some times to connect.
>
> [Your name]

**The intake form questions** (see `templates/intake-form.md` for the full version):
1. What's the main thing you need help with? (One sentence.)
2. What's your deadline — and is it fixed or flexible?
3. What's your budget range for this project?
4. Have you worked with someone in this capacity before?
5. What does success look like to you in 90 days?
6. What's gotten in the way of solving this problem before?
7. Is there anything I should know before we talk?

---

## STEP 2 — Read the responses before the call

- [ ] Read the form 30 minutes before the call — not during it
- [ ] Highlight anything vague, concerning, or that needs clarification
- [ ] Note the budget range: does it match the scope they're describing?
- [ ] Prepare 2–3 follow-up questions that the form didn't answer
- [ ] Look up their website or LinkedIn if applicable — 5 minutes is enough

---

## STEP 3 — Score the lead

Use this quick scoring system to decide how much energy to invest.

| Signal | Score |
|---|---|
| Budget matches scope | +2 |
| Clear, specific goal | +2 |
| Real deadline stated | +1 |
| Has worked with a professional before | +1 |
| Knows what success looks like | +1 |
| Answered every question thoughtfully | +1 |
| **Red flags** | |
| "Budget is flexible" with no number | -2 |
| "ASAP" as the only deadline | -1 |
| Previously worked with multiple providers and it "didn't work" | -2 |
| One-word answers throughout | -2 |
| Asks for a discount before the first call | -3 |

**Score guide:**
- 6+ : High priority — block time, prepare well, make an offer
- 3–5 : Qualified — standard call, no rush to push
- Under 3 : Proceed with caution or decline politely

---

## STEP 4 — After the call

- [ ] Note the score and your gut read
- [ ] If moving forward: send the proposal within 24 hours
- [ ] If not a fit: send a polite "not the right match" email — do it the same day
- [ ] Log in your tracking doc: name, date, score, outcome

**The "not a fit" email:**
> Hi [Name],
>
> It was great to talk. After thinking it over, I don't think I'm the right fit for what you're describing right now — [brief honest reason if appropriate].
>
> I'd rather tell you that clearly than waste your time. I hope you find the right person soon.
>
> [Your name]

---

## The rule

Never book a call without a filled intake form. The form isn't bureaucracy — it's a filter. The clients who skip it are the ones who will skip your invoices, your boundaries, and your process.
