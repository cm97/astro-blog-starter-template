# Pricing Worksheet
### Stop guessing. Calculate your real rate and set prices that work.

---

## PART 1 — Your real hourly floor

Most service providers underprice because they use a vague sense of "what's fair." This replaces that feeling with a number.

**Step 1: Set your annual revenue target**

What do you actually want to take home this year?
(Not gross revenue — what hits your bank account after expenses and taxes.)

Annual target: $___________

**Step 2: Calculate your real working weeks**

52 weeks − vacations − sick days − holidays = actual working weeks

Example: 52 − 3 (vacation) − 1 (sick) − 2 (holidays) = 46 weeks

Your working weeks: ___________

**Step 3: Calculate your billable hours per week**

This is NOT how many hours you work. It's how many hours you can actually bill.
Most service providers bill 50–60% of their time. The rest is admin, marketing, sales, operations.

Example: 40-hour week × 55% billable = 22 billable hours/week

Your billable hours/week: ___________

**Step 4: Your hourly floor**

Annual target ÷ (working weeks × billable hours) = your minimum hourly rate

Example: $80,000 ÷ (46 × 22) = $79.05/hour

**Your hourly floor: $___________/hour**

Never quote below this number. Not for anyone.

---

## PART 2 — Project pricing

Once you have your floor, use this to price specific projects.

**Step 1: Estimate hours honestly**

How many hours will this actually take? Include:
- Client calls and meetings
- Revision rounds
- Admin and communication overhead
- Buffer for the unexpected

Estimated hours: ___________

**Step 2: Apply the buffer multiplier**

Projects almost always take longer than estimated. Add 25–30% to your estimate.

Buffered hours: __________ × 1.3 = ___________

**Step 3: Calculate the project floor**

Buffered hours × your hourly floor = your price floor for this project

Example: 18 hours × $79 = $1,422

**Your project floor: $___________**

**Step 4: The value check**

Before you finalize the price, ask:
- What is this outcome worth to the client?
- What would they pay a large agency for this?
- What is the cost to them of NOT solving this problem?

If your floor is significantly lower than the value, raise your price. The floor is the minimum — not the target.

**Your final project price: $___________**

---

## PART 3 — Retainer pricing

**Monthly retainer rate:**

Hours you'll dedicate per month × your hourly rate × 1.15 (availability premium)

Example: 10 hours × $79 × 1.15 = $908.50/month → round to $900 or $950

**Your monthly retainer: $___________/month**

The availability premium (the ×1.15) is real. A retainer client gets priority access to your time and brain. That has value beyond the hours. Charge for it.

---

## PART 4 — Packaging your services

Single services are hard to sell. Packages convert better because they have a clear outcome, not an hourly commitment.

**The starter package:** One specific deliverable. Low commitment. Low risk for a new client.
→ Example: "Proposal review + feedback" for $150

**The core package:** Your main engagement. Clear scope, clear outcome, fixed price.
→ Example: "Client onboarding system setup" for $1,500

**The complete package:** Everything, bundled. Best value per dollar, highest total price.
→ Example: "Full business operating system" for $4,500

Price the packages so the core package is obviously the best value at a fair price. The starter opens the door; the complete package anchors the range and makes the core look reasonable.

---

## PART 5 — What to do when they say it's too expensive

1. **Don't immediately lower the price.** Ask what budget they have.
2. **Offer a smaller first engagement.** Can you solve one part of their problem for less?
3. **Hold the price and explain the value.** "Here's what's included and what it'll do for you..."
4. **Know when to let it go.** A client who can't afford you now is not your client right now. Stay professional and check back in 6 months.

**What you should never do:**
- Discount more than 15% without removing scope
- Quote a lower price to "get in the door" and expect to charge more later
- Work for free to "build the relationship" — it sets the wrong price permanently

---

## The rule

Price is not a negotiation until you've made an offer. Make the offer. Let them respond. Then decide what to do. The clients who respect your work will pay your real rate. The clients who don't are not your clients.
