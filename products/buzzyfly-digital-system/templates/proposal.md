# Proposal Templates
### One page. One price. One clear next step.

---

## The structure that closes

A proposal that doesn't close is a document, not a sales tool. The difference:

| Weak proposal | Strong proposal |
|---|---|
| Generic overview of your services | Their specific problem, in their words |
| Long list of everything you could do | Specific deliverables with clear scope |
| "Timeline TBD" | Milestones with actual dates |
| Price range | One number |
| "Let me know if you have questions" | One clear next step |

---

## TEMPLATE 1 — Standard service proposal

**Subject: Proposal for [Project Name] — [Client Name]**

---

Hi [Name],

You mentioned [their problem, in their exact words from the intake call]. Here's how I'd approach it.

**The approach**

[2–3 sentences. What you'll do and why it'll work for their specific situation. No jargon. No "leverage synergies."]

**Deliverables**

- [Specific deliverable 1 — be precise. Not "content" — "6 blog posts, 800–1000 words each"]
- [Specific deliverable 2]
- [Specific deliverable 3]
- [X rounds of revisions included]
- [What's NOT included — prevents scope creep before it starts]

**Timeline**

| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| Kickoff call | [Date] |
| [Milestone 1] | [Date] |
| [Milestone 2] | [Date] |
| Final delivery | [Date] |

**Investment**

$[Amount] — [payment terms, e.g., "50% to start, 50% on delivery" or "paid in full upfront"]

Payment by: [Stripe link / invoice / bank transfer]

This proposal is valid until [date — give them 5–7 business days].

**Next step**

Reply to this email with "yes" and I'll send the agreement. Once it's signed and the first payment is received, we're official and I'll send the kickoff invite.

Questions? I'm here.

[Your name]

## TEMPLATE 2 — Retainer proposal

**Subject: Ongoing engagement proposal — [Client Name]**

---

Hi [Name],

Based on what you described, here's how I'd structure an ongoing engagement.

**What this covers**

- [X hours per month] of [type of work]
- [Specific recurring deliverable, e.g., "weekly strategy call" or "4 pieces of content"]
- [Response time for ad-hoc questions]
- [Anything else included]

**What this doesn't cover**

- [Out-of-scope items — be specific]
- Work beyond [X hours] in a given month (billed at $[rate]/hour)

**Investment**

$[Amount]/month — billed on the [1st] of each month.

First month due before we start. Cancel anytime with 30 days' notice.

**Next step**

Reply "yes" and I'll send the agreement. We can start [date].

[Your name]

## TEMPLATE 3 — Quick-turn small project

**Subject: Quick quote — [Project Name]**

---

Hi [Name],

Here's a quick quote for [what they asked for].

**What I'll do:** [One sentence. Precise.]

**Delivery:** [Specific date or "X business days from kickoff"]

**Price:** $[Amount] — paid before I start.

**What I need from you:** [Any info or assets — be specific]

Ready to go when you are. Reply with "yes" and I'll send a payment link.

[Your name]

## The numbers rule

Never give a price range. "Between $2,000 and $4,000" tells the client your floor is $2,000 and invites them to negotiate down to it. Give one number. If they push back, you can discuss — but start with one number.

## The urgency rule

Every proposal needs an expiration date. "Valid until [date]" is not pressure — it's professional. It tells the client this is a real offer that requires a real decision, not a standing invitation they can act on in 3 months.

## The length rule

One page. Two at most. If your proposal is longer than that, you're writing a statement of work, not a proposal. Split them. The proposal closes; the SOW governs.
