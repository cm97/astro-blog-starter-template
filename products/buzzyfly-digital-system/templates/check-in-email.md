# Check-In Email Templates
### For leads, active clients, and anyone who's gone quiet.

---

## VARIATION A — Day 3 gentle nudge (lead follow-up)

**Subject: Re: [original subject]**

Hi [Name],

Just floating this back up — wanted to make sure my last email didn't get buried.

[One sentence restating what you sent or offered.]

No pressure either way. Happy to answer any questions if you have them.

[Your name]

---

## VARIATION B — Day 7 value drop (lead follow-up)

**Subject: One thing I forgot to mention**

Hi [Name],

I was thinking about what you mentioned — [brief reference to their situation] — and wanted to share something that might be useful regardless of whether we work together:

[One genuinely useful tip, resource, or observation. Not a pitch. Actual value.]

Anyway, still happy to chat if the timing ever works. No rush.

[Your name]

---

## VARIATION C — Active client mid-project check-in

**Subject: Quick check-in on [project name]**

Hi [Name],

Just wanted to make sure [the last thing you sent] landed okay and you didn't have any questions before I move to the next phase.

If everything looks good on your end, I'm heads-down on [what you're working on] and on track for [next milestone/date].

Anything you need from me?

[Your name]

---

## VARIATION D — Requesting overdue materials

**Subject: [Project name] — still waiting on [item]**

Hi [Name],

I want to keep your project on schedule, and I'm holding at [current phase] until I have [specific thing you need from them].

If you can get me [the item] by [specific date], I can still hit [milestone date]. If the timing has shifted, just let me know and we'll adjust.

Let me know either way so I can plan accordingly.

[Your name]

**Note:** Always include a specific date. "As soon as you can" doesn't create urgency. A date does.

---

## VARIATION E — Checking in on a stalled active project

**Subject: [Project name] — where are we?**

Hi [Name],

I haven't heard back in [X days] and want to make sure everything's okay on your end.

Here's where we stand: [one sentence on project status]

Two options:
1. We push forward as planned — I just need [what you need] by [date]
2. We put this on hold for now and schedule a restart when the timing is better

Either works for me. Just let me know which direction makes sense.

[Your name]

---

## VARIATION F — The "did this land?" follow-up

**Subject: Re: [Deliverable name]**

Hi [Name],

I sent over [deliverable] on [date] — just wanted to make sure it arrived okay and there weren't any questions.

Once you've had a chance to look it over, I'll need your feedback by [date] to stay on track.

[Your name]

---

## VARIATION G — Quarterly re-engagement (past client)

**Subject: Checking in**

Hi [Name],

It's been a few months since we wrapped up [project]. Hope [the thing you worked on] is going well.

I've been working on [brief update — something relevant to them] and thought of you. No agenda — just wanted to say hello and see how things are going on your end.

If anything new has come up that I might be able to help with, I'm here.

[Your name]

---

## The rules

1. **Short is better.** A check-in email over 5 sentences is a proposal. Pick one.
2. **One ask per email.** Don't ask how they're doing AND request feedback AND ask about a referral. One thing.
3. **Specific beats vague.** "Did you get a chance to review?" is weaker than "I need your feedback on sections 2 and 3 to move forward."
4. **No guilt, no passive aggression.** "I haven't heard back..." is fine. "I've reached out multiple times..." is not.
