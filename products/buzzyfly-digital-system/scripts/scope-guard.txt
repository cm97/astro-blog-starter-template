SCOPE GUARD — WORD-FOR-WORD SCRIPTS FOR HARD CONVERSATIONS
==========================================================

Read these out loud until they don't make you flinch.
The discomfort you feel is normal. It goes away with practice.


---
SCRIPT 1 — THE STANDARD SCOPE CREEP RESPONSE
---

When a client asks for something outside the agreement:

"That's a great idea, and I want to make sure we do it right.
It's outside what we agreed on for this phase, so here's what I'd suggest:

Option 1: We add it as a separate piece of work — I can put together a quick
quote, probably a day or two turnaround.

Option 2: We keep the current scope as-is and you can decide about this later
once you see how the first phase lands.

Which feels better to you?"


---
SCRIPT 2 — WHEN THEY PUSH BACK ("CAN'T YOU JUST...?")
---

"I hear you — it seems like a small thing, and I get that.
The challenge is that [brief honest reason: it's more complex than it looks /
it sets a precedent for the rest of the project / it would push the timeline].

I want to do right by you, and I want to do right by the project.
Here's what I can do: [specific offer — small scope addition at a fair price,
or an alternative that solves their underlying need within scope].

Does that work?"


---
SCRIPT 3 — THE REVISION LOOP (CHANGES AFTER APPROVAL)
---

When they want to change something they already approved:

"Happy to do that. Just want to flag that we're past the revision stage on this
piece — we're on revision [X] of [X included].

I can absolutely make this change. It'll be [Y] additional [hours/flat fee] on
top of the original scope.

Should I put that in writing and get your go-ahead before I start?"


---
SCRIPT 4 — THE MOVING TARGET (THEY CHANGED THE BRIEF)
---

When the project has fundamentally shifted from what you agreed to:

"I want to make sure I understand what you're describing, because it sounds
like the direction has shifted from where we started.

Originally we agreed to [what was in the brief]. What you're describing now
is [what they want now], which is meaningfully different.

I'm not saying no — I just want to make sure we're both clear that this is
essentially a new scope. That means a new timeline and a revised price.

Can we get on a quick call so I can understand exactly what you're picturing?
I want to make sure any new agreement reflects what we're actually doing."


---
SCRIPT 5 — THE ASSUMPTION CREEP (THEY EXPECTED SOMETHING YOU NEVER AGREED TO)
---

When a client insists something was "included" but it wasn't:

"I want to get this right, so let me look back at our agreement.
[Pause. Actually look.]

Looking at what we signed, [deliverable they expected] isn't in what we agreed
on — but I understand why you might have expected it.

Here's what I'd like to do: [option 1 — add it at a fair cost, or option 2 —
do a slimmer version of it within the current scope].

Going forward, I'll be more explicit in the scope document so this doesn't
come up again. What would you like to do here?"


---
SCRIPT 6 — DECLINING ADDITIONAL WORK YOU DON'T WANT
---

"Thanks for thinking of me for this. I want to be honest with you —
I don't think I'm the right person for [this specific thing].

[Optional: brief honest reason — "It's outside my core focus" or
"I don't have bandwidth to do it well right now."]

I'd rather tell you that clearly than take it on and not give it the
attention it deserves."


---
PRACTICE PROTOCOL
-----------------

1. Read each script out loud three times.
2. Record yourself saying Script 1 and listen back.
3. Practice with a friend or partner playing the client.
4. The goal is to say these without pausing, apologizing, or adding
   qualifiers like "I mean..." or "I just..."

The words are not the hard part. The hard part is believing you're
allowed to say them.

You are.


---
THE RULE
---------

Every scope creep conversation you avoid costs you money.
Every one you handle well earns you respect — and usually more work.

The clients who push back hardest on scope are often the ones who,
once they see you hold your ground professionally, trust you most.
