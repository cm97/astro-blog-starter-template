## What each file does

**checklists/intake.md** — Run this before every discovery call. Seven questions that filter bad-fit clients before you spend an hour on a call. Includes a scoring system: 6+ is high priority, under 3 is proceed with caution. The rule: never book a call without a filled intake form.

**checklists/onboarding.md** — Run this every time a client signs. Four phases: before the kickoff call, the kickoff call itself, after the call, and first-week touchpoints. The standard: if you handed this to someone else and they could run the onboarding without asking you a single question, it's done right.

**checklists/weekly-reset.md** — The 20-minute Monday routine. Four parts: last week review, this week's three priorities, calendar and inbox sweep, admin sweep. Plus a Wednesday midweek check-in and a Friday close. The rule: if it takes more than 20 minutes, you're running a strategy session, not a reset.

**checklists/follow-up.md** — The complete follow-up cadence. Lead follow-up (day 0, 3, 7, 14), proposal follow-up (day 0, 3, 7, 12, 18), active client follow-up, post-project follow-up, and quarterly re-engagement. The core rule: most sales happen on follow-up 4, 5, or 6. Most providers stop at 1 or 2.

**checklists/retention.md** — How to keep clients and get referrals. Friday progress updates, the delivery email, post-delivery check-ins at 2 weeks, 30 days, 60 days, 90 days. The math: acquiring a new client costs 5–7x more than keeping an existing one.

**checklists/scope-creep.md** — What to do when clients ask for more than you agreed to. Four forms of scope creep, the response protocol, documentation, revision policy, and red flags. The rule: one absorbed scope request creates the expectation of another.

**templates/welcome-email.md** — Four welcome email templates: standard service-based, digital product delivery, coaching retainer, and complex projects. The rule: send within 1 hour of payment. Every hour of delay increases buyer anxiety.

**templates/intake-form.md** — Ten questions with green, yellow, and red signal reading. Budget ranges, success criteria, red flags. The rule: send it before you book the call, not after.

**templates/kickoff-summary.md** — Send within 24 hours of the kickoff call. Their goal in their words, deliverables, timeline, what you need from them, communication, revisions. The rule: most project problems come from different expectations and delayed communication.

**templates/check-in-email.md** — Seven variations: day 3 gentle nudge, day 7 value drop, active client mid-project, requesting overdue materials, stalled project, did-this-land, quarterly re-engagement. The rules: short is better, one ask per email, specific beats vague.

**templates/close-out.md** — Five templates: the breakup email, project close-out, mutual not-a-fit, ending a retainer, closing a not-yet. The rule: always end with something that keeps the door open unless there's a reason to close it completely.

**templates/proposal.md** — Three proposal templates: standard service, retainer, quick-turn small project. The rules: never give a price range, every proposal needs an expiration date, one page max.

**templates/pricing-worksheet.md** — Calculate your real hourly floor, project pricing with buffer, retainer pricing with availability premium, packaging your services, and what to do when they say too expensive. The rule: price is not a negotiation until you've made an offer.

**scripts/scope-guard.txt** — Six word-for-word scripts: standard scope creep response, when they push back, the revision loop, the moving target, assumption creep, declining additional work. Plus a practice protocol: read each out loud three times, record yourself, practice with a friend.

## How to use it

1. Download this file.
2. Pick one checklist — the one that fixes your biggest problem right now.
3. Run it this week. Actually run it.
4. Note what happened.
5. Then add the next one.

Don't try to do all of it at once. That's how it dies.
